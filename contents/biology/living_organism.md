# LIVING ORGANISMS

*(Comprehensive SS1 Biology Explanation – Self-Study Guide)*

---

## 1. Meaning of Living Organisms

A **living organism** is any entity that has life and can carry out essential life processes. These life processes distinguish living things from non-living things.

Examples of living organisms include human beings, goats, mango trees, grasses, fish, and bacteria. Non-living things include stones, cars, chairs, and water.

Living organisms are active systems that maintain themselves, grow, reproduce, and respond to their surroundings.

---

## 2. Characteristics of Living Things

All living organisms share certain characteristics. These are often summarized using the acronym **MRS GREN**:

* **M**ovement
* **R**espiration
* **S**ensitivity
* **G**rowth
* **R**eproduction
* **E**xcretion
* **N**utrition

Each characteristic is explained below.

### Movement

Movement is the ability to change position or location. Animals move from place to place (locomotion), while plants show movement in response to stimuli. For example, a sunflower turns toward sunlight, and the “touch-me-not” plant folds when touched.

Movement alone does not make something living. A car moves but is not alive because it does not carry out other life processes.

### Respiration

Respiration is the chemical process by which food is broken down in cells to release energy. This energy is used for growth, repair, movement, and other life activities.

Respiration can be:

* **Aerobic** (requires oxygen)
* **Anaerobic** (does not require oxygen)

Respiration is different from breathing. Breathing is only the movement of air in and out of the lungs.

### Sensitivity (Irritability)

Sensitivity is the ability to detect and respond to changes in the environment, known as stimuli. Stimuli may include heat, light, sound, touch, or chemicals.

For example, humans withdraw their hand quickly from a hot object. Plants respond to light by growing toward it.

### Growth

Growth is a permanent increase in size and dry mass. It results from cell division and cell enlargement.

For example, a maize seed grows into a mature plant. Growth is irreversible and measurable.

### Reproduction

Reproduction is the ability to produce new individuals of the same species. It ensures continuity of life.

There are two main types:

* **Sexual reproduction**, involving two parents.
* **Asexual reproduction**, involving one parent.

### Excretion

Excretion is the removal of waste products produced during metabolism. Examples include carbon dioxide, sweat, and urine.

Excretion must not be confused with egestion. Egestion is the removal of undigested food from the body.

### Nutrition

Nutrition is the process by which organisms obtain and use food.

There are two major types:

* **Autotrophic nutrition**, where organisms make their own food (e.g., green plants).
* **Heterotrophic nutrition**, where organisms depend on others for food (e.g., humans and animals).

---

## 3. Differences Between Living and Non-Living Things

Living things:

* Grow naturally.
* Reproduce.
* Respire.
* Are made of cells.
* Respond to stimuli.

Non-living things:

* Do not grow naturally.
* Cannot reproduce.
* Do not respire.
* Are not made of cells.
* Do not respond to stimuli.

---

## 4. The Cell: Basic Unit of Life

All living organisms are made up of cells. A **cell** is the smallest structural and functional unit of life.

The discovery of cells is linked to the work of Robert Hooke, who observed cork under a microscope. Later, Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann contributed to the development of the Cell Theory.

### Cell Theory

1. All living organisms are made of one or more cells.
2. The cell is the basic unit of life.
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Cells carry out all life processes.

---

## 5. Types of Cells

There are two main types of cells:

* Plant cells
* Animal cells

Both have similarities but also important differences.

---

## 6. Structure and Functions of a Plant Cell

A plant cell contains the following main parts:

* **Cell wall**: A rigid outer layer that provides support and protection.
* **Cell membrane**: Controls what enters and leaves the cell.
* **Cytoplasm**: Jelly-like substance where chemical reactions occur.
* **Nucleus**: Controls cell activities and contains genetic material.
* **Chloroplast**: Contains chlorophyll and carries out photosynthesis.
* **Vacuole**: Stores water, food, and waste materials.
* **Mitochondria**: Releases energy during respiration.

Plant cells are usually rectangular due to the presence of the cell wall.

---

## 7. Structure and Functions of an Animal Cell

An animal cell contains:

* **Cell membrane**
* **Cytoplasm**
* **Nucleus**
* **Mitochondria**
* **Small vacuoles**

Animal cells do not have a cell wall or chloroplast. They are usually round or irregular in shape.

---

## 8. Comparison Between Plant and Animal Cells

Plant cells:

* Have a cell wall.
* Contain chloroplasts.
* Have a large central vacuole.
* Usually rectangular.

Animal cells:

* Lack a cell wall.
* Do not contain chloroplasts.
* Have small vacuoles.
* Usually round or irregular.

Both cells contain nucleus, cytoplasm, mitochondria, and cell membrane.

---

## 9. Levels of Organization in Living Organisms

Living organisms are organized in increasing levels of complexity:

**Cell → Tissue → Organ → System → Organism**

* A **cell** is the basic unit of life.
* A **tissue** is a group of similar cells performing the same function.
* An **organ** is a group of tissues working together.
* A **system** is a group of organs working together.
* An **organism** is a complete living individual.

This organization allows specialization and efficiency.

---

## 10. Unicellular and Multicellular Organisms

A **unicellular organism** consists of only one cell. That single cell performs all life processes.

A **multicellular organism** consists of many cells. Different cells specialize to perform different functions.

Unicellular organisms are usually simple, while multicellular organisms are more complex.

---

## 11. Viruses

Viruses are very small particles that can only reproduce inside living cells. Outside a host cell, they behave like non-living matter.

They do not grow, respire, or reproduce independently. Because of this, viruses are considered to have characteristics of both living and non-living things.

---

## 12. Habitat

A **habitat** is the natural environment where an organism lives.

Types of habitat include:

* Terrestrial (land)
* Aquatic (water)
* Arboreal (trees)
* Amphibious (land and water)

Each organism is adapted to survive in its habitat.

---

## 13. Adaptation

Adaptation refers to structural, behavioral, or physiological features that enable an organism to survive in its environment.

Examples:

* Fish have gills for breathing in water.
* Cactus plants have spines to reduce water loss.
* Camels store fat to survive in deserts.

Adaptations increase chances of survival and reproduction.

---

## 14. Classification of Living Organisms

Classification is the grouping of organisms based on similarities and differences. It helps scientists identify and study organisms effectively.

Living organisms are grouped into five kingdoms:

* Monera
* Protista
* Fungi
* Plantae
* Animalia

Each kingdom has unique characteristics based on cell type, nutrition, and organization.

---

## Conclusion

Living organisms are complex systems made up of cells and capable of carrying out life processes. They grow, respire, reproduce, respond to stimuli, and adapt to their environments. Understanding cells, levels of organization, and classification provides the foundation for advanced biological study. Mastery of these concepts develops strong scientific thinking and prepares students for both local and international examinations.
