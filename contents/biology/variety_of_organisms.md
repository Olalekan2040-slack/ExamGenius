# VARIETY OF ORGANISMS

*(Structural, Functional and Behavioural Adaptations – Comprehensive Guide)*

---

# 1. INTRODUCTION TO ADAPTATION

Adaptation refers to special features or behaviours that enable an organism to survive in its environment.

Organisms live in different habitats such as deserts, forests, rivers, swamps and grasslands. To survive in these environments, they develop adaptations.

Adaptations can be grouped into:

1. Structural (physical features of the body)
2. Functional (physiological processes inside the body)
3. Behavioural (actions or patterns of behaviour)

Adaptation ensures survival, reproduction and continuation of species.

---

# 2. STRUCTURAL ADAPTATIONS

Structural adaptations are physical features that help organisms survive.

They can be seen externally.

---

## 2.1 Adaptations for Obtaining Food

Different organisms have different feeding structures depending on their diet.

### (A) Beaks of Birds

Bird beaks are adapted to the type of food they eat.

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Different types of bird beaks showing short thick beak, long slender beak, hooked beak]

Examples:

* Short, thick beak (e.g., chicken) – for cracking seeds
* Long slender beak (e.g., sunbird) – for sucking nectar
* Hooked beak (e.g., hawk) – for tearing flesh

Beak shape reflects feeding habit.

---

### (B) Legs of Birds

* Webbed feet (duck) – swimming
* Strong claws (eagle) – catching prey
* Long legs (heron) – wading in shallow water

---

### (C) Mouthparts of Insects

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Diagram of mosquito, butterfly and grasshopper mouthparts]

Different insects have specialized mouthparts:

* Mosquito – piercing and sucking
* Butterfly and moth – siphoning (long proboscis)
* Grasshopper – biting and chewing

These adaptations help them obtain food efficiently.

---

## 2.2 Adaptations for Protection and Defence

Organisms must protect themselves from predators.

### (A) Camouflage

Camouflage allows organisms to blend with their surroundings.

Examples:

* Stick insect resembles a twig
* Praying mantis looks like a leaf

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Stick insect blending with branch]

This reduces visibility to predators.

---

### (B) Defensive Structures

* Toad secretes poisonous substance
* Porcupine has quills
* Snail has hard shell

These structures prevent attack.

---

## 2.3 Adaptations for Securing Mates

Reproduction is essential for survival of species.

Some organisms develop features to attract mates.

Example:

* Male Agama lizard develops bright red head during breeding season.
* Birds perform courtship displays (dancing, spreading feathers).

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Male Agama with red head display]

Bright colours and displays signal strength and readiness to mate.

---

## 2.4 Adaptations for Regulating Body Temperature

Animals regulate body temperature differently.

### (A) Skin

* Thick skin reduces water loss.
* Sweating helps cooling in humans.

### (B) Feathers

Birds use feathers to:

* Retain heat
* Protect from cold

### (C) Hair or Fur

Mammals use hair to:

* Insulate body
* Maintain constant temperature

Warm-blooded animals (birds and mammals) regulate body temperature internally.

Cold-blooded animals depend on environment.

---

## 2.5 Adaptations for Conserving Water

Organisms in dry areas must reduce water loss.

Examples:

* Cactus has spines instead of leaves (reduce transpiration).
* Thick waxy cuticle prevents water loss.
* Reptiles have scales to reduce evaporation.

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Cactus showing spines]

These features allow survival in desert conditions.

---

# 3. FUNCTIONAL ADAPTATIONS

Functional adaptations involve internal physiological processes.

Examples:

* Production of concentrated urine in desert animals
* Venom production in snakes
* Ability of camel to store fat for energy

These processes occur inside the body and improve survival.

---

# 4. BEHAVIOURAL ADAPTATIONS

Behavioural adaptations are actions organisms take to survive.

---

## 4.1 Adaptive Colouration

Colouration helps organisms survive.

### (A) Countershading

Countershading is when the upper part of the body is darker and the lower part is lighter.

Examples:

* Fish
* Toads
* Snakes

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Fish showing dark back and light belly]

Function:

* From above, dark back blends with water depth.
* From below, light belly blends with sunlight.

This protects from predators.

---

### (B) Warning Colouration

Some organisms have bright colours to warn predators.

Example:

* Poisonous mushrooms

Bright colours signal danger.

Predators learn to avoid them.

---

# 5. BEHAVIOURAL ADAPTATIONS IN SOCIAL ANIMALS

Some animals live in groups called colonies.

Example: Termites.

---

## 5.1 Castes in Termites

Termites have different groups called castes.

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Diagram of termite colony showing queen, workers, soldiers]

### (A) Queen

* Reproductive female
* Lays eggs

### (B) King

* Fertilizes queen

### (C) Workers

* Build nest
* Feed colony
* Care for young

### (D) Soldiers

* Defend colony

Each caste has specialized structure and function.

This division of labour ensures survival.

---

# 6. SPECIAL BEHAVIOURAL RESPONSES

## 6.1 Basking in Lizards

Lizards are cold-blooded.

They bask in the sun to:

* Raise body temperature
* Increase metabolic activity

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Lizard basking on rock]

---

## 6.2 Hibernation

Hibernation occurs during cold conditions.

Animals:

* Reduce body activity
* Sleep for long period

Example: Some rodents.

---

## 6.3 Aestivation

Aestivation occurs during hot, dry conditions.

Animals:

* Become inactive
* Hide underground

Example: Toads and snails.

---

## 6.4 Territorial Behaviour

Some animals defend territory to:

* Secure food
* Attract mates
* Protect offspring

This reduces competition.

---

# 7. SUMMARY OF ADAPTIVE STRATEGIES

Adaptations can be grouped as:

Structural:

* Beaks
* Legs
* Spines
* Scales

Functional:

* Venom production
* Water conservation
* Temperature regulation

Behavioural:

* Basking
* Migration
* Hibernation
* Territorial defence

---

# 8. OVERALL IMPORTANCE OF ADAPTATION

Adaptations allow organisms to:

* Obtain food
* Escape predators
* Reproduce successfully
* Survive extreme conditions
* Maintain ecological balance

Without adaptation, organisms cannot survive environmental changes.

---

# CONCLUSION

The variety of organisms on Earth exists because of adaptation. From insect mouthparts to bird beaks, from countershading in fish to caste systems in termites, each organism possesses special features suited to its environment and way of life.

Understanding adaptation helps explain biodiversity, survival strategies, and ecological balance. Mastery of these concepts builds strong analytical skills required for advanced biological studies and competitive examinations.
