TOPIC: TrigonometryIntroductionTrigonometry is the branch of mathematics that studies relationships between the sides and angles of triangles, especially right-angled triangles. It is one of the most practical and frequently tested topics in JAMB Mathematics because it appears in real-life problems such as surveying, navigation (bearings), heights of buildings/trees (angles of elevation/depression), wave graphs, and area calculations.For a complete beginner:
Trigonometry uses three main ratios: sine (sin), cosine (cos), and tangent (tan).
In a right-angled triangle:  sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse  
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse  
tan θ = opposite / adjacent

These ratios help solve for unknown sides or angles when some are known.This topic is extremely high-scoring in JAMB – usually 6–10 questions every year (sometimes more).
Questions test:Values of sin, cos, tan for special angles (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°, etc., and multiples up to 360°)
Angles of elevation and depression (real-life heights/distances)
Bearings (direction from north)
Area of triangles using (1/2)ab sin C
Sine and cosine rule for non-right triangles
Graphs of y = sin x and y = cos x

World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international standards):
Candidates should be able to:Calculate sine, cosine, and tangent of any angle between –360° and 360° using special angle values and quadrant rules

Quadrant rules (ASTC):Quadrant I (0°–90°): All positive  
Quadrant II (90°–180°): Sin positive  
Quadrant III (180°–270°): Tan positive  
Quadrant IV (270°–360°): Cos positive

Key Points:Negative angles: Use co-function (sin(–θ) = –sin θ, etc.)
For angles > 360° or < –360°: Reduce by multiples of 360°

Visual Type: unit circle & quadrant chart
Visual Items:  Unit circle with special angles and signs

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: Find sin 150°.
Answer: sin(180° – 30°) = sin 30° = 1/2
Medium: Calculate cos(–210°).
Answer: cos(–210°) = cos 210° = cos(180° + 30°) = –cos 30° = –√3/2
Hard: Find tan 315°.
Answer: tan(360° – 45°) = tan(–45°) = –tan 45° = –1
Advanced: If sin θ = √3/2 and θ is between 90° and 180°, find cos θ and tan θ.
Answer: Quadrant II → cos θ = –1/2, tan θ = –√3

Common Mistakes:Forgetting ASTC signs  
Wrong reduction for angles outside 0°–360°

SUBTOPIC: Angles of Elevation and DepressionObjective 2: Solve problems involving angles of elevation and depressionExplanation:
Angle of elevation → angle above horizontal when looking up
Angle of depression → angle below horizontal when looking down
They form right-angled triangles with tan, sin, cos.Key Steps:Draw diagram with horizontal line  
Mark angle of elevation/depression  
Identify opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse  
Use tan = opp/adj (most common)

Visual Type: real-life diagram
Visual Items:  Person looking up to top of building (elevation)  
Person looking down from cliff to boat (depression)

Example Question Pattern:Simple: From a point 20 m from the foot of a vertical pole, the angle of elevation to the top is 60°. Find the height of the pole.
Answer: tan 60° = h/20 → h = 20√3 m
Medium: From the top of a 50 m cliff, the angle of depression to a boat is 30°. Find distance from base of cliff to boat.
Answer: tan 30° = 50/d → d = 50 / (1/√3) = 50√3 m
Hard: Two observers 100 m apart on level ground see the top of a tower at angles of elevation 40° and 55°. Find height of tower.
(Use two right triangles and tan; solve system of equations)
Advanced: A plane at height 2000 m sees airport at angle of depression 15°. Find horizontal distance.
Answer: tan 15° = 2000/d → d = 2000 / tan 15° ≈ 7456 m

Common Mistakes:Confusing elevation and depression  
Using sin instead of tan

SUBTOPIC: BearingsObjective 3: Solve problems involving bearingsExplanation:
Bearings are measured clockwise from north (0°–360°), written as three figures (e.g., 045°, 270°).Key Rules:North = 000° or 360°  
East = 090°  
South = 180°  
West = 270°  
Back bearing = forward bearing ± 180° (adjust to 0°–360°)

Visual Type: compass rose diagram
Visual Items:  8 main directions with bearings  
Example of forward and back bearing

Example Question Pattern:Simple: A ship sails on bearing 120°. What is the back bearing?
Answer: 120° + 180° = 300°
Medium: From A to B is bearing 065°. From B to C is bearing 140°. Find bearing from A to C.
(Use scale diagram or cosine rule in triangle)
Hard: Two towns P and Q are 10 km apart on bearing 075° from P. A third town R is 8 km from P on bearing 150°. Find distance PR and bearing of R from Q.
(Use cosine rule and bearings calculation)

Common Mistakes:Measuring anticlockwise instead of clockwise  
Forgetting three figures (e.g., 45° instead of 045°)

SUBTOPIC: Areas and Solutions of TrianglesObjective 4: Apply trigonometric formulae to find areas of trianglesExplanation:Area of any triangle = (1/2) ab sin C
(where a, b are two sides, C is included angle)Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R
Cosine rule: c² = a² + b² – 2ab cos CVisual Type: triangle diagrams
Visual Items:  Area formula with included angle  
Sine and cosine rule triangles

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm, included angle 60°. Find area.
Answer: (1/2) × 6 × 8 × sin 60° = 24 × (√3/2) = 12√3 cm²
Medium: In triangle ABC, AB = 10 cm, BC = 7 cm, ∠B = 50°. Find area.
Answer: (1/2) × 10 × 7 × sin 50° ≈ 26.8 cm²
Hard: Use sine rule to find side a if ∠A = 40°, b = 12 cm, ∠B = 60°.
Answer: a/sin 40° = 12/sin 60° → a = 12 × sin 40° / sin 60° ≈ 8.9 cm

Common Mistakes:Using wrong included angle  
Forgetting 1/2 in area formula
