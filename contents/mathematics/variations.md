TOPIC: VariationIntroductionVariation describes how one quantity changes in relation to one or more other quantities. It is one of the most practical and frequently tested topics in JAMB Mathematics because it appears in real-life situations such as speed and time, cost and quantity, work and workers, pressure and volume of gas, etc.For a complete beginner:
Think of variation as the "relationship rule" between two or more things.  When one increases and the other also increases → direct  
When one increases and the other decreases → inverse  
When more than two quantities are involved → joint or partial

This topic is extremely important in JAMB – usually 3–6 questions every year, often involving real-life word problems that require setting up the variation equation and solving.World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international curricula):
Candidates should be able to:Recognize, formulate, and solve problems involving direct variation (y ∝ x), inverse variation (y ∝ 1/x), joint variation (y ∝ xz), and partial variation (y = a + bx or similar mixed forms)
Express variation relationships using constants of proportionality (k)
Translate worded problems into mathematical variation equations and solve for unknowns
Calculate and interpret percentage increase and percentage decrease in quantities that vary
Apply variation concepts to real-life contexts (cost, speed, work, physics laws like Boyle’s law, etc.)
Use graphs to visualize variation relationships (straight line through origin for direct, hyperbola for inverse)

If y varies directly as x, then y ∝ x or y = kx (k = constant of variation).
When one quantity doubles, the other doubles too.Key Points:To find k: k = y/x  
Use k to find new values

Example Question Pattern (from simple to hard):Simple: If y varies directly as x and y = 15 when x = 5, find y when x = 12.
(k = 15/5 = 3 → y = 3 × 12 = 36)
Medium: The cost of 8 oranges is ₦200. How much will 25 oranges cost if cost varies directly with number of oranges?
(k = 200/8 = 25 → cost = 25 × 25 = ₦625)
Hard: The distance travelled by a car varies directly with time at constant speed. If it covers 240 km in 3 hours, how far will it go in 5.5 hours?
(k = 240/3 = 80 km/h → distance = 80 × 5.5 = 440 km)
Advanced: The power P developed by an electric motor varies directly as the cube of the speed v. If P = 1250 W at v = 10 m/s, find P at v = 15 m/s.
(k = 1250 / 10³ = 1250/1000 = 1.25 → P = 1.25 × 15³ = 1.25 × 3375 = 4218.75 W)

Common Mistakes:Forgetting to find k first
Using multiplication instead of proportion when values change

SUBTOPIC: Inverse VariationObjective 2: Solve problems involving inverse variationExplanation:
If y varies inversely as x, then y ∝ 1/x or y = k/x or xy = k.
When one quantity doubles, the other halves.Key Points:Constant k = x × y (product is constant)
Graphs: rectangular hyperbola

Example Question Pattern:Simple: If y varies inversely as x and y = 12 when x = 4, find y when x = 6.
(k = 12 × 4 = 48 → y = 48/6 = 8)
Medium: The time taken to complete a job varies inversely with the number of workers. If 10 workers finish in 12 days, how long will 15 workers take?
(k = 10 × 12 = 120 → time = 120/15 = 8 days)
Hard: The pressure P of a fixed mass of gas varies inversely as its volume V at constant temperature (Boyle’s law). If P = 120 kPa when V = 5 dm³, find P when V = 8 dm³.
(k = 120 × 5 = 600 → P = 600/8 = 75 kPa)
Advanced: The intensity I of light from a lamp varies inversely as the square of the distance d from the lamp. If I = 64 units at d = 3 m, find I at d = 8 m.
(I ∝ 1/d² → k = 64 × 3² = 576 → I = 576 / 8² = 576 / 64 = 9 units)

Common Mistakes:Using direct variation formula instead of inverse
Forgetting to square the variable when it’s inverse square

SUBTOPIC: Joint Variation and Partial VariationObjective 3: Solve problems involving joint and partial variationExplanation:
Joint variation: y varies jointly as x and z → y ∝ xz or y = kxz
Partial variation: y varies partly as x and partly constant → y = a + bx (linear partial) or similar formsKey Points:Joint: Find k using one set of values
Partial: Need two sets of values to solve for a and b

Example Question Pattern:Simple (joint): y varies jointly as x and z. If y = 60 when x = 3 and z = 4, find y when x = 5 and z = 6.
(k = 60/(3×4) = 5 → y = 5 × 5 × 6 = 150)
Medium (joint): The volume V of a gas varies jointly with temperature T and inversely with pressure P. If V = 200 cm³ when T = 400 K and P = 100 kPa, find V when T = 600 K and P = 150 kPa.
(k = 200/(400/100) = 50 → V = 50 × 600 / 150 = 200 cm³)
Hard (partial): The cost C of electricity varies partly as a fixed charge and partly as the units used u. When 100 units are used, cost is ₦2500; when 250 units are used, cost is ₦4750. Find the cost when 400 units are used.
(C = a + bu → 2500 = a + 100b, 4750 = a + 250b → b = 30, a = 500 → C = 500 + 30×400 = ₦17,500)
Advanced (joint + inverse): The force F between two objects varies jointly as their masses m₁ and m₂ and inversely as the square of distance d. If F = 100 N when m₁ = 20 kg, m₂ = 50 kg, d = 2 m, find F when m₁ = 30 kg, m₂ = 40 kg, d = 5 m.
(k = 100 × 4 / (20×50) = 0.4 → F = 0.4 × 30 × 40 / 25 = 19.2 N)

Common Mistakes:Treating joint as direct only (forgetting multiple variables)
Solving partial variation with only one equation (need two points)

SUBTOPIC: Percentage Increase and Decrease in VariationObjective 4: Solve problems on percentage increase and decrease in variationExplanation:
Percentage change = (change / original) × 100%
In variation problems, when one quantity changes by a certain percentage, the dependent quantity changes by a multiple of that percentage depending on the power.Key Rules:Direct variation y = kx → % change in y = % change in x  
Inverse variation y = k/x → % change in y = –% change in x  
y ∝ xⁿ → % change in y ≈ n × % change in x (for small changes)

Example Question Pattern:Simple: If x increases by 20% and y varies directly as x, what is the percentage increase in y?
(Answer: 20%)
Medium: If x decreases by 30% and y varies inversely as x, what is the percentage increase in y?
(Answer: 30% / (100% – 30%) × 100% = 42.86%)
Hard: The area A of a circle varies directly as the square of the radius r. If r increases by 25%, what is the percentage increase in A?
(A ∝ r² → % increase ≈ 2 × 25% = 56.25%)
Advanced: The time t to complete a job varies inversely as the number of workers w. If the number of workers is increased by 40%, what is the percentage decrease in time?
(t ∝ 1/w → % change in t ≈ –40% / (100% + 40%) × 100% = –28.57%)

Common Mistakes:Using direct percentage change for inverse variation
Forgetting to use the power when variable is squared/cubed

Final High-Level Tips for World-Class Mastery:Always write the variation equation first (y = kx, y = k/x, etc.)
Find k using given values
For percentage changes, use the power rule for small changes
Practice real-life problems (cost, speed, work, gas laws)
Draw variation graphs when possible – it helps visualize
