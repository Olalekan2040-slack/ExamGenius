TOPIC: Euclidean GeometryIntroductionEuclidean Geometry is the study of shapes, sizes, positions, and properties of points, lines, angles, polygons, and circles based on Euclid's axioms. It forms the foundation of plane geometry and is one of the most important sections in JAMB Mathematics.For a complete beginner:
Imagine geometry as the mathematics of shapes and space on a flat surface (plane).
You will learn how lines, angles, triangles, quadrilaterals, polygons, and circles behave, how angles relate to each other, and how to construct exact angles using only a compass and ruler.This topic is extremely high-scoring in JAMB – usually 6–10 questions every year (sometimes more).
Questions test:Identification of angle types and line properties  
Angle calculations in polygons and circles  
Application of circle theorems (very common)  
Construction of special angles (30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, 90°, etc.)

World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international standards):
Candidates should be able to:Identify and classify lines (parallel, perpendicular, transversal) and angles (acute, obtuse, reflex, complementary, supplementary, vertically opposite, corresponding, alternate, co-interior)
Solve problems involving angle sums in polygons (triangles = 180°, quadrilaterals = 360°, n-sided polygon = (n–2)×180°)
Apply circle theorems accurately to calculate angles (angle at centre, angle in semicircle, angles in same segment, cyclic quadrilaterals, intersecting chords, tangent-chord, alternate segment)
Perform basic constructions using compass and ruler to create special angles (30°, 45°, 60°, 90°, 75° = 45° + 30°, etc.)
Interpret and solve real-life geometry problems (e.g., tiling, shadows, navigation) using geometric properties

Perpendicular lines → meet at 90°  
Transversal → line crossing two or more lines

Angles (when transversal crosses parallel lines):Corresponding angles → equal  
Alternate angles → equal  
Co-interior angles → sum to 180° (supplementary)

Other angles:Vertically opposite → equal  
Adjacent angles on straight line → sum to 180°  
Angles around a point → sum to 360°

Visual Type: labelled diagrams
Visual Items:  Parallel lines + transversal with all angle pairs  
Vertically opposite angles  
Angles around a point

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal and one angle is 65°, find the corresponding angle.
Answer: 65° (corresponding angles are equal)
Medium: In the diagram, if ∠1 = 70° and lines are parallel, find ∠2 (alternate) and ∠3 (co-interior).
Answer: ∠2 = 70° (alternate), ∠3 = 110° (co-interior = 180° – 70°)
Hard: Two parallel lines are cut by two transversals forming a Z-shape. If one interior angle is 125°, find all other marked angles.
Answer: Co-interior pair = 55° each, vertically opposite = 125°, corresponding = 125°, etc.
Advanced (JAMB diagram question): Given a diagram with multiple transversals and parallel lines, calculate the value of x where several angles are given (e.g., 3x + 20°, 2x + 50°, etc.).
(Use co-interior or alternate angles to form equation)

Common Mistakes:Confusing alternate and corresponding angles  
Forgetting to reverse inequality when solving angle equations

SUBTOPIC: Polygons – Triangles, Quadrilaterals, and General PolygonsObjective 2: Solve problems involving polygonsExplanation:Triangle angle sum = 180°
Quadrilateral angle sum = 360°
n-sided polygon interior angle sum = (n – 2) × 180°
Exterior angle sum of any polygon = 360°Key Properties:Equilateral triangle → all sides equal, all angles 60°  
Isosceles → two sides equal, base angles equal  
Parallelogram → opposite sides equal & parallel, opposite angles equal, consecutive angles supplementary  
Rectangle → all angles 90°, opposite sides equal  
Rhombus → all sides equal, diagonals bisect at 90°  
Square → all sides equal, all angles 90°

Visual Type: polygon charts
Visual Items:  Angle sum table for polygons  
Properties of special quadrilaterals

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Find the sum of interior angles of a hexagon.
Answer: (6 – 2) × 180° = 720°
Medium: In a quadrilateral, three angles are 80°, 100°, and 110°. Find the fourth angle.
Answer: 360° – (80 + 100 + 110) = 70°
Hard: The exterior angle of a regular polygon is 36°. Find the number of sides.
Answer: Number of sides = 360° / 36° = 10 sides
Advanced (JAMB diagram question): In parallelogram ABCD, diagonal AC is drawn. If ∠BAC = 35° and ∠ACD = 25°, find ∠BAD.
(Use alternate angles and triangle sums)

Common Mistakes:Using exterior angle sum instead of interior  
Forgetting (n–2) in polygon sum formula

SUBTOPIC: Circles – Angle Properties, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Intersecting ChordsObjective 3: Calculate angles using circle theoremsExplanation (most important JAMB circle theorems):Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference (subtended by same arc)  
Angle in a semicircle = 90°  
Angles in the same segment are equal  
Opposite angles of cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°  
Exterior angle of cyclic quadrilateral = opposite interior angle  
Intersecting chords theorem: Products of segments equal (PA×PB = PC×PD)  
Tangent-chord theorem: Angle between tangent and chord = angle in alternate segment

Visual Type: theorem diagrams
Visual Items:  All major circle theorems illustrated with examples  
Cyclic quadrilateral opposite angles  
Intersecting chords theorem

Example Question Pattern:Simple: In a circle, angle at centre is 120°. Find angle at circumference subtended by same arc.
Answer: 60° (half)
Medium: ABCD is cyclic quadrilateral. If ∠ABC = 70°, find ∠ADC.
Answer: 110° (opposite angles sum to 180°)
Hard: Two chords AB and CD intersect at P. If AP = 4 cm, PB = 6 cm, CP = 3 cm, find PD.
Answer: 4 × 6 = 3 × PD → PD = 8 cm
Advanced (JAMB diagram question): Tangent at point T touches circle, chord TB is drawn. If angle between tangent and chord is 40°, find angle in alternate segment (angle TCB).
Answer: 40° (tangent-chord theorem)

Common Mistakes:Forgetting angle at centre is twice angle at circumference  
Using wrong theorem for intersecting chords vs secants

SUBTOPIC: ConstructionObjective 4: Identify construction procedures of special anglesExplanation (compass and ruler only – no protractor):Standard constructions:90° → perpendicular bisector  
60° → equilateral triangle  
45° → bisect 90°  
30° → bisect 60°  
15° → bisect 30°  
75° = 45° + 30°

Steps for key angles:60°: Draw line, compass from one end draw arc, intersect with another arc  
90°: Perpendicular from a point on line  
45°: Bisect 90° angle

Visual Type: step-by-step construction guides
Visual Items:  Construction of 60° angle

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Describe how to construct 90° angle using compass and ruler.
Answer: Draw line, mark point, draw arc, from intersection points draw arcs to meet at point, join to original point
Medium: Explain steps to construct 45° angle.
Answer: Construct 90°, bisect it using compass
Hard: Outline construction of 75° angle.
Answer: Construct 90°, then subtract 15° (or add 30° to 45°)
Advanced (JAMB diagram question): Given a line segment AB, construct a 60° angle at A, then construct 30° angle within it.

Common Mistakes:Using protractor in description (not allowed)  
Forgetting arcs must intersect for equal lengths
