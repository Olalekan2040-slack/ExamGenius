TOPIC: Measures of DispersionIntroductionMeasures of dispersion tell us how spread out or scattered the data values are around the central value (mean, median, or mode).
While measures of location (mean, median, mode) show the "typical" value, dispersion shows the variability or consistency of the data.For a complete beginner:  If all students score around 70–75 → low dispersion (data is consistent)  
If scores range from 20 to 95 → high dispersion (data is spread out)

This topic is extremely important in JAMB Mathematics – usually 4–7 questions every year.
JAMB focuses on:Calculating range (simple)  
Mean deviation (average distance from mean)  
Variance and standard deviation (most powerful measures of spread)  
Both ungrouped and grouped (simple cases) data

World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international curricula):
Candidates should be able to:Calculate the range as a basic measure of spread for both ungrouped and grouped data
Compute the mean deviation (average absolute deviation from the mean) for ungrouped and grouped data
Calculate variance (average of squared deviations from the mean) for ungrouped and grouped data
Find the standard deviation (square root of variance) as the most reliable measure of dispersion
Understand and interpret the meaning of standard deviation in real-life contexts (e.g., consistency of scores, risk in finance)
Apply these measures to compare variability between two or more datasets
Use grouped data techniques (mid-class values, frequency) accurately for dispersion calculations

Grouped: Upper boundary of highest class – lower boundary of lowest class
(JAMB usually uses inclusive class limits)

Key Points:Quickest and easiest measure  
Very sensitive to extreme values (outliers)  
Does not show distribution inside the range

Visual Type: number line
Visual Items:  Data points on number line with range arrow  
Grouped classes with range highlighted

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: Scores: 45, 52, 60, 38, 75, 49. Find the range.
Answer: 75 – 38 = 37
Medium: In grouped data, lowest class 20–29, highest class 80–89. Find range.
Answer: 89 – 20 = 69
Hard: Two sets of data have same mean but different ranges. Which set is more consistent?
Answer: Smaller range is more consistent
Advanced (JAMB word problem): Heights of plants in cm: 15, 18, 20, 22, 35, 16. A new plant of 50 cm is added. Find new range and explain effect.
Answer: Old range = 35–15 = 20 cm
New range = 50–15 = 35 cm → increased spread

Common Mistakes:Using class marks instead of boundaries for grouped range  
Forgetting to include inclusive limits

SUBTOPIC 2: Mean DeviationObjective 2: Calculate mean deviation for ungrouped and grouped dataExplanation:Mean deviation = average of absolute deviations from the mean
Mean deviation = (sum of |x – mean|) / nFor grouped data:
Mean deviation = (sum of f|x – mean|) / total frequency
(Use mid-class value for x)Key Points:Uses absolute value → ignores direction  
Less affected by outliers than range  
Less common than standard deviation in advanced stats

Visual Type: deviation table
Visual Items:  Table with x, |x – mean|, frequency, f|x – mean|  
Highlighted mean

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Data: 10, 12, 14, 16, 18. Mean = 14. Find mean deviation.
Answer: |10–14| + |12–14| + … = 4+2+0+2+4 = 12 → 12/5 = 2.4
Medium: Grouped data with mid-values and frequencies given. Calculate mean first, then mean deviation.
(Step-by-step table calculation)
Hard: Compare mean deviation of two distributions with same mean.
(Smaller mean deviation → more consistent)

Common Mistakes:Forgetting absolute value (using signed deviation)  
Using class limits instead of mid-values

SUBTOPIC 3: Variance and Standard DeviationObjective 3: Calculate variance and standard deviation for ungrouped and grouped dataExplanation (most important JAMB measures):Variance = average of squared deviations from the mean  Ungrouped: σ² = Σ(x – mean)² / n  
Grouped: σ² = Σf(x – mean)² / n

Standard deviation = √variance
(σ or s – square root brings unit back to original scale)Key Points:Variance uses squared deviations → always positive  
Standard deviation is most reliable – used in real science, finance, quality control  
JAMB uses population formula (divide by n, not n–1)

Visual Type: deviation squared table
Visual Items:  Step-by-step table for variance  
Bell curve with ±1σ, ±2σ shaded

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: Data: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. Mean = 8. Find variance and standard deviation.
Answer: Deviations squared: 16, 4, 0, 4, 16 → sum = 40
Variance = 40/5 = 8
S.D. = √8 = 2√2 ≈ 2.83
Medium: Grouped data with mid-values, frequencies. Calculate variance.
(Use table: mid-value, deviation, squared, f×squared, sum, divide by total freq)
Hard: Two classes have same mean but different standard deviations. Which is more reliable?
Answer: Smaller S.D. → more consistent (less spread)
Advanced (JAMB word problem): Scores of 100 students have mean 60 and standard deviation 10. What percentage of students score between 50 and 70?
Answer: 50 = mean – 1σ, 70 = mean + 1σ → approximately 68% (normal distribution rule)

Common Mistakes:Using sample variance (divide by n–1) instead of population (divide by n)  
Forgetting square root for standard deviation  
Wrong mid-values in grouped calculation

Final High-Level Tips for JAMB Success:Memorise formulas:
Range = max – min
Mean deviation = Σ|x – mean| / n
Variance = Σ(x – mean)² / n
S.D. = √variance
For grouped data: always use mid-class values  
Practice full tables for variance/standard deviation – most errors are in calculation  
Interpret: smaller dispersion → more consistent data
