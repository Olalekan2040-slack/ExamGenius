TOPIC: LociIntroductionA locus (plural: loci) is the set of all points that satisfy a given condition or geometric rule. In 2-dimensional geometry (plane), loci are lines or curves formed by moving points that obey specific rules.For a complete beginner:
Think of a locus as the "path" or "shape" traced by a point that follows a strict rule.
Example: All points exactly 5 cm from a fixed point → a circle of radius 5 cm.
This topic is very important in JAMB Mathematics – usually 3–6 questions every year, often involving diagrams where you identify or describe the locus.World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international curricula):
Candidates should be able to:Define a locus as the set of points satisfying a given geometric condition
Identify and interpret the locus of points that are:Equidistant from two fixed points (perpendicular bisector)
Equidistant from two fixed lines (angle bisector)
At a fixed distance from a fixed line (parallel lines)
At a fixed distance from a fixed point (circle)

Describe and construct loci involving combinations of the above (e.g., intersection points)
Solve locus problems using geometric reasoning, diagrams, and constructions
Apply locus concepts to real-life situations (e.g., paths, boundaries, equidistant points)

The set of all points that are equidistant from two fixed points A and B is the perpendicular bisector of the line segment AB.Key Properties:It is a straight line  
It passes through the midpoint of AB  
It is perpendicular to AB  
Any point on it is equally distant from A and B

Visual Type: labelled diagram
Visual Items:  Line segment AB with midpoint M  
Perpendicular bisector passing through M  
Points P on bisector with PA = PB

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: Describe the locus of points equidistant from two fixed points A and B.
Answer: The perpendicular bisector of segment AB
Medium: In a diagram, points A(2,3) and B(6,7) are given. Describe the locus of points equidistant from A and B.
Answer: The perpendicular bisector of AB (find midpoint and slope)
Hard: Two villages A and B are 10 km apart. A new hospital is to be built so that it is equidistant from A and B. Describe the possible locations.
Answer: Along the perpendicular bisector of AB
Advanced (JAMB diagram question): Given points A and B, the locus of points equidistant from A and B intersects the line segment AB at its midpoint. Explain why.

Common Mistakes:Thinking it’s the line joining A and B  
Forgetting it is perpendicular

SUBTOPIC: Locus of Points Equidistant from Two Fixed Lines (Angle Bisector)Objective 2: Identify and interpret the angle bisector as a locusExplanation:
The set of all points that are equidistant from two fixed intersecting lines is the angle bisector (or bisectors if two angles are formed).Key Properties:It divides the angle into two equal parts  
Any point on the bisector is equally distant from both lines (perpendicular distance)  
There are two bisectors for two intersecting lines (acute and obtuse angles)

Visual Type: diagram
Visual Items:  Two intersecting lines forming angles  
Angle bisectors drawn  
Perpendicular distances from point on bisector to both lines are equal

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Describe the locus of points equidistant from two intersecting straight lines.
Answer: The angle bisectors of the angles formed by the lines
Medium: Two roads intersect at point O forming 70° and 110° angles. Describe the locus of points equidistant from both roads.
Answer: The two angle bisectors (one for 70°, one for 110°)
Hard: A point P is equidistant from two intersecting lines. Explain why P lies on the angle bisector.
Answer: Perpendicular distances to both lines are equal → reflection property

Common Mistakes:Thinking it’s only the bisector of the acute angle  
Confusing with equidistant from points (perpendicular bisector)

SUBTOPIC: Locus of Points at a Fixed Distance from a Fixed Line (Parallel Lines)Objective 3: Identify parallel lines as a locusExplanation:
The set of all points at a fixed distance (say d) from a given straight line is two parallel lines, one on each side of the original line.Key Properties:Both new lines are parallel to the original  
Distance between each new line and original = d  
The original line is the midline

Visual Type: parallel lines diagram
Visual Items:  One fixed line  
Two parallel lines at distance d on both sides  
Perpendicular arrows showing distance

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Describe the locus of points at a distance of 5 cm from a straight line AB.
Answer: Two straight lines parallel to AB, each 5 cm away on either side
Medium: A river is represented by a straight line. The locus of points 100 m from the river bank is required for a safety zone. Describe it.
Answer: Two parallel lines, 100 m on each side of the river

Common Mistakes:Saying it’s a single line  
Forgetting there are two lines (one on each side)

SUBTOPIC: Locus of Points at a Fixed Distance from a Fixed Point (Circle)Objective 4: Identify the circle as a locusExplanation:
The set of all points at a fixed distance (radius r) from a fixed point (centre O) is a circle with centre O and radius r.Key Properties:All points on circle are equidistant from centre  
Equation: x² + y² = r² (in coordinate geometry)

Visual Type: circle diagram
Visual Items:  Centre O  
Radius r to any point on circumference  
All points at distance r from O

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Describe the locus of points 6 cm from a fixed point P.
Answer: A circle with centre P and radius 6 cm
Medium: A goat is tied to a post with a rope 4 m long. Describe the locus of points the goat can reach.
Answer: A circle of radius 4 m with centre at the post

Common Mistakes:Thinking it’s a square or other shape  
Forgetting it’s a circle in a plane

Combined Loci (JAMB loves these):Intersection of two loci gives possible positions (e.g., perpendicular bisector + circle → two points)

Example (Advanced – JAMB common):
Find the locus of points equidistant from two fixed points A and B, and also at a fixed distance 5 cm from point C.
Answer: Intersection of perpendicular bisector of AB and circle centre C radius 5 cm → 0, 1, or 2 points.Final High-Level Tips for JAMB Success:Memorise the four main loci:  Equidistant from two points → perpendicular bisector  
Equidistant from two lines → angle bisector  
Fixed distance from a line → two parallel lines  
Fixed distance from a point → circle
