TOPIC: Permutation and CombinationIntroductionPermutation and Combination are ways of counting and arranging objects or selecting groups from a set.
They are extremely useful in real life: arranging people in a line, seating arrangements, forming teams, passwords, lottery selections, and probability problems.For a complete beginner:  Permutation → arrangement where order matters (e.g., ABC is different from CBA)  
Combination → selection where order does not matter (e.g., choosing 3 friends is the same regardless of order)

JAMB focuses heavily on permutations (linear and circular arrangements) and arrangements with repeated objects.
This topic is very high-scoring – usually 4–7 questions every year.
Most questions are direct application of formulas, but some involve word problems (seating, committees, passwords).World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international curricula):
Candidates should be able to:Understand the fundamental principle of counting and apply it to simple and complex arrangements
Distinguish between permutations (order matters) and combinations (order does not matter)
Calculate linear permutations of distinct objects (P(n,r) = n!/(n–r)!)
Solve circular permutations (arrangements around a table) using (n–1)! for distinct objects
Handle permutations with repeated objects using n!/ (k₁! × k₂! × …) where kᵢ are repetitions
Apply these concepts to real-life problems (seating arrangements, forming words, selecting teams, arranging books, etc.)
Interpret and solve problems involving restrictions (e.g., certain people together, no two alike together)

Number of ways to arrange n distinct objects = n! (n factorial)
Number of ways to arrange r objects out of n = P(n,r) = n! / (n–r)!Circular Arrangement (around a table):
Number of ways to arrange n distinct objects = (n–1)!
(Reason: rotations are considered the same, so fix one person’s position)Key Points:Always check if objects are distinct  
For circular: (n–1)! for distinct, no fixed position  
If rotations and reflections are the same (necklace), divide by 2n (JAMB rarely asks)

Visual Type: arrangement diagrams
Visual Items:  Linear: people in a row  
Circular: people around table with one fixed

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: In how many ways can 5 different books be arranged on a shelf?
Answer: 5! = 120 ways
Medium: How many ways can 6 students sit in a row?
Answer: 6! = 720
Hard: In how many ways can 8 people be seated around a circular table?
Answer: (8–1)! = 7! = 5040
Advanced (JAMB common): 5 boys and 3 girls are to be seated in a row such that no two girls sit together. How many ways?
Answer: First arrange 5 boys: 5! ways
Gaps between boys = 6 (before, between, after)
Choose 3 gaps out of 6 for girls: C(6,3)
Arrange 3 girls in chosen gaps: 3!
Total = 5! × C(6,3) × 3! = 120 × 20 × 6 = 14400

Common Mistakes:Using n! instead of (n–1)! for circular arrangements  
Forgetting restrictions (e.g., boys and girls together/separate)

SUBTOPIC 2: Arrangements Involving Repeated ObjectsObjective 2: Solve problems involving permutations with repeated objectsExplanation:When some objects are identical, we divide by the factorial of the number of identical objects to avoid overcounting.Formula:
Number of distinct arrangements = n! / (k₁! × k₂! × … × kₘ!)
Where:  n = total number of objects  
k₁, k₂, … = number of repetitions of each identical object

Key Points:Very common in JAMB with letters, beads, books, people with same names, etc.  
If all distinct → denominator = 1! = 1

Visual Type: repeated objects illustration
Visual Items:  Example with letters: MISSISSIPPI

Example Question Pattern:Simple: How many distinct arrangements of the letters in the word "BOOK"?
Answer: 4! / (2!) = 24 / 2 = 12
Medium: Find the number of different 7-letter words that can be formed from the letters of "MATHEMATICS".
Answer: Total letters = 11, M=2, A=2, T=2, H=1, E=1, I=1, C=1, S=1
11! / (2! × 2! × 2!) = 39916800 / 8 = 4989600
Hard: How many ways can the letters of "SUCCESS" be arranged such that all S’s are together?
Answer: Treat all S’s as one unit → SUCCESS → UCCESSS (7 units)
7! / (3! for S’s inside unit) wait – better: treat 3 S’s as one super letter
Super S + U C C E → 5 units with 2 C’s
5! / 2! = 60
Advanced (JAMB circular + repeated): 6 people (2 identical twins) sit around a circular table. How many distinct arrangements?
Answer: Circular → (6–1)! = 120
But 2 identical → divide by 2! → 120 / 2 = 60

Common Mistakes:Forgetting to divide by factorials of repeated objects  
Using combination instead of permutation

Final High-Level Tips for JAMB Success:Memorise:  Linear distinct: n!  
Circular distinct: (n–1)!  
Repeated: n! / (k₁! × k₂! × …)

Always identify if order matters (permutation) or not  
For circular: remember to fix one position or use (n–1)!  
Practice word problems: seating, books, letters with repeats  
Solve 50+ questions: 20 linear, 15 circular, 15 repeated  
Draw small diagrams for circular arrangements
