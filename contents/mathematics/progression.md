TOPIC: ProgressionIntroductionProgression (also called sequences) refers to a list of numbers following a definite pattern or rule. The two main types tested in JAMB are Arithmetic Progression (A.P.) and Geometric Progression (G.P.). These are extremely important because they model real-life situations like savings plans, population growth, compound interest, depreciation, and series in physics.For a complete beginner:  Arithmetic Progression (A.P.) → each term increases by a constant difference (e.g., 2, 5, 8, 11… common difference = 3)  
Geometric Progression (G.P.) → each term is multiplied by a constant ratio (e.g., 3, 6, 12, 24… common ratio = 2)

This topic is very high-scoring in JAMB Mathematics – usually 4–7 questions every year. Questions focus on:Finding the nth term  
Calculating the sum of the first n terms  
Finding the sum to infinity for G.P. (when |r| < 1)

World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international standards):
Candidates should be able to:Identify whether a sequence is arithmetic or geometric and determine the common difference (d) or common ratio (r)
Derive and apply the formula for the nth term of an A.P. and G.P.
Use the correct formula to calculate the sum of the first n terms of an A.P. and G.P.
Determine the sum to infinity of a convergent G.P. (|r| < 1)
Solve real-life and algebraic problems involving progressions (e.g., salary increments, savings, depreciation, interest)
Understand the conditions under which a G.P. converges and diverges

nth term = a + (n–1)d
Where:  a = first term  
d = common difference  
n = position of term

Geometric Progression (G.P.)
nth term = a × r^(n–1)
Where:  a = first term  
r = common ratio

Key Points:For A.P.: subtract consecutive terms to find d  
For G.P.: divide consecutive terms to find r  
Always identify the type first!

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: The first term of an A.P. is 7 and common difference is 4. Find the 10th term.
Answer: 7 + (10–1)×4 = 7 + 36 = 43
Medium: In a G.P., first term = 5, second term = 15. Find the 6th term.
Answer: r = 15/5 = 3 → 5th term = 5 × 3^(6–1) = 5 × 243 = 1215
Hard: The 5th term of an A.P. is 23 and the 12th term is 58. Find the first term and common difference.
Answer: 5th: a + 4d = 23
12th: a + 11d = 58
Subtract: 7d = 35 → d = 5
a + 4×5 = 23 → a = 3
Advanced (JAMB word problem): A ball is dropped from a height of 80 m and bounces to 3/4 of its previous height each time. Find the height reached after the 5th bounce.
Answer: This is G.P.: first bounce height = 80 × (3/4), then multiply by 3/4 each time
Height after 5th bounce = 80 × (3/4)^5 = 80 × 243/1024 ≈ 18.95 m

Common Mistakes:Using n instead of (n–1) in A.P. formula  
Forgetting to raise r to power (n–1) in G.P.

SUBTOPIC: Sum of A.P. and G.P.Objective 2: Compute the sum of A.P. and G.P.Explanation:Sum of first n terms of A.P. (Sₙ):
Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n–1)d]
or
Sₙ = n/2 (first term + last term)Sum of first n terms of G.P. (Sₙ):
Sₙ = a (rⁿ – 1)/(r – 1)   when r ≠ 1
or
Sₙ = a (1 – rⁿ)/(1 – r)   when |r| < 1Key Points:Use the first formula for r > 1, second for |r| < 1 (avoids negative denominator)
Always check if r = 1 (then Sₙ = n × a)

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: Find the sum of the first 10 terms of A.P.: 5, 8, 11, …
Answer: a=5, d=3, n=10
S₁₀ = 10/2 [2×5 + 9×3] = 5 × (10 + 27) = 5 × 37 = 185
Medium: Find the sum of the first 8 terms of G.P.: 4, 12, 36, …
Answer: a=4, r=3, n=8
S₈ = 4 (3⁸ – 1)/(3 – 1) = 4 (6561 – 1)/2 = 4 × 3280 = 13120
Hard: The sum of the first 7 terms of an A.P. is 175 and the 7th term is 43. Find the first term.
Answer: S₇ = 7/2 (2a + 6d) = 175 → 2a + 6d = 50
7th term: a + 6d = 43
Subtract: a = 7
(Check: d = (43 – 7)/6 = 6)
Advanced (JAMB word problem): A man saves ₦200 in the first month, ₦250 in the second, ₦300 in the third, and so on. How much will he have saved after 12 months?
Answer: A.P.: a=200, d=50, n=12
S₁₂ = 12/2 [2×200 + 11×50] = 6 × (400 + 550) = 6 × 950 = ₦5700

Common Mistakes:Using wrong formula for sum (mixing A.P. and G.P.)  
Forgetting n/2 in A.P. sum

SUBTOPIC: Sum to Infinity of a G.P.Objective 3: Find the sum to infinity of a given G.P.Explanation (very important JAMB topic):Sum to infinity exists only if |r| < 1 (convergent G.P.).
S∞ = a / (1 – r)Key Points:If |r| ≥ 1 → series diverges (sum → ∞)  
Common in JAMB: repeating decimals, infinite series, geometric investments

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Find the sum to infinity of G.P.: 9 + 3 + 1 + 1/3 + …
Answer: a=9, r=1/3
S∞ = 9 / (1 – 1/3) = 9 / (2/3) = 9 × 3/2 = 13.5
Medium: The sum to infinity of a G.P. is 20 and first term is 8. Find the common ratio.
Answer: 20 = 8 / (1 – r) → 1 – r = 8/20 = 0.4 → r = 0.6
Hard: A ball is dropped from 48 m height and bounces to 3/5 of previous height each time. Find the total distance travelled by the ball before it stops.
Answer: Down = 48 m
Up + down after first bounce = 2 × 48 × (3/5)
Total = 48 + 2 × 48 × (3/5) + 2 × 48 × (3/5)² + …
= 48 + 48 × (6/5) × [1 + (3/5) + (3/5)² + …]
= 48 + (288/5) / (1 – 3/5) = 48 + (288/5) / (2/5) = 48 + 288/2 = 48 + 144 = 192 m
Advanced: The sum to infinity of a G.P. is 15 and the sum of the first two terms is 9. Find the common ratio and first term.
Answer: S∞ = a/(1–r) = 15
a + ar = 9 → a(1 + r) = 9
Solve: a = 15(1–r)
15(1–r)(1+r) = 9 → 15(1–r²) = 9 → 1–r² = 9/15 = 3/5
r² = 2/5 → r = ±√(2/5) (take positive for convergence)
r = √(2/5) ≈ 0.632

Common Mistakes:Using sum to infinity when |r| ≥ 1  
Forgetting the 2 in bouncing ball problems (up + down)

Final High-Level Tips for JAMB Success:Identify A.P. (constant difference) or G.P. (constant ratio) first  
Memorise all formulas – write them daily  
For sum to infinity: always check |r| < 1  
Practice word problems (savings, bouncing ball, salary increments)  
Draw sequence diagrams and label a, d/r, nth term
