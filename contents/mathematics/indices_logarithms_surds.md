TOPIC: Indices, Logarithms and SurdsIntroductionIndices (exponents/powers), logarithms, and surds are extremely important tools in mathematics. They help us handle very large or very small numbers, solve exponential equations, model real-world growth/decay (population, interest, radioactive decay), and simplify complicated expressions.For a complete beginner:  Indices = repeated multiplication (e.g., 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8)  
Logarithms = the opposite of indices (they ask: “To what power must I raise the base to get this number?”)  
Surds = square roots (or other roots) that cannot be simplified to whole numbers (e.g., √5, ∛7)

This topic is very high-scoring in JAMB – usually 4–7 questions every year.
Many students lose marks because they forget the laws or make careless mistakes in simplification.World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and A-Level foundation):
Candidates should be able to:Apply all laws of indices confidently in simplification and calculation (including negative and fractional indices)
Express numbers in standard form (scientific notation) and perform operations
Understand and use the laws of logarithms to simplify expressions and solve equations
Change the base of logarithms and apply change-of-base formula
Establish the relationship between indices and logarithms (logₐ(b) = c means aᶜ = b)
Simplify, rationalise, and perform basic operations (+, −, ×, ÷) on surds
Solve real-life and algebraic problems involving indices, logarithms, and surds

A number written as A × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer

SUBTOPIC: Laws of Logarithms & Relationship with IndicesObjective 2: Apply laws of logarithms and establish relationship with indicesExplanation (very simple):Relationship between indices and logarithms:
If aˣ = b, then logₐ(b) = x
Example: 2³ = 8 → log₂(8) = 3Laws of Logarithms (learn these 4 main laws):logₐ(xy) = logₐ(x) + logₐ(y)  
logₐ(x/y) = logₐ(x) − logₐ(y)  
logₐ(xⁿ) = n logₐ(x)  
logₐ(1) = 0   &   logₐ(a) = 1

Change of Base Formula (very useful):
logₐ(b) = logₓ(b) / logₓ(a)   (usually x = 10 or e)Here are perfect memory aids:This table shows all laws of logarithms with examples – copy this into your notes!This diagram clearly shows the relationship: indices go one way, logarithms go the opposite way.This picture explains the change of base formula visually.Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "Simplify log₁₀(100) + log₁₀(1000)"
Answer: log₁₀(100 × 1000) = log₁₀(100000) = 5  
Question 2: "Express log₂(32) in terms of log₂(2)"
Answer: log₂(32) = log₂(2⁵) = 5 log₂(2) = 5 × 1 = 5

Common Mistakes:  Forgetting to change multiplication to addition in logs  
Thinking log(a + b) = log a + log b (wrong – only for multiplication)

SUBTOPIC: SurdsObjective 3: Simplify, rationalise, and perform operations on surdsExplanation (step-by-step):Surds = irrational roots (cannot be written as a fraction)
Examples: √2, √5, ∛3, ⁵√7Rules for Simplification:√(a×b) = √a × √b   (only when a and b are positive)
√(a/b) = √a / √b
To simplify: take out perfect squares/cubes

Example: √72 = √(36×2) = 6√2Rationalisation:
Make the denominator rational (remove surd from bottom)
Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate (same terms, opposite sign)Example: Rationalise 1 / (√5 + √3)
Multiply by (√5 − √3):
= (√5 − √3) / ((√5)² − (√3)²) = (√5 − √3) / (5 − 3) = (√5 − √3)/2Here are very clear visual guides:This step-by-step picture shows how to simplify surds perfectly.This diagram explains rationalisation of surds with conjugate method.This table shows common surds simplification examples.Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "Simplify √50"
Answer: 5√2  
Question 2: "Rationalise the denominator of 3 / (√7 − 2)"
Answer: 3(√7 + 2) / (7 − 4) = (3√7 + 6)/3 = √7 + 2  
Question 3: "Simplify (√8 + √2)²"
Answer: 8 + 2√16 + 2 = 10 + 8 = 18

Common Mistakes:  Forgetting to multiply both top and bottom when rationalising  
Taking square root of sum instead of product (√(a + b) ≠ √a + √b)
