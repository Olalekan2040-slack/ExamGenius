TOPIC: Coordinate GeometryIntroductionCoordinate geometry (also called analytic geometry) is the study of geometry using a coordinate plane (Cartesian plane) where points are represented by ordered pairs (x, y). It bridges algebra and geometry, allowing us to describe lines, shapes, and distances using equations and calculations.For a complete beginner:
Imagine placing a graph paper (x-axis horizontal, y-axis vertical) and marking points with numbers.
Coordinate geometry lets you:Find distances between points  
Calculate slopes (gradients) of lines  
Determine if lines are parallel or perpendicular  
Write equations of straight lines in different forms

This topic is extremely important in JAMB Mathematics – usually 5–8 questions every year.
It is very high-scoring because most questions are direct calculations or simple applications.World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international standards):
Candidates should be able to:Calculate the midpoint and gradient (slope) of a line segment joining two points
Determine the distance between two points using the distance formula
Identify conditions for lines to be parallel (equal gradients) and perpendicular (product of gradients = –1)
Derive and use different forms of the equation of a straight line:Two-point form
Point-slope form
Slope-intercept form (y = mx + c)
General form (ax + by + c = 0)

Solve real-life and algebraic problems involving straight lines and their equations

M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)Gradient (slope) m of line AB:
m = (y₂ – y₁)/(x₂ – x₁)
(or rise/run = change in y / change in x)Key Points:If x₂ = x₁ → vertical line → gradient is undefined (infinite)  
If y₂ = y₁ → horizontal line → gradient = 0

Visual Type: formula cards & diagram
Visual Items:  Midpoint formula with example points  
Gradient as rise over run with triangle

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: Find the midpoint of points A(2, 3) and B(8, 7).
Answer: ((2+8)/2, (3+7)/2) = (5, 5)
Medium: Calculate the gradient of line joining P(–4, 1) and Q(6, –5).
Answer: m = (–5 – 1)/(6 – (–4)) = (–6)/10 = –0.6
Hard: The midpoint of line segment joining (–3, k) and (5, 7) is (1, 4). Find k.
Answer: ((–3+5)/2, (k+7)/2) = (1, 4) → (k+7)/2 = 4 → k + 7 = 8 → k = 1
Advanced (JAMB application): Points A(1, 2), B(4, 8), and C(–2, –4) are collinear. Verify using gradients.
Answer: Gradient AB = (8–2)/(4–1) = 6/3 = 2
Gradient AC = (–4–2)/(–2–1) = (–6)/(–3) = 2
Same gradient → collinear

Common Mistakes:Forgetting to divide by 2 in midpoint  
Wrong sign when subtracting coordinates in gradient

SUBTOPIC: Distance Between Two PointsObjective 2: Find the distance between two pointsExplanation:Distance formula (derived from Pythagoras theorem):
Distance = √[(x₂ – x₁)² + (y₂ – y₁)²]Key Points:Always positive (use absolute value or square root)  
Works in all quadrants

Visual Type: Pythagoras triangle diagram
Visual Items:  Right triangle with legs (x₂–x₁) and (y₂–y₁)  
Hypotenuse as distance

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Find distance between A(1, 2) and B(4, 6).
Answer: √[(4–1)² + (6–2)²] = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 units
Medium: Calculate distance between P(–3, 5) and Q(2, –1).
Answer: √[(2–(–3))² + (–1–5)²] = √(5² + (–6)²) = √(25 + 36) = √61 units
Hard: Points A(0, 0), B(3, 4), C(6, 0) form a triangle. Find length of AB, BC, CA.
Answer: AB = 5, BC = √(3² + (–4)²) = 5, CA = 6 → isosceles right triangle
Advanced (JAMB word problem): A ladder 5 m long leans against a wall. The foot is 3 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?
Answer: √(5² – 3²) = √(25 – 9) = √16 = 4 m (Pythagoras)

Common Mistakes:Forgetting square root at the end  
Wrong signs when subtracting coordinates

SUBTOPIC: Parallel and Perpendicular LinesObjective 3: Identify conditions for parallelism and perpendicularityExplanation:Parallel lines → same gradient
m₁ = m₂Perpendicular lines → product of gradients = –1
m₁ × m₂ = –1
(or one gradient is undefined and the other is 0 – vertical and horizontal)Key Points:Vertical line: undefined gradient (x = constant)  
Horizontal line: gradient = 0 (y = constant)

Visual Type: slope comparison diagram
Visual Items:  Parallel lines with same slope  
Perpendicular lines with negative reciprocal slopes

Example Question Pattern:Simple: If line 1 has gradient 3, what is the gradient of a line parallel to it?
Answer: 3
Medium: Find the gradient of a line perpendicular to one with gradient –2/5.
Answer: 5/2 (negative reciprocal)
Hard: Line PQ has points P(1, 2) and Q(5, 6). Find equation of line perpendicular to PQ passing through (3, 4).
Answer: Gradient PQ = (6–2)/(5–1) = 4/4 = 1
Perpendicular gradient = –1
Equation: y – 4 = –1(x – 3) → y = –x + 7

Common Mistakes:Forgetting to take negative reciprocal  
Thinking vertical and horizontal are parallel (no – they are perpendicular)

SUBTOPIC: Equations of Straight LinesObjective 4: Find the equation of a line in different formsExplanation (four main forms):Slope-intercept form
y = mx + c
(m = gradient, c = y-intercept)
Point-slope form
y – y₁ = m(x – x₁)
Two-point form
(y – y₁)/(y₂ – y₁) = (x – x₁)/(x₂ – x₁)
General form
ax + by + c = 0

Key Points:Convert between forms easily  
Use two points → two-point or point-slope → slope-intercept

Visual Type: equation forms chart
Visual Items:  All four forms with conversion arrows  
Example line with intercepts

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Find equation of line with gradient 2 and y-intercept –3.
Answer: y = 2x – 3
Medium: Line passes through (2, 5) with gradient –1. Find equation.
Answer: y – 5 = –1(x – 2) → y = –x + 7
Hard: Line through points A(–1, 3) and B(4, –2). Find equation in general form.
Answer: Gradient = (–2 – 3)/(4 – (–1)) = (–5)/5 = –1
y – 3 = –1(x + 1) → y = –x + 2 → x + y – 2 = 0
Advanced (JAMB application): Find equation of line parallel to 2x – 3y + 5 = 0 passing through (1, –1).
Answer: Gradient same → 2/3
y + 1 = (2/3)(x – 1) → multiply by 3: 3y + 3 = 2x – 2 → 2x – 3y – 5 = 0

Common Mistakes:Wrong sign when rearranging to general form  
Mixing up m and c in slope-intercept
