TOPIC: MensurationIntroductionMensuration is the branch of mathematics that deals with the measurement of geometric figures – calculating lengths, perimeters, areas, surface areas, and volumes of plane shapes and solids. It also includes calculations involving the Earth as a sphere (latitude and longitude distances).For a complete beginner:
Mensuration is simply "how to measure" shapes and 3D objects accurately.
You will learn formulas for everyday shapes (triangles, circles, cylinders, spheres, etc.) and how to apply them to composite (combined) figures.
This topic is extremely high-scoring in JAMB Mathematics – usually 6–10 questions every year (sometimes more).
Questions test:Perimeters and areas of plane figures  
Arc lengths, chords, sectors, and segments of circles  
Surface areas and volumes of solids (cuboid, cylinder, cone, pyramid, sphere)  
Composite figures (combined shapes)  
Distance between points on Earth's surface using latitude/longitude

World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and international standards):
Candidates should be able to:Calculate perimeters and areas of triangles (including base × height, (1/2)ab sin C), quadrilaterals, circles (πr², circumference 2πr), and composite plane figures
Determine lengths of arcs (θ/360 × 2πr), chords, perimeters of sectors, and areas of sectors (θ/360 × πr²) and segments
Compute total surface areas and volumes of cuboids, cylinders, cones, pyramids, prisms, spheres, and composite solids
Use the spherical Earth model to calculate great-circle distances between two points using latitude and longitude (using the formula involving cosine rule on sphere)
Solve real-life problems involving mensuration (e.g., painting surfaces, filling tanks, land area, Earth distances)

Example Question Pattern (simple to hard – JAMB style):Simple: Find the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and height 8 cm.
Answer: (1/2) × 10 × 8 = 40 cm²
Medium: A rectangle has length 15 cm and width 9 cm. Calculate its perimeter and area.
Answer: Perimeter = 2(15 + 9) = 48 cm, Area = 15 × 9 = 135 cm²
Hard: A trapezium has parallel sides 12 cm and 20 cm, height 7 cm. Find its area.
Answer: (12 + 20)/2 × 7 = 16 × 7 = 112 cm²
Advanced (composite – JAMB common): A figure consists of a rectangle 20 cm by 12 cm with a semicircle of radius 6 cm attached to one side. Find the total area.
Answer: Rectangle = 20 × 12 = 240 cm²
Semicircle = (1/2)π(6)² = 18π cm²
Total = 240 + 18π cm²

Common Mistakes:Forgetting (1/2) in triangle area  
Using diameter instead of radius in circle formulas

SUBTOPIC: Lengths of Arcs and Chords, Areas of Sectors and SegmentsObjective 2: Find arc length, chord length, perimeters and areas of sectors and segmentsExplanation (key formulas):Arc length = (θ/360°) × 2πr
Chord length = 2r sin(θ/2) (θ in degrees)
Sector area = (θ/360°) × πr²
Segment area = sector area – triangular area
  = (θ/360°) × πr² – (1/2)r² sin θPerimeter of sector = arc length + 2 radiiVisual Type: labelled circle diagram

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Find the length of an arc of a circle radius 7 cm with central angle 60°.
Answer: (60/360) × 2π × 7 = (1/6) × 14π = (7π)/3 cm
Medium: A sector has radius 10 cm and angle 72°. Find sector area and perimeter.
Answer: Area = (72/360) × π × 10² = (1/5) × 100π = 20π cm²
Perimeter = (72/360) × 2π × 10 + 2 × 10 = (1/5) × 20π + 20 = 4π + 20 cm
Hard: Find the area of the segment of a circle radius 14 cm with central angle 120°.
Answer: Sector = (120/360) × π × 14² = (1/3) × 196π ≈ 65.333π
Triangle = (1/2) × 14 × 14 × sin 120° = 98 × (√3/2) ≈ 84.87
Segment ≈ 65.333π – 84.87 cm² (leave in terms of π)
Advanced (JAMB composite): A circle has a sector removed forming a segment. Calculate total area if radius = 5 cm, angle = 90°.
(Sector = (90/360)π25 = (25π)/4, triangle = (1/2)×5×5×1 = 12.5, segment = 25π/4 – 12.5)

Common Mistakes:Using degrees instead of radians (JAMB uses degrees)  
Forgetting to subtract triangle from sector for segment

SUBTOPIC: Surface Areas and Volumes of Simple Solids and Composite FiguresObjective 3: Calculate total surface areas and volumes of solids and composite figuresExplanation (key formulas – memorize these):Cuboid — Volume = lwh, Surface area = 2(lw + lh + wh)
Cylinder — Volume = πr²h, Curved surface = 2πrh, Total = 2πr(h + r)
Cone — Volume = (1/3)πr²h, Curved = πrl (l = slant height), Total = πr(r + l)
Pyramid — Volume = (1/3) base area × height, Surface area = base + lateral faces
Sphere — Volume = (4/3)πr³, Surface area = 4πr²
Composite — Add/subtract volumes/areas of individual partsVisual Type: solid figures chart
Visual Items:  All solid formulas with diagrams  
Composite solid examples (cylinder + cone, etc.)

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Find volume of cylinder radius 7 cm, height 10 cm.
Answer: π × 7² × 10 = 490π cm³
Medium: A cone has radius 6 cm, slant height 10 cm. Find total surface area.
Answer: π × 6 × (6 + 10) = 96π cm²
Hard: A solid consists of a hemisphere of radius 7 cm on top of a cylinder of same radius and height 14 cm. Find total surface area (exclude base of hemisphere).
Answer: Cylinder curved = 2π×7×14 = 196π
Hemisphere curved = 2π×7² = 98π
Total = 196π + 98π = 294π cm²
Advanced (JAMB composite): A bucket is a frustum of cone with top radius 10 cm, bottom radius 5 cm, height 12 cm. Find volume.
Answer: V = (1/3)πh(R² + Rr + r²) = (1/3)π×12(100 + 50 + 25) = 4π × 175 = 700π cm³

Common Mistakes:Forgetting π in answers  
Using radius instead of diameter

SUBTOPIC: The Earth as a Sphere – Longitudes and LatitudesObjective 4: Determine the distance between two points on the Earth's surfaceExplanation:Earth is approximated as a sphere with radius ≈ 6371 km (use 6400 km or 6370 km in JAMB).
Great circle distance (shortest path) uses spherical trigonometry.Key Formula (JAMB uses cosine rule):
Distance = θ/360° × 2πR
Where θ = angular separation (difference in longitude or calculated using latitude)For two points (lat1, long1) and (lat2, long2):
cos c = sin(lat1) sin(lat2) + cos(lat1) cos(lat2) cos(Δlong)
Then distance = c × R (R ≈ 6370 km)Simple cases (same latitude or longitude):Same latitude → distance = Δlong × cos(latitude) × (πR/180)  
Same longitude → distance = Δlat × (πR/180)

Visual Type: Earth sphere diagram
Visual Items:  Latitude and longitude lines  
Great circle path  
Cosine rule on sphere

Example Question Pattern:Simple: Find distance between two points on equator (latitude 0°) with longitude difference 30°.
Answer: 30/360 × 2π × 6370 ≈ 3185 km
Medium: Two towns on same meridian (longitude), latitudes 30°N and 45°N. Find distance.
Answer: Δlat = 15° → distance = 15 × (π × 6370 / 180) ≈ 1662 km
Hard: Calculate distance between Lagos (6.45°N, 3.4°E) and Abuja (9.08°N, 7.5°E). Use R = 6370 km.
(Use cosine rule: cos c = sin 6.45 sin 9.08 + cos 6.45 cos 9.08 cos(4.1°) ≈ 0.997 → c ≈ 3.8° → distance ≈ 420 km)
Advanced: A ship sails along great circle from point A to B. Find shortest distance if angular separation is 45°.
Answer: 45/360 × 2π × 6370 ≈ 4977 km

Common Mistakes:Using plane distance instead of great circle  
Forgetting to convert degrees to radians (JAMB uses degree formula)
