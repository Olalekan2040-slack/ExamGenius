TOPIC: PolynomialsIntroductionPolynomials are mathematical expressions consisting of variables (like x) raised to non-negative integer powers, combined with coefficients using addition, subtraction, and multiplication. They are fundamental in algebra and model real-world phenomena like motion, economics, and engineering designs. Examples: linear (2x + 3), quadratic (x² - 4x + 4), cubic (x³ + 2x² - x + 1).For a complete beginner: Think of polynomials as "recipes" with terms like constants, x, x², etc. – they help solve equations and draw curves.This topic is crucial in JAMB Mathematics – typically 5–8 questions per exam, testing foundational algebra skills. Mastering polynomials builds problem-solving abilities for higher math.World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, IB MYP, and A-Level standards):
Candidates should be able to:Rearrange formulas to change the subject, applying inverse operations systematically.
Use the factor and remainder theorems to factorize and evaluate polynomials.
Factorize polynomials up to degree 3 using methods like grouping, difference of squares, perfect squares, and sum/difference of cubes.
Perform multiplication and division of polynomials (up to degree 3), including synthetic division.
Find roots of polynomials (up to degree 3) using factorization, quadratic formula, or rational root theorem.
Solve systems of simultaneous equations involving one linear and one quadratic equation algebraically or graphically.
Sketch and interpret graphs of polynomials up to degree 3, identifying roots, turning points (maxima/minima), and behavior at infinity, with applications to optimization problems.

SUBTOPIC: Change of Subject of FormulaObjective 1: Find the subject of the formula of a given equationExplanation: Changing the subject means rearranging the equation to isolate a different variable on one side. Use inverse operations: add/subtract, multiply/divide, square/root, etc., applying the same to both sides to maintain equality.Key Points:Start with the target variable and "undo" operations step by step.
Handle fractions by multiplying both sides by the denominator.
For squares/roots, apply the inverse while considering domains.

SUBTOPIC: Factor and Remainder TheoremsObjective 2: Apply factor and remainder theorems to factorize a given expressionExplanation: The factor theorem states that if f(a) = 0, then (x - a) is a factor of f(x). The remainder theorem says that f(a) is the remainder when f(x) is divided by (x - a). Use these to find roots and factorize.Key Points:Test possible rational roots using p/q (factors of constant / leading coefficient).
If f(a) = 0, divide polynomial by (x - a) using synthetic division.
Remainder theorem for quick evaluation without full division.

Example Question Pattern:  Simple: Use remainder theorem to find f(2) for f(x) = x² - 3x + 2. (Answer: 0, so (x-2) is a factor)

SUBTOPIC: Factorization of Polynomials of Degree Not Exceeding 3Objective 3: Factorize by regrouping, difference of two squares, perfect squares, and cubic expressionsExplanation: Factorization breaks a polynomial into simpler products. For degree ≤3: use grouping for four terms, difference of squares (a² - b² = (a-b)(a+b)), perfect squares (a² ± 2ab + b² = (a±b)²), and sum/difference of cubes (a³ ± b³ = (a±b)(a² ∓ ab + b²)).Key Points:Grouping: ab + ac + db + dc = a(b+c) + d(b+c) = (a+d)(b+c)
Cubic: Test rational roots, then factor as (x-r)(quadratic).
Always check if quadratic factors further.

SUBTOPIC: Multiplication and Division of PolynomialsObjective 4: Multiply and divide polynomials of degree not more than 3Explanation: Multiplication: Distribute each term (like FOIL for quadratics). Division: Use long division or synthetic for linear divisors, stopping when remainder degree < divisor degree.Key Points:Multiplication: (x+2)(x²-3x+4) = x(x²-3x+4) + 2(x²-3x+4)
Division: Align like terms, divide leading terms, multiply back, subtract, repeat.
Quotient + remainder/divisor = original polynomial.

SUBTOPIC: Roots of Polynomials Not Exceeding Degree 3Objective 5: Find roots of polynomials not exceeding degree 3Explanation: Roots are values where polynomial = 0. For linear: solve ax + b = 0. Quadratic: x = [-b ± √(b²-4ac)]/(2a). Cubic: Factor using rational root theorem, then quadratic.Key Points:Discriminant D = b² - 4ac (positive: 2 real roots; zero: 1 real; negative: complex).
For cubics, one real root at least; use synthetic to factor.
Sum of roots = -b/a (for x³ + ax² + bx + c = 0).

Max/min: Set derivative=0, solve.
Applications: Optimization (area, volume).
