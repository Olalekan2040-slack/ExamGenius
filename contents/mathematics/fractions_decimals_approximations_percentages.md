TOPIC: Fractions, Decimals, Approximations and PercentagesIntroductionThis topic forms the foundation of all numerical calculations in mathematics.
Fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, rates, and approximations are used in almost every aspect of daily life and higher studies – from shopping discounts, banking interest, business profit/loss, taxes (VAT), to scientific measurements and engineering.For a complete beginner:
Think of fractions/decimals as different ways of showing "parts of a whole".
Percentages mean "parts per hundred" (very useful in real life).
Approximations help when exact answers are not necessary or possible.This topic is extremely important in JAMB – usually 5–8 questions every year.
Many candidates lose marks here because they rush and make small careless mistakes.World-standard learning objectives (aligned with Cambridge IGCSE, WAEC, NECO, and international curricula like IB Middle Years):

Convert fluently between fractions, decimals, and percentages
Express answers to a specified number of significant figures or decimal places
Calculate percentage change (increase/decrease), profit/loss percentage, discount, simple interest, and compound interest (basic level)
Solve real-life problems involving ratio, proportion, rates, shares, and VAT
Understand and calculate percentage error in measurements

Types: proper (less than 1), improper (more than 1), mixed (whole number + fraction)Four basic operations on fractions:Addition/Subtraction → same denominator
Different denominator → find LCM (lowest common multiple)
Multiplication → numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator
Division → multiply by reciprocal (turn second fraction upside down)

Decimals – numbers with decimal point (0.75 = 75/100 = 3/4)Operations on decimals:
Same as whole numbers, just keep track of decimal places when multiplying/dividing.Here are clear worked examples with visuals:This diagram shows how to add/subtract fractions with different denominators (using LCM).This picture shows multiplication and division of fractions (very easy to remember).This chart shows decimal place value (tenths, hundredths, thousandths).Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "Simplify 3/4 + 5/6"

SUBTOPIC: Significant Figures, Decimal Places & ApproximationsObjective 2: Express answers to specified significant figures and decimal placesExplanation (very important for all science & maths exams):Significant figures (s.f.) → number of meaningful digits
Rules:  All non-zero digits are significant  
Zeros between non-zero digits are significant  
Leading zeros are not significant  
Trailing zeros after decimal are significant

Decimal places → number of digits after the decimal pointRounding rules:  5 or above → round up  
Below 5 → round down

Rounding incorrectly when the digit is exactly 5

SUBTOPIC: Percentages, Profit/Loss, Simple Interest & VATObjective 3: Calculate percentages, profit/loss, simple interest, and VATExplanation (real-life applications):Percentage = (part/whole) × 100%Profit/Loss percentage:
Profit % = (Profit / Cost price) × 100%
Loss % = (Loss / Cost price) × 100%Simple Interest (S.I.):
S.I. = (Principal × Rate × Time) / 100
Amount = Principal + S.I.Value Added Tax (VAT):
VAT = (Tax rate / 100) × Selling price
Selling price with VAT = Original price + VATHere are real-life pictures and charts:This picture shows how profit/loss percentage is calculated in business.This chart shows simple interest formula with example.This diagram explains how VAT is added to the price of goods.Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "A shirt costs ₦4,000 and is sold for ₦5,200. What is the profit percentage?"
Answer: 30%  
Question 2: "Find the simple interest on ₦12,000 at 5% per annum for 3 years"
Answer: ₦1,800  
Question 3: "A phone costs ₦80,000 before 7.5% VAT. What is the final price?"
Answer: ₦86,000

Common Mistakes:  Using selling price instead of cost price for profit %  
Forgetting to divide by 100 in interest formula
