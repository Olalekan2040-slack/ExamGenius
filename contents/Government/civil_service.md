**Topic 15: The Civil Service**

This topic focuses on the permanent bureaucracy that supports government operations and implements policies. It links directly to the executive arm (Topic 3), public commissions (later in Part II), and overall governance efficiency. In JAMB, questions frequently ask you to:

- Define the civil service and state its characteristics  
- List and explain its functions  
- Describe its structure and control mechanisms  
- Evaluate its significance and major problems in Nigeria

**JAMB Objective for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Analyse the significance of civil service in governance.

The subtopic is:  
Definition, characteristics, functions, structure, control and problems.

### 1. Definition of the Civil Service

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
The civil service is the permanent body of professional, non-partisan, salaried public officials (civil servants) employed by the government to assist in the formulation, implementation, and administration of public policies and laws. It is the bureaucratic machinery that ensures continuity of administration regardless of changes in political leadership (e.g., after elections or government transitions). Civil servants are expected to be neutral, anonymous, and loyal to the state (not to any political party or individual leader).

**Key Distinctions**  
- **Civil Service** vs. **Public Service**: Civil service is the core bureaucracy (ministries, departments); public service is broader (includes parastatals, judiciary, police, teachers, etc.).  
- **Civil Servants** vs. **Political Appointees**: Civil servants are career/permanent; political appointees (ministers, special advisers) are temporary and partisan.

**Historical Origins and Context**  
Modern civil service originated in 19th-century Britain (Northcote-Trevelyan Report 1854 → merit-based recruitment). In Nigeria: Inherited from British colonial administration (1930s–1960), reformed post-independence (Udoji Commission 1972–74 emphasized efficiency), and further shaped by military regimes (e.g., 1988 reforms under Babangida introduced presidential model).

### 2. Characteristics of the Civil Service

**Exhaustive List with Explanations**

1. **Permanence**  
   Civil servants remain in office regardless of government changes (unlike ministers who resign).

2. **Anonymity**  
   Civil servants act in the name of ministers/political heads; they do not take personal credit or blame publicly.

3. **Neutrality / Impartiality**  
   Must serve any government loyally without political bias (apolitical).

4. **Merit-Based Recruitment and Promotion**  
   Entry via competitive examinations (e.g., Federal Civil Service Commission tests); promotion based on performance/seniority.

5. **Hierarchy / Bureaucratic Structure**  
   Clear chain of command (permanent secretary at top, clerks at bottom).

6. **Security of Tenure**  
   Protected from arbitrary dismissal (except for misconduct, after due process).

7. **Professionalism**  
   Trained, skilled, rule-bound.

8. **Confidentiality**  
   Oath of secrecy; cannot disclose official information.

9. **Accountability**  
   To political heads (ministers) and through internal discipline.

**Nigerian Relevance**  
The 1999 Constitution (Sections 169–175) and Civil Service Rules reinforce these. However, politicization (e.g., “federal character” influencing appointments) and corruption challenge neutrality and merit.

### 3. Functions of the Civil Service

**Exhaustive List with Detailed Explanations**

1. **Policy Advice / Formulation**  
   Provide expert, objective advice to ministers/political heads on policy options.

2. **Policy Implementation / Execution**  
   Translate laws and policies into action (e.g., building roads, distributing fertilizers).

3. **Administration and Management**  
   Day-to-day running of government ministries, departments, agencies.

4. **Record Keeping and Documentation**  
   Maintain official records, files, statistics (essential for continuity).

5. **Service Delivery**  
   Provide public services (education, health, water, security support).

6. **Revenue Collection and Financial Management**  
   Support tax administration, budgeting.

7. **Advisory Role in Legislation**  
   Draft bills, provide technical input to legislature.

8. **Liaison with Other Arms**  
   Coordinate with judiciary, legislature, parastatals.

9. **Continuity and Stability**  
   Ensure government functions during political transitions.

10. **Research and Planning**  
    Long-term development planning (e.g., National Planning Commission support).

**Merits / Significance in Governance**  
- Ensures continuity (politicians come and go).  
- Provides expertise (career professionals).  
- Promotes efficiency and rule-based administration.  
- Maintains institutional memory.  
- Acts as stabilizing force in fragile democracies.

**Nigerian Relevance**  
Civil service implements national programs (e.g., NYSC, fertilizer distribution). It survived multiple military coups and democratic transitions.

### 4. Structure of the Civil Service (Nigeria)

**Federal Level**  
- Head: Head of the Civil Service of the Federation (HCSF).  
- Ministries: ~40+ (e.g., Finance, Education, Health).  
- Permanent Secretaries: Administrative heads of ministries.  
- Directorates/Departments: Divisions under permanent secretaries.  
- Grades: From Grade Level 01 (entry) to 17 (top directors).  

**State Level**  
- Head: Head of Service (HOS).  
- State ministries mirror federal structure.

**Control Mechanisms**  
1. **Political Control** — Ministers (political heads) direct policy; civil servants execute.  
2. **Administrative Control** — Permanent secretaries, HCSF/HOS.  
3. **Financial Control** — Budgets, audits (Auditor-General).  
4. **Disciplinary Control** — Public Service Commission, tribunals.  
5. **Judicial Control** — Courts review unlawful actions.

### 5. Problems of the Civil Service in Nigeria

**Exhaustive List with Analysis**

1. **Politicization**  
   Appointments influenced by “federal character,” godfathers, party loyalty → undermines merit.

2. **Corruption and Indiscipline**  
   Bribery, ghost workers, absenteeism (e.g., Integrated Payroll and Personnel Information System – IPPIS – aimed to curb this).

3. **Inefficiency / Red-Tapism**  
   Excessive bureaucracy, delays in files, slow service delivery.

4. **Poor Motivation / Low Morale**  
   Inadequate salaries, poor working conditions, delayed promotions → brain drain.

5. **Overstaffing / Understaffing Imbalance**  
   Bloated lower grades; shortage of skilled professionals.

6. **Lack of Training / Capacity Building**  
   Outdated skills in digital age.

7. **Executive Dominance**  
   Ministers interfere in routine administration.

8. **Ethnic / Regional Imbalance**  
   Federal character sometimes leads to mediocrity.

9. **Ghost Worker Syndrome**  
   Payment to non-existent staff (billions lost annually).

10. **Resistance to Reform**  
    Bureaucratic inertia blocks restructuring.

**Reforms and Solutions**  
- Udoji (1972–74): Salary increases, efficiency.  
- 1988 Reforms: Presidential model, decentralization.  
- Oronsaye Report (2012): Merge agencies, reduce waste.  
- IPPIS/BVN: Curb ghost workers.  
- Ongoing: Digitalization, performance contracts.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The civil service is best defined as the  
   A. temporary political appointees of the government  
   B. permanent, professional body that implements policies  
   C. elected members of the legislature  
   D. military officers in government  
   **Answer: B**

2. One major characteristic of the civil service is  
   A. partisanship and loyalty to political parties  
   B. permanence and continuity regardless of government changes  
   C. complete control over policy decisions  
   D. temporary tenure like ministers  
   **Answer: B**

3. A major function of the civil service in governance is to  
   A. make laws  
   B. provide continuity and implement government policies  
   C. conduct elections  
   D. appoint political leaders  
   **Answer: B**

4. One significant problem facing the Nigerian civil service is  
   A. complete absence of bureaucracy  
   B. politicization, corruption, and red-tapism  
   C. over-motivation of workers  
   D. lack of any hierarchy  
   **Answer: B**

5. The significance of the civil service in governance lies mainly in its role as  
   A. a source of political patronage  
   B. the stabilizing and implementing machinery of government  
   C. the law-making body  
   D. the judicial interpreter  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- Definition: Permanent, professional bureaucracy.  
- Characteristics: Permanence, anonymity, neutrality, merit, hierarchy.  
- Functions: Advice, implementation, administration, continuity.  
- Problems: Politicization, corruption, inefficiency, low morale.

Mnemonic for characteristics: **P – A – N – M – H – S – C – A**  
Permanence – Anonymity – Neutrality – Merit – Hierarchy – Security – Confidentiality – Accountability.

For functions: **P – I – A – R – S – C**  
Policy advice – Implementation – Administration – Record-keeping – Service delivery – Continuity.

Nigeria: Civil service is vital for continuity but weakened by politicization and corruption. Reforms (Oronsaye, IPPIS) aim to address problems.
