**Topic 2: Forms of Government**

This topic follows directly after Basic Concepts in Government. It helps you understand the different ways societies organize and exercise political power. JAMB frequently tests this topic by asking you to define a form, list its features, state merits and demerits, or compare two forms (e.g., “Distinguish between monarchy and democracy”).

**JAMB Objective for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Distinguish between different forms of government.

The syllabus lists the following forms:  
Monarchy, Aristocracy, Oligarchy, Autocracy, Republicanism, Democracy  
(For each: definitions, features, merits and demerits)

### 1. Monarchy
**Definition**  
A form of government in which supreme power is held by a single person (the monarch) who usually inherits the position (hereditary) and rules for life.

**Features**  
- Hereditary succession (king/queen, emperor, emir passes power to family).  
- Ruler is usually head of state for life (unless abdicates).  
- Can be absolute (monarch has unlimited power) or constitutional/limited (monarch is ceremonial; real power lies with elected parliament).  
- Often tied to tradition and religion.

**Merits**  
- Provides continuity and stability (no frequent changes of leadership).  
- Symbol of national unity (e.g., British monarch).  
- Quick decision-making in absolute monarchies.  
- Respects tradition and cultural heritage.

**Demerits**  
- Risk of incompetent or tyrannical ruler (no removal mechanism).  
- Lack of accountability (especially in absolute monarchy).  
- Can ignore public opinion.  
- May lead to inequality (hereditary privilege).

**Examples**  
- Absolute: Saudi Arabia (King has near-total power).  
- Constitutional: United Kingdom (King/Queen is ceremonial; Prime Minister governs).  
- Nigeria: Traditional monarchs (Obas, Emirs) have cultural influence but no political sovereignty.

### 2. Aristocracy
**Definition**  
Government by the few best or noblest persons (from Greek “rule by the best”). Power is held by a small privileged class based on birth, wealth, education, or ability.

**Features**  
- Rule by an elite group (nobles, landowners, intellectuals).  
- Positions often hereditary or based on status.  
- Not open to ordinary people.

**Merits**  
- Rule by the most qualified/educated (theoretically better decisions).  
- Stability if the elite are wise.  
- Avoids mob rule (extreme democracy).

**Demerits**  
- Excludes majority (undemocratic).  
- Can become corrupt and self-serving.  
- Leads to class inequality and resentment.  
- Difficult to remove bad rulers.

**Examples**  
- Ancient Sparta (rule by warrior elite).  
- Some pre-colonial Nigerian societies (e.g., rule by noble families in certain kingdoms).

### 3. Oligarchy
**Definition**  
Government by a small group of people (from Greek “rule by the few”). The few may be wealthy, military leaders, religious figures, or party bosses.

**Features**  
- Power concentrated in few hands (not necessarily the “best”).  
- Often based on wealth, family, military, or party control.  
- Can disguise itself as democracy (e.g., few families dominate politics).

**Merits**  
- Faster decision-making than large assemblies.  
- Can be efficient if the group is competent.  
- May prevent chaos in unstable societies.

**Demerits**  
- Self-interest and corruption common.  
- Excludes majority → inequality and unrest.  
- Can become tyrannical.  
- Lacks broad legitimacy.

**Examples**  
- Some modern states where a few rich families or military juntas control power.  
- In Nigeria: Critics sometimes describe early post-independence politics or certain state governments as oligarchic (dominated by godfathers).

### 4. Autocracy
**Definition**  
Government by one person with unlimited or absolute power (similar to absolute monarchy but not necessarily hereditary).

**Features**  
- Single ruler (dictator, despot) holds supreme power.  
- No effective checks or balances.  
- Opposition suppressed; no free elections.

**Merits**  
- Quick and decisive action (good in emergencies).  
- Unity of command.  
- Can bring rapid development if ruler is benevolent.

**Demerits**  
- High risk of tyranny and abuse of power.  
- No accountability → corruption, human rights violations.  
- Suppression of freedom and opposition.  
- Instability when ruler dies or is overthrown.

**Examples**  
- North Korea (Kim dynasty).  
- Historical: Idi Amin (Uganda), Sani Abacha (Nigeria military era).

### 5. Republicanism
**Definition**  
A form of government in which the head of state is not a monarch but an elected or nominated person (president or similar), and power comes from the people or their representatives.

**Features**  
- No hereditary ruler.  
- Head of state usually elected (directly or indirectly).  
- Emphasis on constitution and rule of law.  
- Can be democratic or authoritarian.

**Merits**  
- Accountability through elections.  
- Equality (no hereditary privilege).  
- Can combine with democracy.

**Demerits**  
- Risk of instability if elections are disputed.  
- Can become authoritarian (e.g., one-party republics).  
- May lead to personality cults.

**Examples**  
- Nigeria (since 1963 Republican Constitution).  
- United States, India, France.

### 6. Democracy
**Definition**  
Government of the people, by the people, for the people (Abraham Lincoln). Rule where supreme power lies with the citizens, exercised directly or through elected representatives.

**Features**  
- Periodic free and fair elections.  
- Rule of law, fundamental human rights.  
- Separation of powers, checks and balances.  
- Majority rule with minority rights.  
- Freedom of expression, association, press.

**Types**  
- Direct democracy (people vote directly on issues – rare, e.g., ancient Athens).  
- Indirect/Representative democracy (people elect representatives – modern form, e.g., Nigeria).

**Merits**  
- Protects individual rights and freedoms.  
- Promotes accountability and peaceful change of government.  
- Encourages participation and national unity.  
- Reflects popular will.

**Demerits**  
- Can be slow (too many opinions).  
- Risk of majority tyranny over minorities.  
- Manipulation by money/media (e.g., vote-buying).  
- Can lead to instability if poorly managed.

**Examples**  
- Nigeria (Fourth Republic – representative democracy).  
- United States, India, Ghana.

**How to Achieve the Objective**  
“Distinguish between different forms of government” means:  
- Know definitions clearly.  
- List at least 3 features for each.  
- State at least 2 merits and 2 demerits.  
- Compare any two (e.g., “Monarchy differs from democracy because…”).

**Self-Test (JAMB-Style)**  
1. Define democracy and state three features.  
2. Give two merits and two demerits of autocracy.  
3. Distinguish between constitutional monarchy and republicanism.  
4. Which form of government is Nigeria currently practising? Why?

Master this topic well — it links to later topics like systems of governance (presidential/parliamentary) and Nigerian constitutional development.
