Although the official JAMB syllabus lists "Basic Concepts in Government" starting with Power/Authority/etc., many JAMB tutorials, past questions, and recommended textbooks (e.g., Adigwe, Ofoegbu) begin with a clear definition of government as the foundation. This helps you understand why these concepts exist.JAMB expects you to know government as both an institution/process and its role in society. Questions often ask for definitions, functions, or how government relates to state/society.1. Meaning/Definition of GovernmentGovernment is the agency, institution, or machinery through which a state or society exercises authority, maintains order, makes and enforces laws, and provides for the welfare of its people.Simple ways to define it (memorize these for JAMB):As an institution/process: The body of persons or officials who rule and direct the affairs of a state or community (e.g., the president, ministers, legislature, judiciary in Nigeria).
As the art/science of governing: The process of making and implementing decisions for the common good.
Narrow sense: The group of people currently in power (e.g., "the Tinubu administration is the government").
Broad sense: The entire system of rule, including all arms and agencies.

Key Point for JAMB: Government is not the state itself—government is the tool or agent of the state. The state is permanent (people + territory + sovereignty), but government can change (e.g., through elections or coups).Examples:In Nigeria, the Federal Government (President + National Assembly + Courts) is the central machinery.
Abraham Lincoln's famous definition of democracy as "government of the people, by the people, for the people" shows government serves citizens.

2. Functions of Government (Very Important for JAMB)Government performs these core functions to maintain society and achieve state objectives. JAMB often asks "state five functions" or "which is NOT a function."Main functions (group them as maintenance, welfare, and regulatory):Maintenance of law and order — Enforces laws, prevents crime, provides security (police, army).
Protection of lives and property — Defends against internal/external threats.
Administration of justice — Settles disputes through courts.
Provision of social services — Builds schools, hospitals, roads; provides water, electricity.
Regulation of the economy — Controls money supply, taxes, trade policies.
Defence of the country — Foreign policy, military.
Promotion of welfare and development — Poverty reduction, employment, infrastructure.
Representation of the people — In democracy, through elections and representation.
Foreign relations — Diplomacy, treaties.

Nigerian Example: The 1999 Constitution (Section 14–20) lists similar functions, like ensuring security, welfare, and justice.3. Features/Characteristics of GovernmentThese distinguish government from other organizations:Authority — Has the legitimate right to make binding decisions.
Power — Can enforce decisions (coercion if needed).
Territorial coverage — Operates within defined boundaries.
Permanence — The institution lasts, even if leaders change.
Sovereignty — Supreme authority (no higher power internally).
Monopoly of legitimate force — Only government can legally use force (police/army).
Hierarchy/organization — Structured (executive, legislature, judiciary).
Accountability (in democracy) — To the people via elections.

Read step-by-step, then test yourself with the objectives at the end.Subtopic a: Power, Authority, Legitimacy, SovereigntyThese four words are closely related but different. They explain who controls society and why people obey.Power
Power is the ability or capacity of a person, group, or government to influence or control the behaviour of others, even if those others do not want to obey.
It can be used through force (coercion), persuasion, rewards, or threats.
Types/Sources:  Physical/coercive power (e.g., police or army using force).  
Economic power (money, wealth).  
Ideological power (beliefs, religion).  
Positional power (from office or role).
Key Point: Power can exist without being accepted as right (e.g., a military dictator has power through guns, even if people hate it).
Example in Nigeria: A president has power to sign laws, but if he uses only force without acceptance, it may lead to protests.

Authority
Authority is power that is accepted as rightful or legal by the people. It is the right to give commands and expect obedience without constant use of force.
People obey because they believe the ruler has the legitimate right to rule.
Types/Sources (Max Weber's classification – very important for JAMB):  Traditional authority (based on customs, e.g., kings, emirs, obas in pre-colonial Nigeria).  
Charismatic authority (based on personal charm or heroism, e.g., popular leaders like Nnamdi Azikiwe or modern influencers).  
Legal-rational authority (based on laws and constitution, e.g., elected presidents or judges in modern Nigeria).
Key Difference from Power: Authority = accepted power (legitimate); raw power can be illegitimate.

Legitimacy
Legitimacy is the belief or acceptance by the people that a government or leader has the right to rule and make laws. It makes obedience voluntary rather than forced.
A legitimate government is seen as fair and rightful.
Sources/Factors of Legitimacy:  Popular consent (elections, referendums).  
Good performance (providing security, jobs, roads).  
Tradition or ideology.  
Respect for human rights and rule of law.
Example in Nigeria: A government elected through free and fair elections gains legitimacy. If it rigs elections or becomes corrupt, legitimacy is lost → protests or coups may follow.

Sovereignty
Sovereignty is the supreme power or authority of a state to govern itself without external control. It means the state is independent and makes its own laws.
Types/Aspects:  Internal sovereignty: Supreme control over citizens and territory inside the country.  
External sovereignty: Independence from other countries (no foreign interference).
Key Features:  
Permanence (lasts as long as the state exists).  
Absoluteness (no higher authority).  
Indivisibility (cannot be shared).
Limitation in Practice: Globalization, international laws (e.g., UN, ECOWAS), and treaties limit full sovereignty, but legally the state remains sovereign.
Example in Nigeria: Nigeria is sovereign since independence in 1960 – it makes its own laws and is not ruled by Britain anymore.

Subtopic b: Society, State, Nation, Nation-StateThese explain different levels of human organization.Society
A group of people living together in a community, sharing common culture, values, and interactions. It is broader than politics – includes family, religion, economy, etc.
Society exists before the state.
State
A political organization with four essential elements:  Permanent population  
Defined territory  
Government  
Sovereignty
The state is the machinery of government – it makes and enforces laws.

Nation
A group of people who share common identity – history, language, culture, tradition, or feeling of belonging. It is more emotional/cultural than political.
A nation may or may not have its own state (e.g., Kurds are a nation without a full state).
Nation-State
When a nation has its own sovereign state (one nation = one state). It combines cultural unity with political independence.
Examples:  Japan (mostly one nation in one state).  
Nigeria: Multi-national state (many nations/ethnic groups in one state) – this causes challenges like ethnic tensions.

Key Differences  Society → people living together (social).  
State → political structure with power.  
Nation → cultural/emotional group.  
Nation-State → nation + state combined.

Subtopic c: Political ProcessesThese explain how people learn about, engage in, and think about politics.Political Socialization
The process by which individuals learn political values, beliefs, attitudes, and behaviours.
Agents: Family (first teacher), school/education, peer groups, media, religious institutions, political parties.
Example in Nigeria: Children learn patriotism through national anthem in school or family discussions on elections.
Political Participation
The active involvement of citizens in politics.
Forms/Levels:  Conventional: Voting, joining parties, campaigning.  
Unconventional: Protests, strikes, petitions (#EndSARS in Nigeria).
High participation strengthens democracy.

Political Culture
The shared attitudes, beliefs, and values that people have toward government and politics.
Types (Almond & Verba):  Parochial: Little interest in politics (common in rural/traditional areas).  
Subject: People obey but do not participate much.  
Participant: Active involvement and demand for rights (ideal for democracy).
Nigeria's Mix: Blend of participant (urban youth) and parochial/subject (some rural areas).

How to Achieve the Objectives  Identify fundamental concepts → Memorize definitions and differences (Power vs Authority, State vs Nation).  
Analyse political processes → Explain how socialization leads to participation and shapes culture (e.g., media influences Nigerian youth to protest).

## JAMB Practice Questions

### Question 1
What is the primary difference between power and authority?

A. Power is exercised through elections while authority is inherited
B. Power can exist without acceptance while authority requires legitimacy
C. Authority uses force while power uses persuasion
D. Power is temporary while authority is permanent

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Power is the ability to influence or control others even against their will, and can exist without being accepted as rightful (e.g., military dictatorship). Authority, however, is power that is accepted as legitimate by the people being governed. This distinction is fundamental in understanding governance.

---

### Question 2
According to Max Weber, which type of authority is based on customs and traditions?

A. Legal-rational authority
B. Charismatic authority
C. Traditional authority
D. Democratic authority

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Max Weber classified authority into three types: Traditional authority (based on customs and hereditary succession, e.g., kings, emirs, obas), Charismatic authority (based on personal qualities of the leader), and Legal-rational authority (based on laws and constitution). Traditional authority was common in pre-colonial Nigerian societies.

---

### Question 3
Which of the following is NOT an essential element of a state?

A. Population
B. Territory
C. Political party
D. Sovereignty

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The four essential elements of a state are: permanent population, defined territory, government, and sovereignty. Political parties are features of democratic governance but not essential elements that define a state. A state can exist without political parties, as seen in one-party or military-ruled states.

---

### Question 4
The belief by citizens that a government has the right to rule is called

A. Power
B. Authority
C. Legitimacy
D. Sovereignty

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Legitimacy refers to the acceptance by the governed that the government has the rightful authority to rule. It makes obedience voluntary rather than forced. Sources of legitimacy include popular consent through elections, good governance, respect for rule of law, and tradition.

---

### Question 5
Internal sovereignty refers to

A. Recognition by other countries
B. Membership in international organizations
C. Supreme control over citizens and territory within the country
D. Power to sign international treaties

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Internal sovereignty means the supreme authority of the state over all persons and institutions within its territorial boundaries. External sovereignty, on the other hand, refers to independence from foreign control and recognition by other states. Nigeria exercises internal sovereignty through its constitution and legal system.

---

### Question 6
The process by which individuals acquire political values, beliefs and attitudes is called

A. Political participation
B. Political culture
C. Political socialization
D. Political mobilization

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Political socialization is the lifelong process through which people develop their political opinions, beliefs, and values. Agents of political socialization include family (primary agent), schools, peer groups, mass media, religious institutions, and political parties.

---

### Question 7
Nigeria is described as a multi-national state because it

A. Has many international embassies
B. Contains several ethnic groups with distinct identities
C. Practices federalism
D. Has diplomatic relations with many countries

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A multi-national state is one that contains multiple nations or ethnic groups with distinct cultural identities within a single sovereign state. Nigeria has over 250 ethnic groups including Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, and many others, making it a multi-national state. This differs from a nation-state where one dominant nation forms one state.

---

### Question 8
Which of the following best describes charismatic authority?

A. Authority derived from legal provisions
B. Authority based on personal qualities of the leader
C. Authority inherited through family lineage
D. Authority granted by religious institutions

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Charismatic authority, according to Max Weber, derives from the exceptional personal qualities, heroism, or special characteristics of an individual leader. Followers obey because of their admiration and devotion to the leader's personality, not because of tradition or legal rules. Examples include revolutionary leaders.

---

### Question 9
The main difference between a state and a nation is that

A. A state is cultural while a nation is political
B. A state is political while a nation is cultural
C. Both are the same concept
D. A nation has sovereignty while a state does not

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A state is a political and legal entity with defined territory, population, government, and sovereignty. A nation is a cultural concept referring to a group of people who share common history, language, culture, and sense of belonging. A nation may exist without a state (e.g., Kurds), and a state may contain multiple nations (e.g., Nigeria).

---

### Question 10
Which type of political culture is characterized by active citizen involvement in political affairs?

A. Parochial political culture
B. Subject political culture
C. Participant political culture
D. Traditional political culture

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** According to Almond and Verba's classification, participant political culture exists where citizens are actively involved in politics, demand accountability, and exercise their rights. Parochial culture shows little interest in politics, while subject culture involves passive obedience without active participation. Participant culture is ideal for democracy.

---

### Question 11
Government as an institution refers to

A. The process of making laws
B. The body of persons who govern a state
C. The relationship between states
D. The study of politics

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Government can be defined in different ways. As an institution, it refers to the body of persons, officials, or machinery through which a state exercises authority and administers public affairs. This includes the executive, legislature, and judiciary. The "Tinubu administration" represents government as the group of people currently in power.

---

### Question 12
Which of the following is a primary agent of political socialization?

A. Political party
B. Mass media
C. Family
D. Pressure group

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The family is considered the primary (first) agent of political socialization because children first learn political values, attitudes, and orientations from their parents and family members at an early age. Other agents like schools, media, peers, and political parties are secondary agents that reinforce or modify these initial orientations.

---

### Question 13
The ability of a state to make decisions without external interference is known as

A. Legitimacy
B. Authority
C. Sovereignty
D. Power

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sovereignty is the supreme and independent power of a state to govern itself without external control. External sovereignty specifically means the state is free from foreign interference and can determine its own foreign and domestic policies. Nigeria gained sovereignty from Britain in 1960.

---

### Question 14
Which of the following is NOT a function of government?

A. Maintenance of law and order
B. Provision of social services
C. Organizing religious activities
D. Defense of the country

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The main functions of government include maintaining law and order, providing social services (education, health, infrastructure), defending the country, administering justice, and regulating the economy. Organizing religious activities is not a function of government, especially in secular states like Nigeria where the constitution separates state from religion.

---

### Question 15
A government that derives its power from the constitution exercises

A. Traditional authority
B. Charismatic authority
C. Legal-rational authority
D. Revolutionary authority

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Legal-rational authority is based on established laws, rules, and procedures rather than on tradition or the personal qualities of the leader. In Nigeria's democracy, the President and other officials derive their authority from the 1999 Constitution, which defines their powers, limitations, and tenure. This is the basis of modern democratic governance.
