**Topic 3: Process of Decolonization**  
(Part II – Political Development in Nigeria)

This topic is one of the most important and frequently tested in JAMB Government (Part II). It explains how Nigeria moved from British colonial rule to independence in 1960. JAMB questions usually require you to:

- Define nationalism and its types  
- Trace the emergence, goals, and strategies of nationalist movements  
- Assess the roles of major nationalist leaders  
- Evaluate the emergence of nationalist parties  
- Analyse the influence of external factors (Pan-Africanism, WWII, etc.)  
- Explain the overall process of decolonization

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Evaluate the process of decolonization.  
ii. Assess the roles of nationalist leaders and parties.  
iii. Assess the impact of external forces and ideas (Pan-Africanism, Back-to-Africa Movements, Second World War etc).

The subtopics are:  
a. Nationalism – Meaning, Types  
b. Nationalist Movements – emergence, goals, strategies  
c. Nationalist Leaders – Herbert Macaulay, Nnamdi Azikiwe, Obafemi Awolowo, Ahmadu Bello, Ladipo Solanke, Aminu Kano, J. S. Tarka, Tafawa Balewa and others  
d. Emergence of nationalist parties  
e. Influence of external factors

### Subtopic a: Nationalism – Meaning, Types

**Detailed Definition**  
Nationalism is a strong feeling of attachment, loyalty, and devotion to one’s nation or country, combined with the desire for political independence, self-government, and sovereignty from foreign colonial rule. In the colonial context, it was the organised struggle by Africans to end British (or French) domination and achieve political freedom.

**Types of Nationalism in Nigeria**

1. **Cultural Nationalism** (early phase – 1920s–1930s)  
   Emphasis on preserving African culture, identity, and dignity against Western superiority.  
   Examples: Rejection of European names/dress, promotion of African history.

2. **Economic Nationalism**  
   Demand for economic control and end to exploitation (e.g., control of trade, land, resources).  
   Examples: Boycotts of foreign goods, demands for African businesses.

3. **Political Nationalism** (dominant phase – 1940s–1960)  
   Demand for self-government, constitutional reforms, and full independence.  
   Most visible and successful type; led to independence.

**Key Characteristics of Nigerian Nationalism**  
- Multi-ethnic but initially elite-led (educated class).  
- Gradualist (constitutional agitation) rather than violent revolution.  
- Influenced by global ideas (Pan-Africanism, WWII).

### Subtopic b: Nationalist Movements – Emergence, Goals, Strategies

**Emergence**  
- Began in late 19th century with cultural resistance (e.g., Lagos elite protests).  
- Gained momentum in 1920s–1930s with return of educated Nigerians from abroad.  
- Peak in 1940s–1950s after WWII (self-determination ideas).

**Goals**  
1. End colonial rule and achieve independence.  
2. Secure self-government and constitutional reforms.  
3. Unite Nigerians across ethnic/regional lines.  
4. Promote African dignity and end racial discrimination.  
5. Gain economic and political control.

**Strategies**  
1. **Constitutional Agitation** – Petitions, delegations to London, newspapers.  
2. **Press Campaigns** – Newspapers (Lagos Weekly Record, West African Pilot).  
3. **Strikes & Boycotts** – Labour strikes (e.g., 1945 general strike).  
4. **Formation of Political Parties & Unions** – NCNC, AG, NPC.  
5. **Pan-African Conferences** – Manchester 1945 (Azikiwe, Nkrumah).  
6. **Youth & Student Activism** – Nigerian Youth Movement, WASU.

### Subtopic c: Nationalist Leaders

**Detailed Roles of Key Leaders**

1. **Herbert Macaulay (1864–1946)** – “Father of Nigerian Nationalism”  
   - Founded Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP) 1923 – first political party.  
   - Led Lagos protests, used press (Lagos Daily News).  
   - United early nationalists; opposed colonial taxation.

2. **Nnamdi Azikiwe (1904–1996)** – “Zik of Africa”  
   - Founded West African Pilot newspaper (radical voice).  
   - Led NCNC (1944), pushed for independence.  
   - Pan-Africanist; influenced youth and Eastern nationalism.

3. **Obafemi Awolowo (1909–1987)**  
   - Founded Action Group (AG) 1951.  
   - Leader of Western Region; introduced free education, health.  
   - Federalist; advocated regional autonomy.

4. **Ahmadu Bello (1910–1966)** – Sardauna of Sokoto  
   - Founded Northern Peoples Congress (NPC).  
   - Leader of Northern Region; preserved Northern identity.  
   - Conservative; focused on Northern development.

5. **Ladipo Solanke**  
   - Founded West African Students’ Union (WASU) in UK.  
   - Mobilised students abroad for nationalism.

6. **Aminu Kano (1920–1983)**  
   - Founded Northern Elements Progressive Union (NEPU).  
   - Radical voice in North; fought against feudalism.

7. **Joseph Tarka**  
   - United Middle Belt Congress (UMBC); fought for Middle Belt autonomy.

8. **Abubakar Tafawa Balewa**  
   - NPC leader; first Prime Minister (1960).  
   - Moderate; bridged North-South.

### Subtopic d: Emergence of Nationalist Parties

**Key Parties and Emergence**

1. **Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP) – 1923**  
   - Herbert Macaulay; first political party; Lagos-focused.

2. **Nigerian Youth Movement (NYM) – 1934**  
   - Youth-led; multi-ethnic initially; split along ethnic lines.

3. **National Council of Nigeria and the Cameroons (NCNC) – 1944**  
   - Azikiwe-led; nationalistic; strong in East and urban areas.

4. **Action Group (AG) – 1951**  
   - Awolowo-led; Yoruba base; Western Region.

5. **Northern Peoples Congress (NPC) – 1951**  
   - Bello-led; Northern conservative elite.

6. **Northern Elements Progressive Union (NEPU) – 1950**  
   - Aminu Kano; radical Northern opposition.

**Significance**  
- Shift from cultural to political nationalism.  
- Ethnic/regional bases → shaped post-independence politics.

### Subtopic e: Influence of External Factors

**Detailed Impact**

1. **Pan-Africanism**  
   - Ideas from Marcus Garvey, W.E.B. Du Bois, Manchester Conference 1945.  
   - Influenced Azikiwe, Nkrumah; promoted African unity.

2. **Back-to-Africa Movement**  
   - Garvey’s call for African return and self-rule.  
   - Inspired pride and resistance.

3. **Second World War (1939–1945)**  
   - Atlantic Charter (1941): Self-determination promise.  
   - Nigerian soldiers fought; returned with new ideas.  
   - Britain weakened → could not hold colonies.

4. **Indian Independence (1947)**  
   - Gandhi’s non-violent success inspired constitutional agitation.

5. **International Pressure**  
   - UN decolonisation resolutions.  
   - Anti-colonial stance of USA/USSR.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The main goal of political nationalism in Nigeria was  
   A. cultural revival only  
   B. self-government and independence from colonial rule  
   C. economic boycott alone  
   D. religious reform  
   **Answer: B**

2. Which nationalist leader founded the West African Pilot newspaper?  
   A. Herbert Macaulay  
   B. Nnamdi Azikiwe  
   C. Obafemi Awolowo  
   D. Ahmadu Bello  
   **Answer: B**

3. The Second World War influenced Nigerian nationalism mainly by  
   A. strengthening British colonial control  
   B. promoting self-determination ideas and weakening Britain  
   C. banning all political activities  
   D. increasing colonial taxation  
   **Answer: B**

4. The 1945 Manchester Pan-African Congress significantly influenced  
   A. only West African students  
   B. Nigerian nationalists like Azikiwe and Nkrumah  
   C. British colonial policy positively  
   D. French assimilation policy  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- **Nationalism**: Cultural → Economic → Political.  
- **Leaders**: Macaulay (early), Azikiwe (radical), Awolowo (federalist), Bello (conservative).  
- **Parties**: NNDP → NCNC → AG → NPC.  
- **External**: WWII, Pan-Africanism, Atlantic Charter.

Mnemonic for leaders: **M – A – A – B**  
Macaulay – Azikiwe – Awolowo – Bello.

Key phrase: **“Nationalism in Nigeria was constitutional, gradual, and externally influenced, leading to independence in 1960.”**
