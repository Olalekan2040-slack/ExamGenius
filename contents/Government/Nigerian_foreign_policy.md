**Topic: Nigerian Foreign Policy**  
(Part III – Foreign Policy and Nigeria’s Relations with the International Community)

This topic is one of the most important and consistently high-scoring sections in JAMB Government (Part III). It tests your understanding of Nigeria’s external relations since independence, with emphasis on the famous principle that **“Africa is the centrepiece of Nigeria’s foreign policy”**. JAMB questions frequently require you to:

- Identify and explain the major objectives of Nigeria’s foreign policy  
- Analyse the principle and practice of non-alignment (theory vs. reality)  
- Evaluate Nigeria’s relations with major powers (US, UK, Russia, China, France)  
- Discuss relations with developing countries (Technical Aid Corps, South-South cooperation)  
- Assess shifts across civilian and military regimes

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Identify the major objectives of Nigeria’s foreign policy.  
ii. Analyse Nigeria’s non-aligned posture.

The subtopics are:  
a. Relations with major powers  
b. Relations with developing countries, e.g., the Technical Aid Corps (TAC), etc.  
c. Nigeria’s Non-Alignment Policy

### Subtopic a: Relations with Major Powers

**Detailed Explanation**  
Nigeria’s relations with major powers have been pragmatic, driven by national interest (security, economic development, global influence) rather than rigid ideology. Since 1960, Nigeria has pursued **multi-alignment** — balanced engagement with all major powers to avoid over-dependence on any single bloc.

1. **United Kingdom (UK)**  
   - **Historical Context**: Colonial ruler (1914–1960). Strong post-independence ties (Commonwealth membership, education, military training).  
   - **Economic**: Major trading partner (oil exports, investments in banking, oil).  
   - **Political**: Influence declined after 1970s nationalisation (indigenisation decrees). Strained during Abacha era (1995–1999 suspension from Commonwealth).  
   - **Current (2025)**: Development aid, security cooperation (counter-terrorism), cultural/educational ties (Chevening scholarships, diaspora).  
   - **Evaluation**: From dominant colonial power to pragmatic partner; Nigeria now more assertive.

2. **United States of America (USA)**  
   - **Cold War Era**: Nigeria officially non-aligned but economically tied to West (oil exports). US seen as capitalist/imperialist.  
   - **Post-Cold War**: Strategic partnership (oil buyer, counter-terrorism – Boko Haram, ISWAP).  
   - **Economic**: Historically largest buyer of Nigerian crude (until shift to Asia); AGOA beneficiary; investments (ExxonMobil, Chevron).  
   - **Political**: Tensions during military rule (sanctions); improved under democracy (Obama, Trump, Biden eras).  
   - **Security**: Military training, equipment (despite Magnitsky Act restrictions on some officers).  
   - **Evaluation**: Critical for security and economy; Nigeria wary of perceived US hegemony (e.g., AFRICOM concerns).

3. **Russia (formerly USSR)**  
   - **Cold War**: USSR supported liberation movements (ANC, MPLA); supplied arms during civil war (MiGs).  
   - **Post-Cold War**: Arms supplier (helicopters, fighter jets for Boko Haram fight).  
   - **Economic**: Ajaokuta Steel revival talks, nuclear energy cooperation (Rosatom).  
   - **Political**: Closer ties under Buhari (anti-Western sentiment, multipolar world).  
   - **Evaluation**: Strategic counterbalance to Western influence; limited economic depth compared to China.

4. **China**  
   - **Early Relations**: Diplomatic ties 1971; supported anti-apartheid.  
   - **2000s Boom**: Massive infrastructure loans (roads, railways, airports – Belt and Road Initiative).  
   - **Economic**: Largest trading partner (imports: machinery, electronics; exports: oil).  
   - **Political**: Non-interference policy attractive; debt-trap diplomacy concerns.  
   - **Current (2025)**: Growing investments (dams, tech, railways); significant loans.  
   - **Evaluation**: Rapid rise; “win-win” rhetoric but rising debt and trade imbalance.

5. **France**  
   - **Historical**: Colonial rival (neighbours Benin, Niger, Cameroon).  
   - **Post-Independence**: Strained (Bakassi dispute – resolved 2008 ICJ ruling in Cameroon’s favour).  
   - **Current**: Security cooperation (Lake Chad Basin – Multinational Joint Task Force).  
   - **Evaluation**: Pragmatic but limited compared to Anglophone ties.

**General Evaluation**  
Nigeria pursues **pragmatic multi-alignment** — no exclusive bloc. Relations shift with regimes: radical (Murtala/Obasanjo), pragmatic (Babangida/Abacha), citizen-focused (Yar’Adua/Jonathan), assertive (Buhari), economic (Tinubu 2023+).

### Subtopic b: Relations with Developing Countries

**Detailed Explanation**  
Nigeria positions itself as a leader of the Global South, emphasizing South-South cooperation, technical assistance, and solidarity against imperialism.

**Key Areas**

1. **Technical Aid Corps (TAC)**  
   - Launched 1987 by Babangida.  
   - Nigerian professionals (doctors, teachers, engineers, lawyers) serve in African, Caribbean, Pacific countries.  
   - Objectives: Technical cooperation, capacity building, soft power, diaspora engagement.  
   - Impact: Over 5,000 volunteers deployed; improved Nigeria’s image; cost-effective diplomacy.  
   - Example: Doctors in Sierra Leone during Ebola; teachers in Caribbean.

2. **Support for Liberation Movements (1960s–1990s)**  
   - Financial, diplomatic, and military support to ANC (South Africa), SWAPO (Namibia), MPLA (Angola), ZANU-PF (Zimbabwe), PAIGC (Guinea-Bissau).  
   - Boycotts of apartheid South Africa (sports, Commonwealth).  
   - Mandela praised Nigeria as “moral conscience of the world” on apartheid.

3. **ECOWAS and Regional Integration**  
   - Founded 1975 (Lagos Treaty); Nigeria led establishment.  
   - Free movement protocol, trade liberalisation (ETLS).  
   - Peacekeeping (ECOMOG – Liberia, Sierra Leone, Guinea-Bissau).  
   - Mediation (Gambia 2017, Mali 2020–2022).

4. **Commonwealth and Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)**  
   - Active Commonwealth member (hosted CHOGM 2003).  
   - NAM co-founder role (1960s–70s); supported South-South solidarity.

5. **Relations with Caribbean and Pacific**  
   - Commonwealth solidarity; scholarships, aid.  
   - Cultural/educational exchanges.

6. **South-South Cooperation**  
   - Partnerships with China, India, Brazil (trade, technology transfer).  
   - Forum on China-Africa Cooperation (FOCAC) participation.

**Evaluation**  
Nigeria’s Africa-centric and South-South policy enhances prestige and soft power but is costly (TAC, peacekeeping). Domestic critics argue funds should address internal poverty and infrastructure.

### Subtopic c: Nigeria’s Non-Alignment Policy

**Detailed Explanation**

**Definition**  
Non-alignment is the policy of not formally aligning with or against any major power bloc (especially during the Cold War), while maintaining independence in foreign policy decisions and pursuing national interest.

**Historical Origins in Nigeria**  
- Adopted at independence (1960) by Prime Minister Abubakar Tafawa Balewa.  
- Influenced by India (Nehru), Egypt (Nasser), Yugoslavia (Tito).  
- Formalised in Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) – Bandung Conference 1955, Belgrade Summit 1961.

**Key Principles (in Theory)**  
- Independence in judgment and decision-making.  
- Peaceful co-existence with all nations.  
- Anti-colonialism and anti-imperialism.  
- Support for national liberation movements.  
- Disarmament and peaceful resolution of disputes.  
- Opposition to racial discrimination and apartheid.

**Nigeria’s Practice (Theory vs. Reality)**  
- **Cold War Era (1960–1990)**: Officially non-aligned but economically and militarily tilted toward the West (UK/US ties, oil exports).  
  - Radical actions: Supported liberation movements (ANC, SWAPO), anti-apartheid stance.  
  - Accepted Western aid, investment, and military training.  
- **Post-Cold War (1990s–present)**: Non-alignment evolved into **multi-alignment** or **pragmatic engagement** with all major powers (US, Russia, China, EU).  
  - No longer strictly ideological; focus on economic diplomacy, security, investment.

**Criticisms and Evaluation**  
- **Successes**: Avoided Cold War entrapment; built African leadership; peacekeeping credibility; moral authority on decolonisation.  
- **Limitations**: Not truly non-aligned (pro-Western economic dependence).  
- Selective application (anti-apartheid but silent on some African dictatorships).  
- Pragmatic rather than ideological (especially post-1999).  
- Domestic critics argue it lacks consistency.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The centrepiece of Nigeria’s foreign policy since independence has been  
   A. Europe and North America  
   B. Africa and African unity  
   C. Asia and China  
   D. the Middle East  
   **Answer: B**

2. The Technical Aid Corps (TAC) is a key example of Nigeria’s  
   A. military intervention policy  
   B. relations with developing countries and technical cooperation  
   C. alignment with Western powers  
   D. isolationist policy  
   **Answer: B**

3. Nigeria’s non-aligned posture during the Cold War was mainly  
   A. complete neutrality and isolation  
   B. independence from major blocs while supporting liberation movements  
   C. formal alliance with the Soviet Union  
   D. exclusive partnership with the United States  
   **Answer: B**

4. In practice, Nigeria’s non-alignment has been described as  
   A. rigid and unchanging  
   B. pragmatic multi-alignment with all major powers  
   C. total rejection of Western ties  
   D. alignment only with African states  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- **Major Objectives**: Africa centrepiece, non-alignment, peace, economic diplomacy, good neighbourliness.  
- **Non-Alignment**: Theory = independence; Practice = balanced relations with all powers.  
- **Major Powers**: UK (historical), US (security/economy), Russia (arms), China (infrastructure), France (limited).  
- **Developing Countries**: TAC, liberation support, ECOWAS leadership, South-South cooperation.

Mnemonic for objectives: **A – N – P – E – G**  
Africa centrepiece – Non-alignment – Peacekeeping – Economic diplomacy – Global South solidarity.

Nigeria’s foreign policy slogan: **“Africa is the centrepiece”** — repeated by every leader since 1960.
