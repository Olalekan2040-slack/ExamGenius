**Topic 10: Citizenship**

This topic is one of the most important and frequently examined in JAMB Government. It directly relates to syllabus objective iv: “explain the concept of citizenship and define the duties and obligations of a citizen.” JAMB questions often require you to:

- Differentiate methods of acquiring citizenship  
- List and explain citizenship rights  
- State duties/obligations of citizens and the state  
- Discuss issues like dual citizenship, renunciation, deprivation  
- Apply to the Nigerian context (1999 Constitution, Sections 25–32)

The subtopics are:  
a. Meaning, types  
b. Citizenship rights  
c. Dual citizenship, renunciation, deprivation  
d. Duties and obligations of citizens  
e. Duties and obligations of the state

### Subtopic a: Meaning and Types of Citizenship

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
Citizenship is the legal and political relationship between an individual and a state that confers full membership rights and imposes corresponding duties/obligations. A citizen enjoys protection, participation, and privileges from the state, while owing allegiance and obedience. Citizenship differs from nationality (ethnic/cultural belonging) and subjecthood (under monarchy, implying subjection).

**Historical Origins and Context**  
The concept originated in ancient Greek city-states (polis), where citizens (free adult males) participated in governance (Aristotle). Roman citizenship expanded rights across empire. Modern citizenship evolved with nation-states, revolutions (French 1789 – “citoyen”), and decolonization. In Africa, post-independence constitutions defined citizenship to build national identity amid ethnic diversity.

**Key Principles/Features**  
- Legal status granted by state law/constitution.  
- Involves rights (protection, participation) and duties (loyalty, obedience).  
- Can be lost or renounced under certain conditions.  
- In federal states like Nigeria: Dual citizenship at federal and state levels.

**Types / Modes of Acquiring Citizenship** (Nigeria’s 1999 Constitution, Sections 25–32)

1. **Citizenship by Birth**  
   - Born in Nigeria before or after October 1, 1960, and either parent or any grandparent is/was Nigerian.  
   - Or born abroad to Nigerian parents (one or both).  
   - Automatic and unconditional.

2. **Citizenship by Registration**  
   - Applies to Commonwealth citizens or foreign women married to Nigerian men (before 1999 amendments restricted this).  
   - Requires application, good character, intent to reside in Nigeria.  
   - Granted by the President.

3. **Citizenship by Naturalization**  
   - For non-Commonwealth foreigners.  
   - Requirements: 15 years continuous residence, good character, intent to reside permanently, ability to contribute, oath of allegiance.  
   - Discretionary grant by President (on recommendation of Minister of Interior).

**Other Distinctions**  
- **Natural-born citizen** vs. **Naturalized citizen** (naturalized can be deprived more easily).  
- **Citizen by descent** (born abroad to Nigerian parents).  
- **Honorary citizenship** (rare, ceremonial – not in Nigeria).

**Merits of Clear Citizenship Rules**  
- Promotes national identity and loyalty.  
- Defines who enjoys rights/protection.  
- Prevents statelessness.

**Demerits/Challenges**  
- Discriminatory (e.g., gender bias in old laws – women couldn’t pass citizenship to foreign husbands).  
- Statelessness risks for border communities.  
- Disputes over “indigene” vs. citizenship in Nigeria.

**Nigerian Relevance**  
1999 Constitution (as amended) removed gender discrimination in registration (Section 26 now gender-neutral after court rulings). Citizenship certificate issued by Ministry of Interior.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

   **Answer: B**

2. Naturalization as a mode of acquiring Nigerian citizenship requires

### Subtopic b: Citizenship Rights

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
Citizenship rights are the entitlements and freedoms guaranteed to citizens by the state, usually enshrined in a bill of rights. They protect individuals from state abuse and enable participation in governance.

**Historical Origins and Context**  
From Magna Carta (1215), English Bill of Rights (1689), US Bill of Rights (1791), Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948). In Nigeria: Chapter IV of 1999 Constitution (Sections 33–46).

**Key Categories and Specific Rights (Nigeria 1999 Constitution)**  
1. **Civil/ Personal Rights**  
   - Right to life (Section 33)  
   - Dignity of human person (no torture, inhuman treatment – Section 34)  
   - Personal liberty (no arbitrary arrest – Section 35)  
   - Fair hearing (Section 36)  
   - Private/family life (Section 37)  
   - Freedom of thought/conscience/religion (Section 38)  
   - Freedom of expression (Section 39)  
   - Peaceful assembly/association (Section 40)  
   - Freedom of movement (Section 41)  
   - Freedom from discrimination (Section 42)  
   - Right to acquire/own property (Section 43)  
   - Compulsory acquisition only with compensation (Section 44)

2. **Political Rights**  
   - Right to vote and be voted for (at 18 – Section 77, Electoral Act)  
   - Freedom of association (join parties)

3. **Economic/Social Rights** (Directive Principles – Chapter II, not justiciable)  
   - Right to education, health, work, social security (aspirational)

**Limitations**  
- Rights can be limited during emergencies (derogation clauses).  
- Public order, morality, defence can restrict some freedoms.

**Merits**  
- Protects vulnerable groups.  
- Enables active citizenship.  
- Promotes justice and equality.

**Demerits/Challenges**  
- Many rights violated in practice (police brutality, delayed justice).  
- Directive principles not enforceable.  
- Socio-economic rights weak.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. Which of the following is a fundamental right in Nigeria’s 1999 Constitution?  
   A. Right to free education at all levels  
   B. Right to fair hearing  
   C. Right to employment guarantee  
   D. Right to free housing  
   **Answer: B**

2. The right to freedom of movement is contained in  
   A. Section 33  
   B. Section 41  
   C. Section 44  
   D. Section 46  
   **Answer: B**

3. A major limitation on citizenship rights in Nigeria is that  
   A. all rights are absolute  
   B. some rights can be derogated during emergencies  
   C. no court can enforce them  
   D. they apply only to naturalized citizens  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic c: Dual Citizenship, Renunciation, Deprivation

**Exhaustive Explanation**

**Dual / Multiple Citizenship**  
- Holding citizenship of two or more countries simultaneously.  
- **Nigeria’s Position**: Allowed since 1999 (Section 28) – Nigerians can acquire another citizenship without losing Nigerian citizenship (except by renunciation).  
- **Advantages**: Travel freedom, property rights abroad, dual allegiance.  
- **Disadvantages**: Conflict of loyalty (e.g., military service), taxation issues, diplomatic protection complications.

**Renunciation**  
- Voluntary giving up of citizenship.  
- **Nigeria**: Adult citizen can renounce by declaration to President (Section 29).  
- Requires approval; cannot renounce if it causes statelessness (international law).

**Deprivation**  
- State withdrawal of citizenship.  
- **Nigeria (Section 29)**: Only for naturalized citizens (not by birth/descent).  
- Grounds: Fraud in application, disloyalty (e.g., treason), serving enemy state in war, imprisonment >12 months within 5 years of naturalization.  
- President decides; subject to judicial review.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. In Nigeria, dual citizenship is  
   A. completely prohibited  
   B. allowed for Nigerians who acquire another citizenship  
   C. only permitted for naturalized citizens  
   D. restricted to Commonwealth citizens  
   **Answer: B**

2. Deprivation of citizenship in Nigeria can only apply to  
   A. citizens by birth  
   B. citizens by naturalization  
   C. citizens by registration  
   D. all citizens equally  
   **Answer: B**

3. Renunciation of Nigerian citizenship requires  
   A. automatic loss upon acquiring another  
   B. a formal declaration approved by the President  
   C. approval by the National Assembly only  
   D. no formal process  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic d: Duties and Obligations of Citizens

**Exhaustive List and Explanation** (1999 Constitution, Section 24 + common law duties)

1. Abide by the Constitution and laws.  
2. Respect institutions and lawfully constituted authorities.  
3. Protect and defend Nigeria (national service if required).  
4. Render national service (e.g., NYSC).  
5. Pay taxes and dues promptly.  
6. Declare assets (public officers).  
7. Avoid disloyal acts (treason, sedition).  
8. Promote national unity and tolerance.  
9. Participate in elections/voting.  
10. Report crimes and assist law enforcement.

**Importance**  
- Ensures social order and state survival.  
- Reciprocity: Rights come with responsibilities.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. One constitutional duty of Nigerian citizens is to  
   A. disobey unlawful orders  
   B. defend Nigeria and render national service  
   C. avoid paying taxes  
   D. promote ethnic division  
   **Answer: B**

2. Citizens are expected to  
   A. ignore the constitution  
   B. pay taxes and dues as prescribed by law  
   C. refuse to vote in elections  
   D. promote disloyalty  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic e: Duties and Obligations of the State

**Exhaustive List** (1999 Constitution, Sections 14–20 – Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles)

1. Maintain security and welfare of the people (Section 14(2)(b)).  
2. Ensure fundamental rights are respected.  
3. Provide free and compulsory education (up to junior secondary).  
4. Promote national integration and unity.  
5. Provide adequate medical/health facilities.  
6. Ensure equal pay for equal work.  
7. Provide social security and welfare.  
8. Prevent exploitation and monopoly.  
9. Secure independence of judiciary.  
10. Conduct free/fair elections.

**Note**: Chapter II (Directive Principles) is not justiciable (cannot be enforced in court), but guides policy.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. One obligation of the Nigerian state is to  
   A. ignore fundamental rights  
   B. promote national integration and provide security  
   C. encourage monopoly  
   D. abolish education  
   **Answer: B**

2. The duty to provide free compulsory education up to junior secondary is found in  
   A. Chapter IV of the Constitution  
   B. Chapter II (Directive Principles)  
   C. Section 1 only  
   D. Military decrees  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
Use this mnemonic for acquisition: **B – R – N** (Birth, Registration, Naturalization).  
For rights: Memorize Chapter IV sections (33–46).  
For duties: Citizens = obey, defend, pay, vote; State = secure, educate, protect rights.
