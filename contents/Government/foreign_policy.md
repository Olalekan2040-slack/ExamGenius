**Topic 20: PART III – Foreign Policy and Nigeria’s Relations with the International Community**  
**1. Foreign Policy: Definition, Purpose, Determining Factors; Formulation and Implementation**

This is the opening topic of Part III of the JAMB Government syllabus. Foreign policy is one of the most frequently tested sections in recent JAMB examinations because it combines theory (definition, determinants) with Nigeria-specific application (Africa as centrepiece, non-alignment, international organisations). Questions usually require you to:

- Define foreign policy and distinguish it from domestic policy  
- State its main purposes / objectives  
- List and explain the internal and external determinants  
- Describe who formulates and who implements it in Nigeria  
- Evaluate Nigeria’s foreign policy posture (e.g., non-alignment in practice)

**JAMB Objective for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Define foreign policy, identify and explain its determinants.

### 1. Definition of Foreign Policy

**Exhaustive and Detailed Explanation**  
Foreign policy refers to the set of strategies, principles, objectives, decisions, and actions adopted by a sovereign state in its relations with other states, international organisations, and non-state actors in the international system. It is the official position and behaviour of a country towards the outside world, aimed at protecting and promoting its national interests.

**Key Elements in the Definition**  
- **Strategies and actions** → not just statements, but concrete steps (diplomacy, aid, military action, treaties).  
- **Sovereign state** → only independent countries have foreign policy.  
- **Other states and actors** → bilateral (country-to-country), multilateral (UN, AU), and non-state (MNCs, NGOs, terrorists).  
- **National interest** → core driver (security, economic prosperity, prestige, ideology).

**Distinction from Domestic Policy**  
- Domestic → internal affairs (economy, education, security within borders).  
- Foreign → external relations (trade agreements, defence pacts, peacekeeping).  
- Overlap exists (e.g., foreign policy affects domestic economy through oil prices).

**Nature of Foreign Policy**  
- Dynamic (changes with leadership, global events).  
- Continuous (even during crises).  
- Multi-dimensional (political, economic, cultural, military).  
- Goal-oriented (protect sovereignty, promote development).

**Nigeria’s Working Definition (Post-Independence)**  
Nigeria’s foreign policy has been officially described as “Africa-centred” with emphasis on non-alignment, decolonization, anti-apartheid, and peaceful co-existence.

### 2. Purpose / Objectives of Foreign Policy

**Exhaustive List (Core + Nigeria-Specific)**

**General / Universal Objectives**  
1. Protection of national sovereignty, territorial integrity, and independence.  
2. Promotion of national security (defence against external threats).  
3. Economic development and prosperity (trade, investment, aid).  
4. Promotion of international peace and security.  
5. Enhancement of national prestige and influence.  
6. Protection of citizens abroad (diaspora welfare).  
7. Advancement of ideological or moral values (democracy, human rights).

**Nigeria-Specific Objectives (1960–present – consistent across regimes)**  
1. **Africa as the centrepiece** → liberation of African states, unity, conflict resolution.  
2. **Non-alignment** → avoid Cold War blocs (in theory).  
3. **Promotion of African unity** → support OAU → AU.  
4. **Peacekeeping and conflict mediation** → ECOMOG, UN missions.  
5. **Economic diplomacy** → attract FDI, debt relief, trade.  
6. **Good neighbourliness** → peaceful borders (e.g., Bakassi resolution).  
7. **Global south solidarity** → South-South cooperation, anti-imperialism.

### 3. Determining Factors / Determinants of Foreign Policy

**Exhaustive Classification**

**Internal / Domestic Determinants**  
1. **Geography / Size** → Large landmass, population → regional power aspirations.  
2. **Natural Resources** → Oil dependence → OPEC membership, energy diplomacy.  
3. **Historical Experience** → Colonial rule → anti-imperial stance.  
4. **Political System / Ideology** → Democracy vs. military → shifts in style.  
5. **Economic Needs** → Poverty, debt → aid-seeking, investment diplomacy.  
6. **Ethnic / Religious Composition** → Diversity → careful balancing in relations.  
7. **Public Opinion / Pressure Groups** → Domestic pressure shapes policy (e.g., anti-apartheid sentiment).  
8. **Leadership / Personality** → Personal style (e.g., Murtala/Obasanjo radical, Babangida pragmatic).

**External / International Determinants**  
1. **Global Power Structure** → Cold War → non-alignment; post-Cold War → unipolar (US influence).  
2. **Bilateral Relations** → UK (colonial), US (aid), China (investment).  
3. **Regional Dynamics** → West African neighbours → ECOWAS leadership.  
4. **International Organisations** → UN, Commonwealth, AU, OPEC obligations.  
5. **Global Events / Crises** → Oil crises, apartheid, terrorism, climate change.  
6. **International Law / Norms** → Human rights, WTO rules constrain behaviour.

**Nigeria-Specific Determinants**  
- Oil economy → OPEC, energy security.  
- Ethnic diversity → careful relations with neighbours.  
- Military legacy → peacekeeping experience.  
- Leadership changes → Obasanjo (Afrocentrism), Yar’Adua (citizen diplomacy), Jonathan (transformation agenda), Buhari (economic diplomacy).

### 4. Formulation and Implementation of Foreign Policy in Nigeria

**Formulation (Who Makes Policy?)**  
- **Principal Actor**: President (as Head of State and Chief Diplomat – 1999 Constitution, Section 5).  
- **Key Advisers**:  
  - Minister of Foreign Affairs  
  - National Security Adviser  
  - Chief of Defence Staff  
  - Intelligence agencies (DSS, NIA)  
- **Supporting Institutions**:  
  - Ministry of Foreign Affairs (technical input, drafting)  
  - National Assembly (ratifies treaties – Section 12)  
  - Federal Executive Council (approves major decisions)  
- **Other Influences**: Public opinion, pressure groups, think-tanks.

**Implementation (Who Carries It Out?)**  
- **Primary Organ**: Ministry of Foreign Affairs (career diplomats).  
- **Key Actors**:  
  - Ambassadors / High Commissioners (in missions abroad)  
  - Consular officers  
  - Permanent missions (UN, AU, ECOWAS)  
- **Other Agencies**:  
  - Directorate of Technical Aid Corps (TAC)  
  - Nigerian Institute of International Affairs (NIIA – research)  
  - Diaspora Commission (NIDCOM)

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. Foreign policy is primarily concerned with  
   A. internal economic planning  
   B. a country’s relations with other states and international actors  
   C. local government administration  
   D. judicial interpretation of laws  
   **Answer: B**

2. The centrepiece of Nigeria’s foreign policy since independence has been  
   A. Europe and America  
   B. Africa and African unity  
   C. Asia and China  
   D. the Middle East  
   **Answer: B**

3. Which of the following is NOT an internal determinant of foreign policy?  
   A. Geography and natural resources  
   B. Leadership personality  
   C. Global power structure  
   D. Ethnic composition  
   **Answer: C**

4. In Nigeria, the principal formulator of foreign policy is  
   A. the Minister of Foreign Affairs  
   B. the President  
   C. the National Assembly  
   D. the Chief of Defence Staff  
   **Answer: B**

5. The main organ responsible for implementing Nigeria’s foreign policy is  
   A. the National Intelligence Agency  
   B. the Ministry of Foreign Affairs  
   C. the Federal Executive Council  
   D. the Nigerian Army  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- **Definition**: State’s strategy towards the outside world.  
- **Purpose**: Protect sovereignty, promote security/economy, enhance prestige, Africa-centred for Nigeria.  
- **Determinants**: Internal (geography, resources, leadership) + External (global system, organisations).  
- **Formulation**: President-led.  
- **Implementation**: Ministry of Foreign Affairs + diplomats.

Mnemonic for determinants: **G – H – P – E – L – R**  
Geography – History – Political system – Economy – Leadership – Regional/Global environment.

Nigeria’s foreign policy slogan: **“Africa as centrepiece” + “Non-alignment” + “Good neighbourliness”**.
