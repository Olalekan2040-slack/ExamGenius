**Topic 9: The Structure and Workings of Nigerian Federalism**

This topic is one of the most frequently tested and highest-scoring areas in JAMB Government (Part II). It examines how Nigeria organises power between the federal (central) government, states, and local governments, why federalism was adopted, how it functions in practice, and the persistent problems that have led to calls for restructuring. JAMB questions often require you to:

- Explain the rationale for adopting federalism  
- Describe the tiers of government and their relationships (power sharing, fiscal federalism)  
- Trace the history of state creation (1963–1996)  
- Identify major problems (census, revenue allocation, conflicts)  
- Evaluate corrective measures (federal character, derivation principle, etc.)

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Examine the workings of Nigerian federalism.  
ii. Identify its problems.  
iii. Evaluate the corrective measures adopted.

The subtopics are:  
a. Rationale for a Federal System

c. Creation of States – 1963, 1967, 1976, 1987, 1991, 1996  
d. Problems of Nigerian Federalism – census, revenue allocation, conflicts etc. solutions e.g. Federal character, etc.

### Subtopic a: Rationale for a Federal System

**Detailed Explanation**  
Federalism is a system of government in which power is constitutionally divided between a central (federal) government and component units (states/regions), each operating independently within its own sphere of authority. Nigeria adopted federalism because a unitary system could not accommodate its diversity.

**Major Reasons / Rationale**  
1. **Ethnic, Cultural, Religious, and Linguistic Diversity**  
   Nigeria has over 250 ethnic groups, major religions (Islam, Christianity, traditional), and languages. Federalism allows each group some autonomy to preserve identity and manage local affairs.

2. **Large Geographical Size and Population**  
   Nigeria is one of Africa’s largest countries (923,768 km²) with over 200 million people. A centralised system would be inefficient and prone to neglect of distant areas.

3. **Historical Pre-colonial Diversity**  
   Pre-colonial polities varied: centralized (Hausa-Fulani emirates, Yoruba kingdoms) vs. decentralised (Igbo village republics, Tiv segmentary). Federalism accommodates these differences.

4. **Colonial Legacy and Amalgamation (1914)**  
   Lord Lugard amalgamated Northern and Southern Protectorates without consultation → created North-South divide. Federalism was seen as a way to manage this artificial union.

5. **Post-Independence Stability**  
   After independence (1960), regional tensions (e.g., 1962–1965 Western crisis) showed unitary rule would fail. Federalism balances unity with regional autonomy.

6. **Minority Protection**  
   Smaller ethnic groups feared domination by major ones (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo). Federalism (and later state creation) protects minorities.

**Evaluation**  
Federalism was a pragmatic choice for unity in diversity, but implementation has been flawed (over-centralisation, fiscal imbalance).

### Subtopic b: Tiers of Government and Their Relationship

**The Three Tiers (1999 Constitution)**  
1. **Federal Government** (Central)  
   - Exclusive powers (Second Schedule Part I): Defence, foreign affairs, currency, aviation, police, railways, etc.  
   - Concurrent powers (Part II): Education, health, agriculture (shared with states).

2. **State Governments (36 states)**  
   - Residual powers (anything not on exclusive/concurrent lists).  
   - Concurrent powers shared with federal.  
   - Control local governments (create, fund, supervise).

3. **Local Governments (774 LGAs)**  
   - Functions (Fourth Schedule): Primary education, health centres, markets, roads, sanitation.  
   - Subordinate to states (no independent existence).

**Relationship Between Tiers**  
- **Vertical (Federal–State–Local)**: Cooperative but hierarchical (federal supremacy in conflicts – Section 4(5)).  
- **Horizontal (Among states)**: Equal status; inter-state cooperation (e.g., boundary disputes via NBC).  
- **Fiscal Federalism**: Revenue sharing from Federation Account (vertical: federal 52.68%, states 26.72%, LGAs 20.60%; horizontal: equality, population, landmass, etc.).  
- **Inter-governmental Relations**: National Council on Intergovernmental Relations (proposed but weak), joint committees, Supreme Court adjudication.

### Subtopic c: Creation of States

**Chronological History**  
- **1963**: Mid-Western Region created from Western Region (first post-independence state).  
- **1967**: 12 states created by Gowon (Decree No. 14) to weaken regions and prevent secession after civil war.  
- **1976**: 19 states (Murtala/Obasanjo) – 7 new states for minority protection.  
- **1987**: 21 states (Babangida) – Akwa Ibom and Katsina.  
- **1991**: 30 states (Babangida) – 9 new states (e.g., Abia, Delta).  
- **1996**: 36 states (Abacha) – 6 new states (e.g., Bayelsa, Ebonyi, Nasarawa).

**Rationale for State Creation**  
- Reduce regional domination (1967).  
- Promote minority rights and development.  
- Bring government closer to people.  
- Political patronage (military leaders reward loyalty).

**Impact**  
- Reduced secession risk.  
- Increased administrative costs.  
- Ethnic tensions persist in some areas.

### Subtopic d: Problems of Nigerian Federalism and Solutions

**Major Problems**

1. **Census Controversies**  
   Disputed figures (1962–1963, 1991, 2006) → affect revenue, representation.

2. **Revenue Allocation**  
   Over-dependence on oil (Federation Account). Vertical imbalance (federal takes too much); horizontal disputes (derivation vs. equality).

3. **Conflicts and Crises**  
   Ethnic/religious clashes (Jos, Southern Kaduna). Resource control (Niger Delta militancy). Boundary disputes.

4. **Over-Centralisation**  
   Federal dominance (police, land, mines) despite federalism.

5. **Weak Local Government**  
   States control LGAs (no autonomy).

6. **Federal Character Principle**  
   Aims for equity but often promotes mediocrity.

**Corrective Measures / Solutions Adopted**

1. **Federal Character Principle** (1979 & 1999 Constitutions)  
   Ensure equitable representation in public service, appointments.

2. **Derivation Principle**  
   13% of oil revenue to producing states (1999).

3. **State Creation**  
   Reduce regional tension (36 states now).

4. **Revenue Mobilisation Allocation and Fiscal Commission (RMAFC)**  
   Recommend sharing formula.

5. **National Boundary Commission**  
   Resolve disputes.

6. **Calls for Restructuring**  
   State police, fiscal federalism, true devolution (ongoing debate).

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The main reason for adopting federalism in Nigeria is  
   A. small size and homogeneity  
   B. ethnic, cultural, and regional diversity  
   C. desire for unitary control  
   D. influence of military only  
   **Answer: B**

2. The creation of 12 states in 1967 was primarily to  
   A. promote economic development  
   B. weaken regional power and prevent secession  
   C. increase colonial influence  
   D. abolish local governments  
   **Answer: B**

3. A major problem of Nigerian federalism is  
   A. complete state autonomy  
   B. over-dependence on federal allocation and revenue disputes  
   C. absence of any revenue sharing  
   D. lack of ethnic diversity  
   **Answer: B**
