**Topic 9: Processes of Legislation**

This topic explains how laws are made in a democratic system, focusing on the different types of legislative enactments and the step-by-step procedures involved. In JAMB Government, this is a very important and frequently tested area. Questions often ask you to:

- Identify or define types of legislative enactments (acts, edicts, bye-laws, etc.)
- Describe the stages in the law-making process
- Differentiate between civilian and military law-making
- Explain delegated legislation and its advantages/disadvantages

**JAMB Objective for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Analyse the processes involved in the making of laws.

The subtopic is:  
Legislative Enactments – acts, edicts, bye-laws, delegated legislation, decrees.

### 1. Meaning of Legislation / Legislative Enactment

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
Legislation is the process by which laws are made by the competent law-making body (legislature). A legislative enactment is the final product of that process – a written law that is binding on citizens and enforceable by the state. It is one of the primary functions of the legislature (Topic 3) and the most important source of law in modern states.

**Key Characteristics of Legislative Enactments**  
- They are written and published (in gazette or official bulletin).  
- They are prospective (apply to future actions, not retroactive unless stated).  
- They are general in application (apply to all or a class of persons).  
- They can be amended or repealed only by the same or superior body.

**Importance**  
- Provides certainty and predictability in society.  
- Reflects the will of the people (in democracy).  
- Allows adaptation to changing needs (e.g., new cybercrime laws).

### 2. Types of Legislative Enactments

**a. Acts**  
**Definition**: Laws passed by a civilian legislature (National Assembly or State House of Assembly) in a democratic system and assented to by the executive head (President or Governor).

**Features**  
- Made through full legislative process (introduction, readings, committee stage, passage, assent).  
- Highest form of law at federal or state level.  
- Can be amended or repealed only by another act or constitutional amendment.

**Examples in Nigeria**  
- Electoral Act 2022  
- Petroleum Industry Act 2021  
- Finance Act (annual budget-related)

**b. Edicts**  
**Definition**: Laws issued by a military governor during military rule at the state level.

**Features**  
- Issued unilaterally by the military administrator.  
- No legislative debate or public input.  
- Supremacy over existing state laws (but subordinate to federal decrees).  
- Temporary in nature; many repealed or replaced after return to democracy.

**Examples**  
- Various state edicts during 1966–1979 and 1983–1999 military regimes (e.g., edicts on taxation, chieftaincy matters).

**c. Bye-laws**  
**Definition**: Subsidiary laws made by local government councils or other delegated bodies (e.g., universities, market associations) under powers granted by a higher law.

**Features**  
- Made by local/subordinate authorities.  
- Must conform to parent act or constitution.  
- Cover local matters (markets, sanitation, parking, etc.).  
- Approved or ratified by supervising authority (e.g., state governor for local bye-laws).

**Examples in Nigeria**  
- Lagos State traffic bye-laws  
- University senate bye-laws on student conduct  
- Market stall allocation bye-laws

**d. Delegated Legislation (Subordinate / Secondary Legislation)**  
**Definition**: Laws made by bodies or persons (ministers, agencies, local councils) to whom the legislature has delegated law-making power through a parent act.

**Features**  
- Types: Statutory Instruments, Orders, Rules, Regulations, Bye-laws.  
- Made under express authority in an act (enabling clause).  
- Must not exceed or contradict the parent act (ultra vires if it does).  
- Published in official gazette.  
- Can be reviewed/annulled by the parent legislature.

**Reasons for Delegated Legislation**  
- Technical complexity (e.g., health/safety regulations).  
- Need for quick response (emergencies).  
- Saves legislative time for major policy issues.  
- Allows expert input (e.g., NAFDAC regulations).

**Merits**  
- Speed and flexibility.  
- Expertise from specialists.  
- Handles details legislature lacks time for.  
- Adaptable to changing circumstances.

**Demerits**  
- Risk of abuse (executive overreach).  
- Lack of public debate/transparency.  
- Possible inconsistency with parent law.  
- Over-delegation weakens legislature.

**e. Decrees**  
**Definition**: Laws issued by a military head of state or supreme military council during military regimes at the federal level.

**Features**  
- Highest law during military rule (supreme over all other laws except the constitution when partially suspended).  
- Issued without debate or public input.  
- Can suspend parts of the constitution.  
- Often retrospective or ouster clauses (prevent court challenge).

**Examples**  
- Decree No. 34 of 1966 (Unification Decree)  
- Decree No. 1 of 1984 (military takeover)  
- Numerous decrees under Babangida, Abacha, etc.

### 3. The Legislative Process in a Civilian Democratic System (Nigeria)

**Exhaustive Step-by-Step Process (How a Bill becomes an Act)**

1. **Introduction / First Reading**  
   - Bill introduced by a member (private member’s bill) or executive (government bill).  
   - Title read; no debate. Ordered to be printed.

2. **Second Reading**  
   - General debate on principles and merits.  
   - If approved, referred to committee.

3. **Committee Stage**  
   - Detailed clause-by-clause scrutiny (Committee of the Whole or Standing Committee).  
   - Amendments proposed.

4. **Report Stage**  
   - Committee reports back with amendments.  
   - House debates and votes on amendments.

5. **Third Reading**  
   - Final debate (limited to form, not substance).  
   - Final vote; if passed → sent to other chamber (in bicameral).

6. **Concurrence / Harmonization** (Bicameral system)  
   - If Senate and House versions differ → Joint Committee harmonizes.

7. **Assent**  
   - Sent to President (federal) or Governor (state).  
   - President can assent → becomes Act.  
   - Or withhold assent (veto) → can be overridden by 2/3 majority in both chambers.

8. **Publication**  
   - Published in Official Gazette → becomes law.

**Special Cases**  
- Money Bills: Originate only in House of Representatives.  
- Constitutional Amendment: Requires 2/3 majority in both chambers + approval by 24 state assemblies.

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
Use this mnemonic for types of enactments: **A-E-B-D-D**  
- **A**cts – Civilian legislature  
- **E**dicts – Military state level  
- **B**ye-laws – Local/delegated  
- **D**elegated legislation – Subordinate rules  
- **D**ecrees – Military federal level  

For the process: **I – S – C – R – T – C – A – P**  
Introduction → Second Reading → Committee → Report → Third Reading → Concurrence → Assent → Publication
