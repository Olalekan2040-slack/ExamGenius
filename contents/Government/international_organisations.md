### a. Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)

**Origin**  
- Established May 28, 1975 in Lagos, Nigeria.  
- Treaty signed by 15 West African states (founding members).  
- Nigeria played leading role (General Gowon hosted signing).

**Objectives** (ECOWAS Treaty 1975, Revised 1993)  
1. Promote economic integration and cooperation.  
2. Establish a common market and single currency (Eco – planned).  
3. Ensure free movement of persons, goods, services, capital.  
4. Promote peace, security, and stability.  
5. Harmonize policies in industry, agriculture, transport, energy.

**Structure / Main Organs**  
- Authority of Heads of State and Government (supreme organ).  
- Council of Ministers (policy-making).  
- ECOWAS Commission (executive body – Abuja).  
- Community Parliament (advisory).  
- Community Court of Justice (settles disputes).  
- ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development (EBID).  
- Specialised agencies (e.g., WAMZ – West African Monetary Zone).

**Functions / Activities**  
- Trade liberalisation (ECOWAS Trade Liberalisation Scheme – ETLS).  
- Free movement protocol (visa-free travel for 90 days).  
- Peacekeeping (ECOMOG in Liberia, Sierra Leone, Guinea-Bissau).  
- Conflict mediation (Gambia 2017, Mali 2020–2022).  
- Health cooperation (West African Health Organisation).  
- Energy (West African Power Pool).

**Achievements**  
- Successful peacekeeping (ended wars in Liberia, Sierra Leone).  
- Free movement protocol implementation (though incomplete).  
- Regional trade growth (ETLS).  
- Single currency roadmap (Eco – delayed).  
- COVID-19 coordination (regional vaccine strategy).

**Problems / Challenges**  
- Frequent military coups (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger 2020–2023).  
- Funding dependence on Nigeria (over 70% of budget).  
- Poor implementation of protocols.  
- Border closures and xenophobia (e.g., Nigeria-Ghana border issues).  
- Weak enforcement of Court decisions.

**Prospects / Solutions**  
- Strengthen sanctions regime.  
- Diversify funding.  
- Accelerate monetary union.  
- Enhance early-warning systems.

**Nigeria’s Role**  
- Largest contributor (funds, troops).  
- Hosts Commission and Parliament.  
- Led ECOMOG.  
- Mediates conflicts.

### b. Organization of African Unity (OAU) → African Union (AU)

**Origin**  
- OAU: Established May 25, 1963 in Addis Ababa (32 founding members).  
- AU: Launched July 9, 2002 in Durban, South Africa (OAU transformed).

**Objectives**  
**OAU (1963 Charter)**  
- Promote unity and solidarity.  
- Eradicate colonialism and apartheid.  
- Defend sovereignty and territorial integrity.  
- Promote international cooperation.

**AU (2000 Constitutive Act)**  
- Accelerate political and economic integration.  
- Promote democratic principles, human rights, rule of law.  
- Establish peace, security, and stability.  
- Promote sustainable development.  
- Coordinate policies in trade, science, technology.

**Structure / Main Organs (AU)**  
- Assembly of Heads of State and Government (supreme).  
- Executive Council (Ministers of Foreign Affairs).  
- AU Commission (secretariat – Addis Ababa).  
- Pan-African Parliament (advisory).  
- African Court on Human and Peoples’ Rights.  
- Peace and Security Council (PSC).  
- NEPAD (now AUDA-NEPAD).

**Functions / Activities**  
- Peacekeeping (AMISOM, MNJTF).  
- Conflict prevention (early warning).  
- Agenda 2063 (50-year development plan).  
- AfCFTA (African Continental Free Trade Area).  
- APRM (African Peer Review Mechanism).

**Achievements**  
- Ended colonialism/apartheid.  
- AU peacekeeping missions.  
- AfCFTA launched 2019 (world’s largest free trade area by number of countries).  
- COVID-19 vaccine procurement (Africa CDC).

**Problems**  
- Funding dependence (foreign donors ~70%).  
- Weak enforcement (non-compliance with decisions).  
- Coups and unconstitutional changes (PSC struggles).  
- Slow integration pace.

**Prospects**  
- Self-financing (0.2% levy on imports).  
- Stronger PSC.  
- Agenda 2063 implementation.

**Nigeria’s Role**  
- Founding OAU member.  
- Key in AU transformation (Obasanjo).  
- Largest peacekeeping contributor.  
- Hosts AU institutions temporarily.

### c. The Commonwealth

**Origin**  
- Evolved from British Empire (British Commonwealth 1926 → modern Commonwealth 1949).  
- Voluntary association of 56 independent states (mostly former British colonies).

**Objectives**  
- Promote democracy, human rights, rule of law.  
- Economic development and trade.  
- Peace and international understanding.  
- Youth and education programmes.

**Structure**  
- Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM) – biennial.  
- Commonwealth Secretariat (London).  
- Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group (CMAG) – enforces values.

**Functions / Activities**  
- Election observation.  
- Technical assistance and scholarships.  
- Trade and investment promotion.  
- Youth, sports, education programmes (Commonwealth Games).

**Achievements**  
- Peaceful decolonisation support.  
- Suspension mechanism (Nigeria 1995–1999).  
- Development programmes (CFTC – Commonwealth Fund for Technical Cooperation).

**Problems**  
- Perceived British dominance.  
- Limited enforcement power.  
- Declining relevance post-Brexit.

**Nigeria’s Role**  
- Joined 1960.  
- Hosted CHOGM 2003.  
- Suspended 1995–1999 (Abacha).  
- Benefits: Scholarships, election support.

### d. Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC)

**Origin**  
- Founded September 14, 1960 in Baghdad (Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela).  
- Nigeria joined 1971.

**Objectives**  
- Coordinate petroleum policies.  
- Stabilise oil markets and prices.  
- Ensure fair returns for producers.  
- Provide efficient supply to consumers.

**Structure**  
- Conference (ministers – supreme).  
- Board of Governors.  
- Secretariat (Vienna).  
- OPEC Fund for International Development.

**Functions / Activities**  
- Production quotas.  
- Price setting influence.  
- Research and data publication.  
- Development assistance (OPEC Fund).

**Achievements**  
- Oil price stabilisation (1970s oil shocks).  
- Revenue for member states.  
- Global energy dialogue.

**Problems**  
- Non-OPEC producers (US shale, Russia).  
- Renewables transition.  
- Internal disagreements on quotas.  
- Price volatility.

**Nigeria’s Role**  
- Major producer (Africa’s largest).  
- Influential in quota decisions.  
- Benefits: Revenue stability.  
- Challenges: Production cuts hurt economy.

### e. United Nations Organisation (UNO / UN)

**Origin**  
- Founded October 24, 1945 (post-WWII).  
- Nigeria joined October 7, 1960.

**Objectives** (UN Charter)  
- Maintain international peace and security.  
- Develop friendly relations among nations.  
- Promote human rights and fundamental freedoms.  
- Achieve international cooperation in solving problems.

**Structure**  
- General Assembly (all members).  
- Security Council (15 members – 5 permanent with veto).  
- Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC).  
- International Court of Justice (ICJ).  
- Secretariat.  
- Specialised agencies (WHO, UNESCO, etc.).

**Functions / Activities**  
- Peacekeeping (blue helmets).  
- Humanitarian aid (UNHCR, WFP).  
- Development (SDGs, UNDP).  
- Decolonisation and human rights.

**Achievements**  
- Prevented world war III.  
- Decolonisation (over 80 countries).  
- Human rights framework (UDHR).  
- Development goals (MDGs → SDGs).

**Problems**  
- Veto power of P5.  
- Funding shortages.  
- Bureaucracy.  
- Selective enforcement.

**Nigeria’s Role**  
- Top peacekeeping contributor (over 200,000 troops).  
- Non-permanent UNSC member multiple times.  
- Pushes for UNSC reform (permanent African seat).  
- Benefits: Prestige, training, aid.

### f. African Petroleum Producers Association (APPA)

**Origin**  
- Established January 27, 1987 in Lagos, Nigeria.  
- Initiative of Nigeria and other African oil producers.

**Objectives**  
- Promote cooperation among African oil-producing countries.  
- Harmonise petroleum policies.  
- Ensure fair returns from oil.  
- Develop technical cooperation and training.

**Structure**  
- Conference of Ministers.  
- Secretariat (Cotonou, Benin).  
- Council of Ministers.

**Functions / Activities**  
- Policy coordination.  
- Training and research (African Petroleum Producers Institute).  
- Joint ventures and information sharing.

**Achievements**  
- Strengthened African voice in global oil industry.  
- Technical cooperation among members.

**Problems**  
- Limited influence compared to OPEC.  
- Funding constraints.  
- Competition among members.

**Nigeria’s Role**  
- Founding member and host of first summit.  
- Leading voice.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. Nigeria was suspended from the Commonwealth between 1995 and 1999 primarily due to  
   A. failure to pay dues  
   B. human rights violations under General Abacha  
   C. withdrawal from ECOWAS  
   D. refusal to join OPEC  
   **Answer: B**

2. One of Nigeria’s major contributions to the African Union is its  
   A. refusal to participate in peacekeeping  
   B. leadership in peacekeeping missions and conflict mediation  
   C. complete financial dependence on the AU  
   D. opposition to Agenda 2063  
   **Answer: B**

3. The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) was founded mainly to  
   A. promote military alliances only  
   B. foster economic integration and cooperation in West Africa  
   C. replace the United Nations in Africa  
   D. eliminate all borders permanently  
   **Answer: B**
