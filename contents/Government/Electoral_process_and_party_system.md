### Subtopic a: Suffrage - Evolution, Types

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
Suffrage (franchise) is the right or privilege to vote in public elections, enabling citizens to participate in choosing representatives or deciding policies. It is a cornerstone of democracy, symbolizing political equality and empowerment. Suffrage determines who is eligible to vote, based on criteria like age, residency, or citizenship.

**Historical Origins and Context**  
Suffrage evolved from ancient Athens (limited to free adult males) and Rome (census-based). Medieval Europe restricted it to property owners. Modern expansion came with revolutions: American (1776, initially white males), French (1789, universal manhood suffrage). 19th–20th centuries saw waves: Abolition of property qualifications (Jacksonian democracy, US 1820s), women's suffrage (New Zealand 1893, UK 1918, Nigeria 1950s in South), universal adult suffrage post-WWII decolonization. In Africa, colonial restrictions (e.g., British indirect rule limited voting) led to independence struggles for full suffrage. Global milestones: UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948, Article 21) affirming equal suffrage.

**Key Principles/Features**  
- Universal principle: Equal right for all qualified adults.  
- Qualifications: Age (usually 18), citizenship, residency, sound mind, no criminal disqualification.  
- Evolution: From limited (census/property-based) to universal.

**Types of Suffrage (Franchise)**  
1. **Universal Adult Suffrage**: All adults (usually 18+) vote, regardless of gender, property, education. Most modern democracies.  
2. **Limited/Restricted Suffrage**: Based on qualifications like property ownership (census suffrage), education (literacy tests), gender (manhood suffrage), or race (e.g., apartheid South Africa). Historical, now rare.  
3. **Equal Suffrage**: Each voter has one vote (one-man-one-vote).  
4. **Weighted Suffrage**: Some voters have more votes (e.g., based on property – historical UK).  
5. **Compulsory Suffrage**: Voting mandatory (e.g., Australia – fines for non-voting).  

**Merits (Advantages)**  
- Promotes inclusivity and equality (universal type).  
- Strengthens democracy by reflecting diverse views.  
- Reduces marginalization (e.g., women's suffrage empowered gender issues).  
- Enhances legitimacy of government.  
- Encourages civic education and participation.

**Demerits (Disadvantages)/Challenges**  
- Limited types exclude groups, leading to inequality (e.g., historical disenfranchisement of women/minorities).  
- Universal can include uninformed voters, risking populism.  
- Administrative burdens (voter registration).  
- Manipulation (e.g., voter suppression via ID laws).

**Real-World Examples and Nigerian Relevance**  
- Global: Universal in most countries; US evolution from property-based (1787) to universal (1965 Voting Rights Act).  
- In Nigeria: Universal adult suffrage since 1979 (age 18, Electoral Act 2022). Evolution: Colonial limited to educated/property owners; women in North gained in 1979. Challenges: Low turnout (35% in 2023), IDPs disenfranchised, age debates (some push for 16).

**Criticisms and Application to Objectives**  
Critics argue universal suffrage dilutes quality; supporters say it's essential for equity. To distinguish (objective i): Universal (inclusive) vs. limited (exclusive). Application: Nigeria's universal franchise enables broad participation but faces literacy/low awareness issues.

### Subtopic b: Election - Types, Ingredients of Free and Fair Election

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
An election is the formal process by which citizens select representatives or decide public issues through voting. It is the practical exercise of suffrage, ensuring peaceful power transfer and accountability.

**Historical Origins and Context**  
Ancient: Athenian lotteries/direct votes. Modern: British parliamentary elections (13th century); US secret ballot (1880s). In Africa: Pre-colonial consensus (e.g., Igbo assemblies); colonial indirect elections; post-independence universal.

**Key Principles/Features**  
- Periodic (regular intervals).  
- Competitive (multiple candidates).  
- Secret ballot (privacy).  
- Inclusive (universal suffrage).

**Types of Elections**  
1. **General Election**: Nationwide to elect national/state officials (e.g., every 4 years in Nigeria).  
2. **By-Election**: Fills vacancy (death, resignation) in a constituency.  
3. **Primary Election**: Party internal to select candidates.  
4. **Run-Off Election**: If no majority winner (e.g., French presidential).  
5. **Referendum**: Direct vote on issues (e.g., UK Brexit 2016; Nigeria rare).  
6. **Plebiscite**: Vote on territorial status (e.g., Cameroon 1961).  

**Ingredients (Conditions) of Free and Fair Election**  
1. Independent electoral body (e.g., INEC).  
2. Up-to-date voter register.  
3. Secret ballot and secure polling.  
4. Equal access to media/campaigning.  
5. No intimidation/violence.  
6. Transparent counting/adjudication.  
7. Judicial redress for disputes.  
8. Civic education and high turnout.  

**Merits**  
- Legitimizes government.  
- Promotes accountability.  
- Peaceful conflict resolution.

**Demerits/Challenges**  
- Costly.  
- Manipulation (rigging, fake news).  
- Low turnout/apathy.

**Nigerian Relevance**  
General elections every 4 years (1999+); 2023 had BVAS for fairness, but challenges like violence, logistics.

**Criticisms**  
Elections can be "managed" in illiberal democracies.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. A by-election is held to  
   A. elect all national officials  
   B. fill a vacant seat in the legislature  
   C. decide on a policy issue  
   D. select party candidates  
   **Answer: B**

2. One ingredient of a free and fair election is  
   A. secret ballot to prevent intimidation  
   B. open voting in public  
   C. exclusion of opposition parties  
   D. mandatory voting for all  
   **Answer: A**

3. In Nigeria, the 2023 election introduced  
   A. manual voting only  
   B. BVAS for biometric verification  
   C. property-based suffrage  
   D. abolition of INEC  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic c: Electoral System - Types, Advantages and Disadvantages of Each

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
An electoral system is the method by which votes are translated into seats or outcomes. It affects representation, stability, and fairness.

**Historical Origins and Context**  
Evolved from plurality (UK) to proportional (Europe 19th century). In Nigeria: Inherited British FPTP, debated for reform.

**Key Types with Features, Advantages/Disadvantages**

1. **First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) / Simple Majority**  
   - Features: Winner takes seat with most votes (even <50%). Single-member constituencies.  
   - Advantages: Simple, quick results; strong constituency link; stable governments.  
   - Disadvantages: Wasted votes; under-represents minorities; disproportional (party with most votes may lose seats).  
   - Example: Nigeria, UK, US House.

2. **Absolute Majority / Two-Round System**  
   - Features: Candidate needs >50%; if not, run-off between top two.  
   - Advantages: Ensures majority support; reduces extremism.  
   - Disadvantages: Costly (two rounds); voter fatigue.  
   - Example: French presidential, some Nigerian governorships if no 25% in 2/3 LGAs.

3. **Proportional Representation (PR)**  
   - Features: Seats allocated by vote proportion (list system, multi-member districts).  
   - Advantages: Fair representation; includes minorities; proportional outcomes.  
   - Disadvantages: Weak constituency link; unstable coalitions; complex.  
   - Example: South Africa, Israel, EU Parliament.

4. **Mixed Member Proportional (MMP)**  
   - Features: Combines FPTP (constituency seats) + PR (party lists).  
   - Advantages: Balances local rep with proportionality.  
   - Disadvantages: Two classes of reps; complexity.  
   - Example: Germany, New Zealand.

5. **Preferential / Alternative Vote**  
   - Features: Voters rank candidates; lowest eliminated, votes redistributed until majority.  
   - Advantages: Ensures broad support; reduces negative campaigning.  
   - Disadvantages: Complex counting; potential for tactical voting.  
   - Example: Australia House.

**Nigerian Relevance**  
FPTP since independence; criticisms for winner-take-all (e.g., 2023 – APC won presidency with 36% votes). Calls for PR to address regionalism.

**Criticisms**  
No perfect system; choice depends on society (e.g., FPTP suits Nigeria's federalism but amplifies ethnic divides).

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. In the first-past-the-post system, the winner needs

   C. proportional allocation  
   D. ranking by voters  
   **Answer: B**

2. An advantage of proportional representation is

   C. strong single-member constituencies  
   D. always stable majorities  
   **Answer: B**

   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic d: Electoral Commission - Functions, Problems

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
An electoral commission is an independent body responsible for organizing, conducting, and supervising elections to ensure credibility.

**Historical Origins and Context**  
Emerged in 19th-century reforms (e.g., UK Electoral Commission 2000). In Africa: Post-independence to prevent rigging (e.g., Nigeria's FEDECO 1978).

**Key Principles/Features**  
- Independence from government.  
- Neutrality and transparency.  
- Composition: Appointed experts (e.g., INEC: Chairman + 12 commissioners).

**Functions (INEC in Nigeria – Electoral Act 2022)**  
1. Conduct general/by-elections.  
2. Voter registration and register maintenance.  
3. Civic education and voter mobilization.  
4. Delimit constituencies.  
5. Regulate political parties (registration, funding oversight).  
6. Monitor campaigns and enforce rules.  
7. Announce results and resolve disputes (pre-court).  
8. Use technology (e.g., BVAS, IReV).  
9. Research and recommend reforms.

**Merits**  
- Ensures impartiality.  
- Builds trust in democracy.  
- Professionalizes elections.

**Demerits/Challenges/Problems**  
1. Political interference (appointments by president).  
2. Funding shortages (delayed materials).  
3. Logistical issues (vast geography, insecurity).  
4. Corruption/rigging allegations.  
5. Low capacity (staff training).  
6. Legal disputes/delays.  
7. Voter apathy/low turnout.  
8. Technological failures (e.g., 2023 glitches).

**Nigerian Relevance**  
INEC (established 1998) – successes: 2015 peaceful transition; challenges: 2023 controversies over result transmission.

**Criticisms**  
Often seen as biased; solutions: Full independence, tech upgrades.

   A. over-funding from government  
   B. political interference in appointments  
   C. complete absence of technology  
   D. too many successful elections  
   **Answer: B**

3. INEC's role in regulating political parties includes

   C. selecting party candidates  
   D. abolishing opposition parties  
   **Answer: B**
