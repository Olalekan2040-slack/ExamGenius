**Topic 16: PART II – Political Development in Nigeria**  
**1. Pre-colonial Polities**

This marks the beginning of Part II of the JAMB Government syllabus, which shifts focus from general concepts (Part I) to the historical and specific development of politics and governance in Nigeria. Understanding pre-colonial systems is essential because they explain the roots of Nigeria’s ethnic diversity, federal character, centralised vs. decentralised traditions, and many modern governance challenges (e.g., North-South differences, chieftaincy conflicts).

**JAMB Objective for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Appreciate the effectiveness of the pre-colonial political systems.  
ii. Compare pre-colonial systems of governance.

The subtopics are:  
Pre-jihad Hausa, Emirate, Tiv, Igbo, Yoruba  
a. Their structural organization  
b. The functions of their various political institutions

### Subtopic: Pre-jihad Hausa States

**Exhaustive Explanation**

**Structural Organization**  
Before Usman dan Fodio’s jihad (1804–1808), the Hausa states (also called Hausa Bakwai + Banza Bakwai) were independent city-states with centralized monarchical systems.  
- **Centralized kingship**: Each state had a king (Sarki) at the top.  
- **Council of kingmakers / nobles**: Limited the king’s power (e.g., Kingmakers in Kano could depose a bad ruler).  
- **Administrative divisions**: Divided into districts headed by district heads (Hakimi).  
- **Hierarchy**: Sarki → Galadima / Madaki (chief ministers) → district heads → village heads → commoners.  
- **Taxation and military**: Organized tribute collection and standing army (dogarai).

**Functions of Political Institutions**  
- **Sarki (King)**: Head of state, commander-in-chief, chief judge, spiritual leader, tax collector.  
- **Kingmakers / Sarkin Yaki / Galadima**: Advised king, checked abuse, selected successor.  
- **Alkali courts**: Administered Islamic law (Sharia) in criminal/civil matters.  
- **Village heads**: Local administration, tax collection, dispute resolution.  
- **Age-grade / guild systems**: Provided labour, defence, community service.

**Effectiveness**  
Highly centralized and stable; efficient taxation and trade (trans-Saharan); Islamic law promoted order. Weaknesses: Internal rivalries, frequent wars between states.

### Subtopic: Sokoto Emirate (Post-Jihad Fulani Empire)

**Structural Organization**  
After the 1804–1808 jihad led by Usman dan Fodio, the Hausa states were conquered and reorganized into the Sokoto Caliphate – one of the largest pre-colonial African empires.  
- **Theocratic monarchy**: Sultan (Amir al-Mu’minin) as supreme religious and political head in Sokoto.  
- **Emirs**: Appointed to rule each emirate (e.g., Kano, Zaria, Katsina) under Sultan’s authority.  
- **Centralized but decentralized**: Emirs had significant autonomy in local affairs but owed allegiance and tribute to Sultan.  
- **Judicial hierarchy**: Alkali courts under Qadis; final appeal to Sultan.  
- **Military**: Standing army + local contingents.

**Functions of Political Institutions**  
- **Sultan**: Spiritual leader, final judicial authority, received tribute, declared jihad.  
- **Emirs**: Local administration, tax collection (zakat, jangali), justice, defence.  
- **Alkali / Qadis**: Applied Maliki Islamic law.  
- **Waziri / Madaki**: Chief ministers/advisers at emirate level.  
- **Village heads (Sarakunan Gari)**: Grassroots administration.

**Effectiveness**  
Unified large area under Islamic law; promoted education (Quranic schools); efficient taxation. Weaknesses: Over-centralization led to resistance; heavy taxation caused discontent.

### Subtopic: Tiv Political System

**Structural Organization**  
The Tiv (Middle Belt) operated a **segmentary lineage / stateless / acephalous** system – no centralized king or chief.  
- **Patrilineal clans**: Society organized into segments (ipaven → ipaven tar → larger tar).  
- **Elders’ councils**: Age-grade elders and lineage heads made decisions.  
- **No single ruler**: Power diffused among elders and age-sets.  
- **Consensus-based**: Disputes settled through moots (council meetings).  
- **Age-grade system**: Young men formed defence and labour units.

**Functions of Political Institutions**  
- **Elders / lineage heads**: Resolved disputes, allocated land, performed rituals.  
- **Age-grades**: Defence, communal labour, enforcement of decisions.  
- **Moot / council**: Collective decision-making, conflict resolution.  
- **Religious leaders (e.g., Tsav)**: Spiritual guidance, witchcraft accusations.

**Effectiveness**  
Highly democratic and egalitarian; effective conflict resolution through consensus. Weaknesses: Difficult to unite against external threats (e.g., Fulani incursions); vulnerable to segmentation.

### Subtopic: Igbo Political System

**Structural Organization**  
The Igbo (Southeast) practised a **republican / democratic / acephalous / village democracy** system – no kings or centralized authority.  
- **Village assembly (Ama-ala)**: All adult males participated.  
- **Council of elders (Ndi Ichie / Ozo title holders)**: Advised and led.  
- **Age-grades**: Enforced decisions, defence, community work.  
- **Title societies (Ozo, Ekpe)**: Prestige-based leadership.  
- **Decentralized**: Each village autonomous; no paramount ruler.

**Functions of Political Institutions**  
- **Village assembly**: Made laws, settled disputes, declared war/peace.  
- **Elders / Ozo holders**: Interpreted customs, mediated conflicts.  
- **Age-grades**: Implemented decisions, policing, communal labour.  
- **Women’s associations (Umuada, Otu Omu)**: Handled women’s affairs, moral policing.

**Effectiveness**  
Highly participatory and democratic; effective local governance and conflict resolution. Weaknesses: Slow decision-making; difficult to unite for large-scale defence (e.g., against British).

### Subtopic: Yoruba Political System

**Structural Organization**  
The Yoruba (Southwest) had **centralized constitutional monarchies** with checks and balances.  
- **Oba (King)**: Hereditary ruler (e.g., Ooni of Ife, Alaafin of Oyo).  
- **Council of chiefs (Oyomesi in Oyo, Ogboni in others)**: Advised, checked king’s power.  
- **Checks on king**: Oyomesi could compel suicide of bad king (empty calabash ritual).  
- **Administrative hierarchy**: Palace chiefs → provincial governors (Ajele) → village heads.  
- **Military**: Ogboni and war chiefs (e.g., Eso in Oyo).

**Functions of Political Institutions**  
- **Oba**: Spiritual leader, judge, administrator, commander.  
- **Oyomesi / Ogboni**: Advised, checked abuse, selected king.  
- **Bale / village heads**: Local governance.  
- **Age-grades / guilds**: Labour, defence.

**Effectiveness**  
Balanced power (king + council); stable empires (Oyo Empire). Weaknesses: Succession disputes; over-centralization in Oyo led to collapse.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The pre-colonial Igbo political system was characterized by  
   A. absolute monarchy  
   B. village democracy and acephalous structure  
   C. centralized emirate system  
   D. hereditary paramount chiefs  
   **Answer: B**

2. In the Oyo Yoruba system, the Oyomesi council served mainly to  
   A. collect taxes only  
   B. check the power of the Alaafin and depose bad rulers  
   C. lead military campaigns  
   D. administer Islamic law  
   **Answer: B**

3. The effectiveness of the Tiv segmentary system lay in its  
   A. strong centralized kingship  
   B. consensus-based decision-making and egalitarianism  
   C. reliance on appointed emirs  
   D. hereditary succession rules  
   **Answer: B**

4. Which pre-colonial system had the strongest checks on the ruler through a council that could force suicide?  
   A. Pre-jihad Hausa  
   B. Sokoto Emirate  
   C. Yoruba (Oyo)  
   D. Igbo  
   **Answer: C**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- Centralized: Hausa (pre-jihad), Emirate, Yoruba → efficient but risk of tyranny.  
- Decentralized/Acephalous: Tiv, Igbo → democratic but weak against external threats.  
- Nigeria’s diversity: Explains federalism, ethnic associations, and calls for restructuring.

Mnemonic for comparison: **C – D – C**  
Centralized (Hausa/Emirate/Yoruba) → Democratic/Decentralized (Tiv/Igbo).
