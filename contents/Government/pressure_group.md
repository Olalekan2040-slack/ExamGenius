**Topic 13: Pressure Groups**

This topic examines organized groups that seek to influence public policy and government decisions without directly contesting elections or aiming to capture state power. It contrasts sharply with political parties (Topic 12) and complements political participation and public opinion (Topics 1 and 14). In JAMB, questions often require you to:

- Define pressure groups and distinguish them from political parties  
- Identify types, functions, and methods (modus operandi)  
- Evaluate their role in democracy (positive/negative)  
- Give Nigerian examples and assess effectiveness

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Evaluate the functions and the modus operandi of pressure groups.  
ii. Distinguish between pressure groups and political parties.

The subtopics are:  
a. Definition, types, functions and modes of operation  
b. Differences between Pressure Groups and Political Parties

### Subtopic a: Definition, Types, Functions and Modes of Operation

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
A pressure group (also called interest group, advocacy group, or lobby group) is an organized collection of individuals or organizations that share common interests, values, or goals and actively seek to influence government policies, legislation, or administrative decisions in their favour—without contesting elections or seeking to form a government. They operate outside formal political structures but exert influence through persuasion, mobilization, or coercion. Pressure groups are vital in pluralist democracies as they articulate sectional interests that parties may overlook.

**Historical Origins and Context**  
Emerged prominently in 19th–20th century industrial democracies (e.g., trade unions in Britain, farmers’ lobbies in US). In Africa, colonial-era groups (e.g., Nigerian Youth Movement 1930s) evolved into nationalist movements, then post-independence advocacy (trade unions, ethnic associations). Modern globalization amplified transnational groups (e.g., Amnesty International, Greenpeace).

**Key Principles/Features**  
- Goal-oriented: Specific, narrow interests (unlike broad party manifestos).  
- Non-governmental: Independent of state control (though some receive funding).  
- Non-electoral: Do not field candidates (though some endorse).  
- Influence-focused: Seek to shape policy, not implement it.  
- Voluntary membership: Based on shared concern.

**Types of Pressure Groups** (Common classifications for JAMB)

1. **Associational / Economic / Sectional Groups**  
   - Represent occupational/economic interests.  
   - Examples: Trade unions (NLC, ASUU), employers’ associations (MAN), professional bodies (NBA, NMA), farmers’ groups.

2. **Promotional / Cause / Ideological Groups**  
   - Advocate broad causes or values (not self-interest).  
   - Examples: Human rights (Amnesty, CLO), environmental (Greenpeace, NESREA allies), women’s rights (WACOL), anti-corruption (TI Nigeria).

3. **Institutional Groups**  
   - Formal bodies within the state structure that lobby (e.g., military, civil service associations, traditional rulers’ councils).  
   - Examples: Nigeria Police Force Association, ASUU (quasi-institutional).

4. **Ethnic / Regional / Cultural Groups**  
   - Advocate ethnic or regional interests.  
   - Examples: Afenifere (Yoruba), Ohanaeze Ndigbo (Igbo), Arewa Consultative Forum (Northern).

5. **Peak / Apex / Umbrella Groups**  
   - Coordinate multiple groups.  
   - Examples: TUC (umbrella for unions), NACCIMA (business).

6. **Insider vs. Outsider Groups**  
   - Insider: Regular access to policymakers (e.g., NBA influencing justice policy).  
   - Outsider: Rely on public campaigns (e.g., #EndSARS protesters).

**Functions of Pressure Groups**

1. **Interest Articulation**  
   Voice grievances and demands of members (e.g., ASUU strikes for better funding).

2. **Interest Aggregation**  
   Combine similar demands into coherent policy proposals.

3. **Policy Influence / Lobbying**  
   Shape legislation/administration through direct contact (meetings, memoranda).

4. **Political Education and Socialization**  
   Raise awareness, mobilize public opinion (e.g., #BringBackOurGirls campaign).

5. **Checks and Balances on Government**  
   Expose abuses, monitor implementation (e.g., SERAP suing over budget transparency).

6. **Alternative Channels of Participation**  
   For those disillusioned with parties/elections.

7. **Recruitment of Political Leaders**  
   Many politicians emerge from groups (e.g., union leaders).

8. **Promotion of Stability**  
   Channel discontent peacefully (preventing violence).

**Modes of Operation (Modus Operandi)**

1. **Lobbying**  
   Direct contact with policymakers (meetings, position papers, gifts – ethical or corrupt).

2. **Mass Mobilization / Protests / Demonstrations**  
   Public marches, strikes, sit-ins (e.g., #EndSARS 2020).

3. **Media Campaigns**  
   Press conferences, social media, advertisements.

4. **Litigation / Judicial Activism**  
   Court cases (e.g., SERAP vs. government on fuel subsidy).

5. **Petitions and Memoranda**  
   Formal submissions to legislature/executive.

6. **Boycotts and Strikes**  
   Economic pressure (e.g., NLC nationwide strikes).

7. **Electoral Endorsement / Support**  
   Campaign for sympathetic candidates (without contesting).

8. **Alliance / Coalition Building**  
   Partner with other groups or parties.

**Merits (Positive Evaluation)**  
- Pluralism: Represent diverse interests parties ignore.  
- Participation: Alternative avenue beyond voting.  
- Accountability: Check government excesses.  
- Expertise: Provide specialist input (e.g., NMA on health policy).  
- Democratization: Mobilize marginalized voices.

**Demerits (Negative Evaluation)**  
- Sectionalism: Promote narrow interests over national good.  
- Unequal influence: Wealthy groups dominate (e.g., oil companies vs. poor farmers).  
- Disruption: Strikes/protests cause economic loss.  
- Corruption: Bribery in lobbying.  
- Violence: Some protests turn chaotic.

**Nigerian Relevance**  
- Strong examples: NLC/ASUU (strikes), #EndSARS (youth-led), ethnic associations (influence zoning), SERAP (litigation).  
- Challenges: Repression (e.g., Lekki shooting 2020), co-optation by government, ethnic/regional bias.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. A pressure group differs from a political party mainly because it  
   A. contests elections to form government  
   B. seeks to influence policy without seeking power  
   C. is always funded by the government  
   D. has no organized structure  
   **Answer: B**

2. One major mode of operation used by pressure groups is  
   A. fielding candidates in elections  
   B. organizing strikes and public demonstrations  
   C. suspending the constitution  
   D. appointing ministers  
   **Answer: B**

3. An example of a promotional pressure group in Nigeria is  
   A. Nigerian Labour Congress (NLC)  
   B. Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP)  
   C. Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU)  
   D. Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN)  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic b: Differences between Pressure Groups and Political Parties

**Exhaustive Comparison**

| Aspect                     | Political Parties                              | Pressure Groups                                |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Primary Goal               | Capture and exercise political power           | Influence policy without seeking power         |
| Contest Elections          | Yes – field candidates                         | No – do not contest elections                  |
| Scope of Interests         | Broad (national manifesto, all issues)         | Narrow/specific (one or few issues)            |
| Membership                 | Open to all who share ideology                 | Often sectional (e.g., workers, professionals) |
| Ideology                   | Usually have comprehensive manifesto           | May be single-issue or ideological             |
| Accountability             | To voters through elections                    | To members and public opinion                  |
| Structure                  | Formal hierarchy, branches nationwide          | Varies – formal or loose                       |
| Role in Government         | Form government if win                         | Influence/oppose from outside                  |
| Methods                    | Campaigns, elections, manifestos               | Lobbying, protests, litigation, media          |
| Examples (Nigeria)         | APC, PDP                                       | NLC, ASUU, #EndSARS, SERAP                     |

**Key Evaluation Points**  
- **Complementary Roles**: Parties aggregate broad interests; groups articulate specific ones. Together they enrich democracy.  
- **In Nigeria**: Parties often weak in ideology → pressure groups fill gaps (e.g., ethnic associations influence zoning).  
- **Risks**: Groups can undermine parties (e.g., godfathers using groups), or parties co-opt groups.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. Which of the following is a major difference between pressure groups and political parties?  
   A. Pressure groups contest elections  
   B. Political parties do not seek to influence policy  
   C. Pressure groups do not aim to form government  
   D. Political parties have no manifesto  
   **Answer: C**

2. Pressure groups are likely to use which method that political parties rarely use as their primary tool?  
   A. Manifestos and campaigns  
   B. Mass protests and strikes  
   C. Fielding candidates  
   D. Forming government  
   **Answer: B**

3. In a democracy, pressure groups and political parties are  
   A. completely identical in goals  
   B. complementary – parties govern, groups influence  
   C. always in conflict  
   D. both irrelevant to policy-making  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- Definition: Organized influence without power-seeking.  
- Types: Economic (NLC), Promotional (SERAP), Ethnic (Afenifere).  
- Functions: Articulate, aggregate, influence, educate, check.  
- Modes: Lobbying, protest, litigation, media.  
- Difference from parties: Influence vs. Power.

Mnemonic for functions: **A – A – I – E – C – P**  
Articulate → Aggregate → Influence → Educate → Check → Participate.

Nigeria: Pressure groups often more effective than weak parties in highlighting issues (e.g., #EndSARS forced policy review).
