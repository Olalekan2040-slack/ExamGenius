**Topic 2: Imperialist Penetration**  
(Part II – Political Development in Nigeria)

This topic is one of the most frequently tested sections in JAMB Government (Part II). It examines how European colonial powers (especially Britain and France) penetrated and controlled African territories, with special focus on Nigeria. JAMB questions usually require you to:

- Trace the step-by-step process of British acquisition in Nigeria  
- Explain the difference between direct rule and indirect rule (British policies)  
- Compare British and French colonial administrative policies (indirect rule vs. assimilation/association)  
- Evaluate the political, economic, and socio-cultural impacts of British colonial rule in Nigeria  
- Distinguish British from French colonial practices

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Trace the processes of imperialist penetration.  
ii. Assess the impact of British and French policies.  
iii. Distinguish between British and French colonial practices.

The subtopics are:  
a. The British process of acquisition – trade, missionary activities, company rule, crown colony, protectorate  
b. The British colonial administrative policy – direct and indirect rule  
c. The French colonial administrative policy – assimilation and association  
d. Impact of British colonial rule – economic, political, socio-cultural  
e. Comparison of British and French colonial administration

### Subtopic a: The British Process of Acquisition

**Detailed Step-by-Step Process (Chronological Order)**

1. **Trade and Commercial Penetration (1850s–1880s)**  
   - British traders (e.g., Royal Niger Company under George Taubman Goldie) dominated palm oil, palm kernels, and later groundnut trade along the Niger River and coast.  
   - Trading posts established in Lagos, Bonny, Calabar, Brass.  
   - Goal: Secure raw materials for British industries and markets for British goods.

2. **Missionary Activities**  
   - Christian missionaries (CMS – Church Missionary Society, led by Samuel Ajayi Crowther) arrived from 1840s.  
   - Established schools, churches (Abeokuta, Badagry, Lagos).  
   - Converted local leaders, promoted Western education → created educated elite who later demanded reforms.  
   - Missionaries lobbied British government for protection against slave traders and local resistance.

3. **Company Rule (Royal Niger Company – 1886–1900)**  
   - 1886: Royal Niger Company (RNC) received Royal Charter from British government.  
   - Powers: Make treaties, maintain order, collect customs duties, administer justice.  
   - Signed over 300 treaties with local rulers (often under duress or deception).  
   - Controlled Niger-Benue trade route; clashed with local kings (e.g., Jaja of Opobo exiled 1887).  
   - Criticised for monopoly and brutality → charter revoked 1899.

4. **Crown Colony (Lagos – 1861–1900)**  
   - 1861: Lagos annexed as Crown Colony after bombardment and deposition of King Dosunmu (Cession Treaty – forced).  
   - Direct British rule: Governor, Executive Council, Legislative Council (limited African representation later).  
   - Became administrative and commercial hub.

5. **Protectorate (Oil Rivers / Southern & Northern Nigeria – 1885–1914)**  
   - 1885: Berlin Conference recognised British sphere of influence.  
   - 1891–1900: Oil Rivers Protectorate → Niger Coast Protectorate (under Consuls).  
   - 1900: Southern Nigeria Protectorate (amalgamated Lagos + Niger Coast).  
   - 1900: Northern Nigeria Protectorate (under High Commissioner Frederick Lugard).  
   - 1914: Amalgamation of Northern and Southern Protectorates by Lugard → Colony and Protectorate of Nigeria.

**Key Methods Used**  
- Treaty-making (often unequal or fraudulent)  
- Military force (gunboat diplomacy, bombardments)  
- Economic penetration (trade monopolies)  
- Missionary persuasion (cultural/religious influence)

### Subtopic b: The British Colonial Administrative Policy – Direct and Indirect Rule

**Direct Rule**  
- Used mainly in Southern Nigeria (Lagos Colony, parts of East).  
- British officials directly administered (governors, district officers).  
- Traditional rulers sidelined or reduced to minor roles.  
- Western laws, courts, taxation imposed directly.  
- Reason: Southern societies seen as less centralized; resistance to British authority.

**Indirect Rule**  
- Introduced by Frederick Lugard (Northern Nigeria, later parts of South).  
- British ruled through existing traditional rulers (emirs, chiefs).  
- Native Authorities retained local powers (tax collection, justice under native law/custom).  
- British officials supervised (Residents, District Officers).  
- Reason: Northern emirate system was centralized and hierarchical → easy to control through emirs.

**Merits of Indirect Rule (British View)**  
- Cheap (fewer British officials).  
- Less resistance (used existing structures).  
- Preserved some traditions.

**Demerits**  
- Strengthened autocratic rulers (emirs became more powerful).  
- Created North-South divide (indirect in North, direct in South).  
- Slowed Western education in North.

### Subtopic c: The French Colonial Administrative Policy – Assimilation and Association

**Assimilation** (Early Policy – 19th century to 1914)  
- Goal: Make Africans “French” in culture, language, law.  
- Full French citizenship for “assimilated” Africans (e.g., in Senegal’s Four Communes).  
- French laws, language, education imposed.  
- Africans expected to abandon traditions.  
- Failed: Very few assimilated; expensive; caused resentment.

**Association** (Later Policy – 1914 onwards)  
- Recognised failure of assimilation.  
- Rule through traditional chiefs under French supervision.  
- No citizenship; Africans remained subjects.  
- Indirect elements but full French control (no real autonomy).  
- Goal: Exploit resources with minimal cost.

**Key Differences**  
- Assimilation: Cultural integration → French identity.  
- Association: Cooperation with local elites → French supremacy.

### Subtopic d: Impact of British Colonial Rule

**Economic Impact**  
- **Positive**: Introduced cash crops (cocoa, groundnut, palm oil), railways, ports, modern banking.  
- **Negative**: Exploitation (raw materials exported), taxation burden, neglect of food production, dependency on Britain.

**Political Impact**  
- **Positive**: Unified Nigeria (1914 amalgamation), introduced modern administration, trained elite.  
- **Negative**: Arbitrary boundaries (ethnic divisions), indirect rule strengthened autocracy in North, limited African representation.

**Socio-Cultural Impact**  
- **Positive**: Western education, Christianity, English language, new elite class.  
- **Negative**: Cultural erosion, racism, missionary intolerance, North-South educational gap.

### Subtopic e: Comparison of British and French Colonial Administration

**Detailed Comparison Table**

| Aspect                  | British (Nigeria) – Indirect Rule                  | French (e.g., Senegal, Dahomey) – Assimilation/Association |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Ruling Method           | Through traditional rulers (emirs, chiefs)         | Direct French officials (assimilation) or supervised chiefs (association) |
| Citizenship             | No citizenship for Africans                        | Limited citizenship for assimilated (Four Communes)         |
| Legal System            | Native law/custom + British law                    | French civil law imposed                                    |
| Education/Language      | Limited Western education; English gradual         | French language compulsory; more schools                    |
| Autonomy for Locals     | Some local autonomy (indirect)                     | Very little (centralised control)                           |
| Economic Goal           | Exploitation with minimal cost                     | Full integration into French economy                        |
| Long-term Effect        | Preserved traditions but created divisions         | More cultural disruption but uniform administration         |

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The Royal Niger Company was used by Britain mainly for  
   A. missionary work  
   B. trade monopoly and treaty-making  
   C. direct military conquest  
   D. education of locals  
   **Answer: B**

2. Indirect rule was most successfully applied by the British in  
   A. Southern Nigeria  
   B. Northern Nigeria (emirates)  
   C. Lagos Colony  
   D. Eastern Nigeria  
   **Answer: B**

3. The main difference between British indirect rule and French assimilation is that  
   A. British used traditional rulers while French imposed French culture  
   B. French allowed full autonomy  
   C. British banned traditional rulers  
   D. French never used chiefs  
   **Answer: A**

4. One major economic impact of British colonial rule in Nigeria was  
   A. complete industrialisation  
   B. introduction of cash crops and export economy  
   C. abolition of all taxes  
   D. equal wealth distribution  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- **British Acquisition**: Trade → Missionaries → Company Rule → Crown Colony → Protectorate → 1914 Amalgamation.  
- **British Policy**: Indirect (North) vs. Direct (South).  
- **French Policy**: Assimilation (failed) → Association.  
- **Impact**: Exploitation + some infrastructure/education.  
- **Comparison**: British preserved traditions (indirect) vs. French imposed culture (assimilation).

Mnemonic for British process: **T – M – C – C – P – A**  
Trade → Missionaries → Company Rule → Crown Colony → Protectorate → Amalgamation.
