### 12. Political Parties and Party Systems

#### Subtopics

##### a. Political Parties: Definition, Organization (Structure), Functions
- **Definition**: A political party is an organized group of individuals with shared ideologies, values, or interests who seek to gain control of government through elections to implement their policies and programs. Unlike pressure groups, parties aim to capture and exercise power directly. They mobilize citizens, aggregate interests, and provide a platform for political participation. In democracies, they are essential for competition and accountability.
- **Organization (Structure)**: Parties have hierarchical structures to ensure coordination and discipline. In Nigeria (e.g., APC, PDP), this includes:
  - **National Level**: National Convention (supreme body for decisions/amendments), National Executive Committee (NEC – day-to-day management), National Chairman/Secretary.
  - **State/Zonal Level**: State Executive Committees, Zonal Coordinators for regional coordination.
  - **Local/Ward Level**: Local Government Committees, Ward Executives (grassroots mobilization).
  - **Special Organs**: Youth/Women's Wings, Disciplinary Committees, Board of Trustees (advisory elders).
  - Features: Vertical hierarchy for control; caucuses for internal democracy (though often weak in Nigeria due to godfatherism).
- **Functions**: Parties perform multiple roles in democracy:
  - Mobilize voters through campaigns, rallies, and voter education to increase participation.
  - Form government by winning elections and appointing officials (e.g., president, governors).
  - Policy formulation through manifestos and platforms, addressing national issues like security, economy.
  - Additional functions: Political recruitment (select candidates), interest aggregation (unite diverse groups), opposition role (criticize ruling party), linkage between citizens and government.

##### b. Party Systems: Definition, Types (One-Party, Two-Party, Multi-Party), Organization, Functions
- **Definition**: A party system refers to the number, organization, and interaction of political parties in a country's political landscape. It determines how power is contested, governments formed, and stability maintained.
- **Types**:
  - **One-Party System**: Only one legal/dominant party; opposition suppressed. Organization: Highly centralized, fused with state (e.g., party officials hold government posts). Functions: Mobilizes for national unity, implements ideology without competition. Examples: North Korea, former USSR.
  - **Two-Party System**: Two major parties alternate power; smaller parties marginal. Organization: Strong national structures, competitive but stable. Functions: Provides clear choices, ensures strong opposition. Examples: USA (Democrats vs. Republicans), UK (Conservatives vs. Labour).
  - **Multi-Party System**: Several parties compete; coalitions common. Organization: Fragmented, with parties often regional/ideological. Functions: Represents diversity, promotes inclusivity. Examples: Nigeria (APC, PDP, LP, etc.), India.
- **Organization and Functions in Each System**:
  - One-Party: Organization fused with government; functions focus on control and ideology enforcement.
  - Two-Party: Balanced organization for alternation; functions emphasize accountability and moderation.
  - Multi-Party: Diverse organization; functions include coalition-building and broad representation.

#### Learning Objectives
Candidates should follow this orderly sequence:
1. Define political parties and assess their role/functions (e.g., how they mobilize voters and form government).
2. Distinguish between types of party systems (e.g., multi-party in Nigeria vs. two-party elsewhere).
3. Analyze organization and functions in each system (e.g., centralized in one-party vs. fragmented in multi-party).
4. Evaluate impact on democracy (e.g., multi-party system's role in representation in Nigeria).
