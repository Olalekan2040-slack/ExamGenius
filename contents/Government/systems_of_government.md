**Topic 5: Systems of Governance**

This topic focuses on the different ways the executive and legislature relate to each other in running the government. It is closely linked to Topic 3 (Arms of Government) and Topic 4 (Structures), and JAMB often tests it by asking for distinctions, features, or which system Nigeria uses.

**JAMB Objective for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Distinguish between the different systems of governance.

The subtopics are:  
Presidential, Parliamentary, and Monarchical systems.

### Subtopic a: Presidential System

**Definition**  
A system of government in which the president is both head of state and head of government, elected directly or indirectly by the people, and operates independently of the legislature.

**Features**  
- Separation of powers between executive and legislature is strict.  
- President is elected for a fixed term (usually 4 years in Nigeria).  
- President is not a member of the legislature and cannot be easily removed by it.  
- President appoints ministers (often from outside the legislature).  
- Legislature cannot dissolve the executive; executive cannot dissolve legislature.  
- Clear checks and balances (e.g., veto power, impeachment).

**Merits**  
- Stability due to fixed tenure.  
- Strong and independent executive leadership.  
- Clear accountability (president answers directly to the people).  
- Prevents frequent changes of government.

**Demerits**  
- Risk of gridlock (if president and legislature are from different parties).  
- Can lead to executive dominance or authoritarianism.  
- Impeachment is difficult and rarely successful.  
- May encourage personality cult.

**Examples**  
Nigeria (since 1979 Constitution), United States, Brazil, most Latin American countries.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. In a presidential system of government, the president is  
   A. chosen by the legislature  
   B. both head of state and head of government  
   C. removable at any time by a vote of no confidence  
   D. required to be a member of parliament  
   **Answer: B**

2. One major feature that distinguishes the presidential system from the parliamentary system is  
   A. fusion of executive and legislative powers  
   B. fixed tenure for the chief executive  
   C. ability of the legislature to dissolve the executive  
   D. appointment of ministers only from parliament  
   **Answer: B**

3. Which of the following is a merit of the presidential system?  
   A. Frequent change of government  
   B. Stability due to fixed term of office  
   C. Easy removal of the president by parliament  
   D. Complete fusion of powers  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic b: Parliamentary System

**Definition**  
A system in which the executive (prime minister and cabinet) is drawn from and responsible to the legislature (parliament). The head of state (monarch or ceremonial president) is separate from the head of government.

**Features**  
- Fusion of powers: Executive members are also legislators.  
- Prime minister is leader of the majority party/coalition in parliament.  
- Prime minister can be removed by a vote of no confidence.  
- Parliament can be dissolved by the executive (prime minister advises head of state).  
- Collective responsibility: Cabinet members resign together if defeated.  
- Head of state is ceremonial (e.g., monarch or president).

**Merits**  
- Flexible – government can change quickly without full election.  
- Executive is directly accountable to legislature.  
- Promotes cooperation between executive and legislature.  
- Faster law-making due to fusion of powers.

**Demerits**  
- Instability if no clear majority (coalition crises).  
- Prime minister may dominate parliament (party discipline).  
- Frequent changes of government possible.  
- Weak separation of powers.

**Examples**  
United Kingdom, Canada, India, Australia, pre-1966 Nigeria (First Republic).

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

4. In a parliamentary system, the prime minister can lose office through  
   A. fixed term expiry only  
   B. a vote of no confidence in parliament  
   C. direct election by the people  
   D. impeachment by the judiciary  
   **Answer: B**

5. A key difference between parliamentary and presidential systems is that in parliamentary  
   A. the executive is independent of the legislature  
   B. there is fusion of executive and legislative powers  
   C. the head of government has a fixed term  
   D. ministers are appointed from outside parliament  
   **Answer: B**

6. One advantage of the parliamentary system is  
   A. complete separation of powers  
   B. flexibility and quick response to political crises  
   C. fixed tenure for the prime minister  
   D. no need for party majority in parliament  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic c: Monarchical System

**Definition**  
A system of government in which a monarch (king, queen, emperor) is the head of state. It can be absolute (monarch has real power) or constitutional (monarch is ceremonial, real power lies with elected officials).

**Features**  
- Hereditary head of state (monarch rules for life).  
- In **absolute monarchy**: Monarch has unlimited powers (makes laws, executes, judges).  
- In **constitutional monarchy**: Monarch performs ceremonial duties; real power is with prime minister/parliament (parliamentary system).  
- Often combined with parliamentary system in modern times.

**Merits**  
- Provides continuity and national symbol (monarch above politics).  
- Stability in constitutional form.  
- In absolute form: Quick decisions.  
- Preserves cultural/traditional values.

**Demerits**  
- In absolute form: High risk of tyranny and abuse.  
- Hereditary rule may produce incompetent rulers.  
- In constitutional form: Monarch is expensive (royalty costs).  
- Can be undemocratic if absolute.

**Examples**  
- Absolute: Saudi Arabia, Brunei, Vatican City (Pope).  
- Constitutional: United Kingdom, Sweden, Japan, Spain, Canada (Queen/King as head of state).

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

7. In a constitutional monarchy, the monarch  
   A. exercises real executive and legislative powers  
   B. performs mainly ceremonial functions  
   C. is elected by the people every four years  
   D. can veto all parliamentary decisions  
   **Answer: B**

8. Which of the following is a demerit of absolute monarchy?  
   A. Provides national unity  
   B. Risk of tyranny and lack of accountability  
   C. Ensures quick democratic elections  
   D. Promotes separation of powers  
   **Answer: B**

9. A modern example of a constitutional monarchy is  
   A. Saudi Arabia  
   B. United Kingdom  
   C. North Korea  
   D. Iran  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- Presidential = Nigeria today (fixed term, separation).  
- Parliamentary = UK (fusion, flexible).  
- Monarchical = UK (ceremonial king/queen + parliamentary) or Saudi (absolute power).
