**Topic 6: Institutions of Government in Post-Independence Nigeria**

This topic examines how the three arms of government (legislature, executive, judiciary) have functioned in Nigeria since independence in 1960. It builds on earlier topics like arms of government (Part I), constitutional development, and the military’s impact. Post-independence institutions evolved through parliamentary (1960–1966), military interruptions (1966–1979), presidential (1979–1983), and return to presidential democracy (1999–present). JAMB questions often ask you to:

- Describe the structure, functions, and workings of each arm in post-independence Nigeria  
- Compare changes across constitutions (e.g., parliamentary 1960–1966 vs. presidential 1979+)  
- Evaluate effectiveness, interrelations (separation of powers, checks and balances), and challenges (e.g., executive dominance)

### Subtopic a: The Legislature in Post-Independence Nigeria

**Structure (Evolution Across Periods)**  
- **1960–1966 (First Republic – Parliamentary)**: Bicameral federal parliament – House of Representatives (elected) + Senate (regional nominees). Regional Houses of Assembly.  
- **1966–1979 (Military)**: Suspended; replaced by Supreme Military Council (SMC).  
- **1979–1983 (Second Republic)**: Bicameral National Assembly – Senate (5 per state = 95) + House of Representatives (population-based).  
- **1983–1999 (Military)**: Suspended; AFRC, Provisional Ruling Council.  
- **1999–present (Fourth Republic)**: Bicameral National Assembly – Senate (3 per state + 1 FCT = 109) + House of Representatives (360 – population-based).

**Functions and Working**  
1. Law-making (primary function): Bills go through introduction, readings, committee stage, passage, presidential assent.  
2. Oversight: Probes, investigations, question ministers.  
3. Budget approval: Appropriation of funds.  
4. Representation: Constituency services.  
5. Impeachment: Remove president/governors, judges.  
6. Treaty ratification (Senate).  
7. Constitutional amendment (2/3 majority + states).

**Working in Practice**  
- First Republic: Regional parliaments strong; federal parliament dominated by North (NPC).  
- Second Republic: Assembly assertive (e.g., probes).  
- Fourth Republic: Often accused of executive dominance (budget padding, weak oversight). Positive: 8th/9th Assemblies passed key laws (Petroleum Industry Act, Electoral Act 2022).

**Challenges**  
- Executive interference (budget control).  
- Corruption (constituency projects).  
- Low public trust.

### Subtopic b: The Executive in Post-Independence Nigeria

**Structure (Evolution)**  
- **1960–1966 (Parliamentary)**: Prime Minister (head of government) + Cabinet from parliament; Governor-General (ceremonial). Regional Premiers.  
- **1966–1979 (Military)**: Head of State (military) + SMC → Federal Military Government; Military Governors.  
- **1979–1983 (Presidential)**: Executive President + Vice + Ministers (Federal Executive Council).  
- **1983–1999 (Military)**: Head of State + AFRC/Provincial Ruling Council; Military Administrators.  
- **1999–present**: Executive President (directly elected) + Vice + Ministers (FEC); Governors in states.

**Functions and Workings**  
1. Policy formulation and execution.  
2. Law implementation/enforcement.  
3. Foreign relations and defence (Commander-in-Chief).  
4. Budget preparation and presentation.  
5. Appointments (ministers, judges – with confirmation).  
6. Emergency powers (in crises).  
7. Signing/vetoing bills.

**Working in Practice**  
- First Republic: Prime Minister led coalition; regional premiers powerful.  
- Military eras: Absolute power (decrees).  
- Fourth Republic: Strong executive (president appoints ministers, controls security); often accused of overreach (e.g., non-compliance with court orders).

**Challenges**  
- Executive dominance (weak legislature/judiciary).  
- Corruption (e.g., Dasukigate, NNPC scandals).  
- Federal character sometimes leads to mediocrity.

### Subtopic c: The Judiciary in Post-Independence Nigeria

**Structure (Evolution)**  
- **1960–1966**: Supreme Court apex; High Courts; Privy Council appeals (UK).  
- **1966–1979**: Military decrees suspended parts; Supreme Court retained.  
- **1979–1983**: Supreme Court; Court of Appeal; Federal/State High Courts.  
- **1983–1999**: Military decrees limited judicial review.  
- **1999–present**: Supreme Court (apex); Court of Appeal; Federal High Court; State High Courts; Sharia/Customary Courts of Appeal; National Industrial Court.

**Functions and Workings**  
1. Interpretation of constitution and laws.  
2. Adjudication of disputes (civil, criminal).  
3. Judicial review: Declare laws/actions unconstitutional.  
4. Protection of fundamental rights (enforce Chapter IV).  
5. Election petitions (presidential, governorship).  
6. Advisory opinions (rare).

**Working in Practice**  
- First Republic: Independent but limited by regional influence.  
- Military eras: Decrees ousted jurisdiction (e.g., Decree 1 of 1984).  
- Fourth Republic: Active in election petitions (e.g., 2007, 2019, 2023); upheld rights (e.g., against executive overreach). Challenges: Delayed justice, corruption, executive non-compliance.

**Challenges**  
- Funding dependence on executive.  
- Appointment politics (NJ C recommends, president appoints).  
- Case backlog (millions pending).

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. In the 1960–1966 parliamentary system, the head of government was the  
   A. Governor-General  
   B. Prime Minister  
   C. President  
   D. Chief Justice  
   **Answer: B**

2. A major feature of the 1999 Constitution in the executive arm is  
   A. ceremonial president  
   B. executive president with direct election  
   C. prime minister from parliament  
   D. military head of state  
   **Answer: B**

3. The judiciary in post-independence Nigeria gained most independence under the  
   A. 1963 Constitution  
   B. military regimes  
   C. 1979 and 1999 Constitutions (separation of powers)  
   D. 1946 Richards Constitution  
   **Answer: C**

4. One challenge facing the judiciary in the Fourth Republic is  
   A. complete executive control over appointments  
   B. delayed justice and non-compliance with court orders  
   C. absence of fundamental rights  
   D. no appellate system  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- Legislature: Law-making, oversight; evolved from regional to bicameral federal.  
- Executive: Implementation; shifted from PM (parliamentary) to President (presidential).  
- Judiciary: Interpretation; gained independence but faces funding/political interference.

Mnemonic for evolution: **1960–66 P – 1966–79 M – 1979–83 P – 1983–99 M – 1999+ P**  
Parliamentary – Military – Presidential – Military – Presidential.

Key phrase: **“Post-independence institutions aimed at separation of powers but were weakened by military interruptions and executive dominance.”**
