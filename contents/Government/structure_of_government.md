**Topic 4: Structures of Governance**

This topic examines the major ways power is distributed between the central government and other units (regions, states, etc.). It is very important in JAMB because Nigeria practises federalism, and questions often ask for comparisons, merits/demerits, or reasons why Nigeria adopted federalism.

**JAMB Objective for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Compare the various political structures of governance.

The subtopics are:  
a. Unitary – features, reasons for adoption, merits and demerits  
b. Federal – features, reasons for adoption, merits and demerits  
c. Confederal – features, reasons for adoption, merits and demerits

### Subtopic a: Unitary Structure

**Definition**  
A system of government in which all powers are concentrated in one central (national) government. Sub-units (local governments, provinces) exist only as administrative arms of the centre and derive their authority from it.

**Features**  
- Single central authority with supreme power.  
- No constitutional division of powers between centre and regions.  
- Local units can be created, altered or abolished by the centre.  
- Uniform laws, policies, and administration across the country.  
- Single constitution and single citizenship.

**Reasons for Adoption**  
- Small size of country.  
- Homogeneous population (similar culture, language, religion).  
- Desire for strong central control and national unity.  
- Historical tradition of central rule.

**Merits**  
- Promotes national unity and uniformity in laws/policies.  
- Quick and efficient decision-making.  
- Easier coordination and resource allocation.  
- Less duplication of government functions.

**Demerits**  
- Over-centralization can lead to neglect of local needs.  
- Risk of dictatorship or abuse of power at the centre.  
- May cause ethnic/regional resentment in diverse societies.  
- Slow response to local emergencies.

**Examples**  
United Kingdom, France, Ghana (unitary with strong centre).

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. Which of the following is a major feature of a unitary system of government?  
   A. Power is shared equally between central and regional governments  
   B. Local governments derive their powers from the central authority  
   C. Regions have independent constitutions  
   D. The central government can be overruled by regional units  
   **Answer: B**

2. One major reason a country adopts a unitary system is  
   A. large population and ethnic diversity  
   B. desire for rapid economic development only  
   C. small size and cultural homogeneity  
   D. need for strong regional autonomy  
   **Answer: C**

3. A major demerit of the unitary system is that it  
   A. encourages local initiative  
   B. may lead to over-centralization and neglect of local interests  
   C. allows easy secession of regions  
   D. guarantees perfect national unity always  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic b: Federal Structure

**Definition**  
A system in which governmental powers are constitutionally divided between a central (federal) government and component units (states/regions), each operating independently within its sphere.

**Features**  
- Written constitution that divides powers (exclusive, concurrent, residual lists).  
- Supremacy of the federal constitution.  
- Independent legislative, executive, and judicial powers at both levels.  
- Bicameral legislature at federal level (often).  
- Dual citizenship (federal + state).  
- Independent sources of revenue for both levels.

**Reasons for Adoption**  
- Large geographical size.  
- Ethnic, cultural, religious, or linguistic diversity.  
- Historical existence of separate units before unification.  
- Desire to balance unity with regional autonomy.

**Merits**  
- Accommodates diversity and reduces ethnic tension.  
- Allows local experimentation and initiative.  
- Promotes national unity while preserving local identity.  
- Provides checks against central tyranny.

**Demerits**  
- Duplication of functions and high cost of administration.  
- Conflicts over revenue allocation and jurisdiction.  
- Risk of secessionist movements.  
- Slower decision-making due to consultations.

**Examples**  
Nigeria, United States, India, Canada, Germany.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

4. The division of powers into exclusive, concurrent and residual lists is a major characteristic of  
   A. unitary system  
   B. confederal system  
   C. federal system  
   D. monarchical system  
   **Answer: C**

5. Nigeria adopted federalism mainly because of  
   A. small size and cultural similarity  
   B. ethnic and regional diversity  
   C. desire for absolute central control  
   D. influence of military rule only  
   **Answer: B**

6. One advantage of federalism over unitary system is that it  
   A. ensures quicker policy implementation  
   B. allows for greater regional autonomy and diversity management  
   C. eliminates all forms of conflict  
   D. reduces government expenditure significantly  
   **Answer: B**

### Subtopic c: Confederal Structure

**Definition**  
A loose union of independent states where the central authority is weak and subordinate to the component units. The centre exists mainly to coordinate common interests.

**Features**  
- Component states retain most sovereignty.  
- Central government has limited powers (e.g., defence, foreign affairs only).  
- States can secede easily.  
- No strong central executive or judiciary.  
- Decisions often require unanimous consent.

**Reasons for Adoption**  
- Desire to preserve maximum independence of units.  
- Fear of domination by a strong centre.  
- Historical alliance of sovereign states.

**Merits**  
- Maximum regional/state autonomy.  
- Protects minority interests.  
- Flexible and voluntary union.

**Demerits**  
- Very weak central authority → inefficiency.  
- Difficulty in taking quick national decisions.  
- Risk of disintegration (easy secession).  
- Conflicts over common issues.

**Examples**  
- Historical: Confederate States of America (1861–1865), early Swiss Confederation.  
- Modern: Very rare; European Union has some confederal features but is not fully confederal.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

7. In a confederal system, the central government is  
   A. supreme over the component units  
   B. weak and subordinate to the component states  
   C. responsible for all internal affairs  
   D. able to abolish state governments  
   **Answer: B**

8. A major demerit of a confederal system is  
   A. over-centralization of power  
   B. difficulty in achieving national unity and quick decisions  
   C. excessive regional development  
   D. elimination of ethnic conflicts  
   **Answer: B**

9. Which of the following is most likely to adopt a confederal structure?  
   A. A small homogeneous country  
   B. Sovereign states wanting loose cooperation only  
   C. A large diverse nation needing strong unity  
   D. A monarchy with hereditary rulers  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- Unitary = strong centre (UK).  
- Federal = balanced power sharing (Nigeria).  
- Confederal = weak centre, strong units (rare).
