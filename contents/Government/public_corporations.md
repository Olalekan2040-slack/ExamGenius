**Topic 17: Public Corporations and Parastatals**  
(Part II – Political Development in Nigeria)

This topic examines government-owned enterprises created to provide essential public services, promote economic development, and achieve socio-economic objectives. It is highly relevant in JAMB because Nigeria has a long history of state involvement in the economy through these entities, followed by major policy shifts (privatization, commercialization) since the 1980s–1990s. Questions frequently ask you to:

- Define and classify public corporations/parastatals  
- State their purposes, functions, sources of finance, and control mechanisms  
- Explain the concepts of deregulation, privatization, and commercialization (with merits/demerits)  
- Compare public corporations and parastatals  
- Evaluate their performance/problems and the rationale for reforms in Nigeria

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Examine the operations of public corporations and parastatals.  
ii. Identify the processes involved in privatization and commercialization.  
iii. Assess the economic importance of privatization and commercialization.

The subtopics are:  
a. Definition, types, purpose and functions  
b. Finance, control and problems  
c. Deregulation, privatization, commercialization – objectives, features, merits and demerits  
d. Comparison between public corporations and parastatals

### Subtopic a: Definition, Types, Purpose and Functions

**Exhaustive Definition and Explanation**  
Public corporations (also called statutory corporations or state enterprises) are government-owned organizations established by a special Act of Parliament (or decree during military rule) to carry out commercial or quasi-commercial activities on behalf of the state. They are legal entities with perpetual succession, common seal, and power to sue/be sued.  

**Parastatals** is a broader term often used interchangeably in Nigeria to include public corporations, regulatory agencies, research institutes, and other government-owned bodies that are not full ministries but operate semi-autonomously.

**Key Characteristics**  
- Government ownership (wholly or majority shares).  
- Created by statute (Act/Decree).  
- Semi-autonomous (separate legal personality).  
- Commercial orientation (generate revenue) or social service orientation.  
- Board of directors appointed by government.

**Types of Public Corporations / Parastatals** (Common JAMB classification)

1. **Commercial / Profit-oriented**  
   Operate like private businesses, aim for profit.  
   Examples: NNPC (Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation), former NEPA/PHCN (now privatized), Nigeria Ports Authority (NPA), Nigerian Railway Corporation.

2. **Service / Non-profit-oriented**  
   Provide essential services at subsidized rates.  
   Examples: Former NITEL (telecoms), Nigerian Television Authority (NTA), Federal Radio Corporation of Nigeria (FRCN).

3. **Regulatory / Promotional**  
   Regulate industries or promote development.  
   Examples: Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC), NAFDAC.

4. **Research / Educational**  
   Conduct research or training.  
   Examples: Nigerian Institute of Medical Research, National Agency for Science and Engineering Infrastructure (NASENI).

5. **Mixed / Joint Ventures**  
   Government + private partnership.  
   Examples: Some oil blocks, liquefied natural gas projects.

**Purposes / Reasons for Establishment**  
- Provide essential services where private sector is unwilling/unable (e.g., rural electrification).  
- Promote economic development and industrialization (import substitution).  
- Achieve national self-reliance (e.g., oil exploration via NNPC).  
- Generate revenue for government.  
- Ensure social equity (subsidized services).  
- Control strategic sectors (oil, power, transport).

**Functions**  
1. Provide public utilities (electricity, water, transport).  
2. Generate revenue and foreign exchange (e.g., NNPC oil sales).  
3. Promote industrialization and employment.  
4. Regulate private sector activities (e.g., NAFDAC).  
5. Undertake research and development.  
6. Implement government policies in key sectors.

**Nigerian Historical Context**  
- Peak in 1970s (oil boom → nationalization of banks, oil companies).  
- Decline from 1986 (SAP → privatization push).

### Subtopic b: Finance, Control and Problems

**Sources of Finance**  
1. Government subventions / grants.  
2. Loans from government or banks.  
3. Internally generated revenue (sales, fees).  
4. Foreign loans/grants.  
5. Equity participation (government shares).

**Control Mechanisms**  
1. **Ministerial / Political Control** — Supervising minister gives policy direction.  
2. **Board of Directors** — Appointed by government; oversees management.  
3. **Legislative Control** — National Assembly approves budgets, probes.  
4. **Judicial Control** — Courts review ultra vires actions.  
5. **Audit / Financial Control** — Auditor-General audits accounts.  
6. **Public Complaints Commission / EFCC** — Investigates maladministration/corruption.

**Major Problems**  
1. **Political Interference** — Ministers appoint cronies; boards become political.  
2. **Corruption and Mismanagement** — Embezzlement, inflated contracts.  
3. **Inefficiency / Red-tapism** — Slow decision-making, bureaucracy.  
4. **Financial Dependence** — Heavy reliance on government subventions.  
5. **Poor Performance / Losses** — Many run at loss (e.g., former NEPA, NRC).  
6. **Overstaffing / Ghost Workers** — Bloated workforce.  
7. **Lack of Autonomy** — Excessive ministerial control.  
8. **Technological Backwardness** — Outdated equipment.  
9. **Monopoly Abuse** — No competition → poor service.

**Nigerian Examples**  
- NNPC: Oil revenue but allegations of opacity/corruption.  
- Former PHCN: Chronic power failure → privatization.  
- Ajaokuta Steel: Billions spent, still moribund.

### Subtopic c: Deregulation, Privatization, Commercialization

**Definitions and Objectives**

1. **Deregulation**  
   Removal or relaxation of government rules/regulations in an industry to allow more competition and private participation.  
   Objective: Reduce state monopoly, improve efficiency, attract investment.

2. **Privatization**  
   Transfer of ownership and control of public enterprises from government to private individuals or companies (full or partial sale of shares).  
   Objective: Improve efficiency, reduce government burden, raise revenue, promote competition.

3. **Commercialization**  
   Reforming public enterprises to operate strictly on commercial principles (profit-oriented) while retaining government ownership.  
   Objective: Make them self-sustaining, reduce subventions, improve management.

**Features / Processes**

- **Privatization** (Bureau of Public Enterprises – BPE): Sale of shares (public offer, core investor sale), concession, lease, management contract.  
- **Commercialization**: Performance targets, user charges, profit orientation.  
- **Deregulation**: Removal of price controls, licensing barriers (e.g., telecoms 2001).

**Merits (Advantages)**

**Privatization**  
- Efficiency and better service (e.g., GSM revolution after telecom privatization).  
- Reduces government expenditure.  
- Raises revenue from sales.  
- Attracts foreign investment.  
- Reduces corruption (less public funds to mismanage).

**Commercialization**  
- Self-financing → less burden on treasury.  
- Professional management.  
- Improved accountability.

**Deregulation**  
- Competition lowers prices.  
- Innovation and choice (e.g., telecoms).

**Demerits (Disadvantages)**

**Privatization**  
- Job losses (retrenchment).  
- Higher prices (no subsidies).  
- Monopoly by private owners.  
- Loss of strategic control (e.g., oil, power).  
- Corruption in sale process (undervaluation).

**Commercialization**  
- May neglect social objectives (e.g., rural access).  
- Still vulnerable to interference.

**Deregulation**  
- Market failure (e.g., poor rural service).  
- Exploitation if no strong regulation.

**Nigerian Context**  
- Started with SAP (1986) → Structural Adjustment.  
- Privatization Programme (1988–present): Over 200 enterprises sold/privatized (e.g., telecoms → success; power → mixed).  
- Bureau of Public Enterprises (BPE) oversees.  
- National Council on Privatization (NCP) chaired by Vice President.

### Subtopic d: Comparison between Public Corporations and Parastatals

**Exhaustive Comparison**

| Aspect                     | Public Corporations                            | Parastatals (Broader Term)                     |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Legal Basis                | Established by specific Act/Decree             | May be by Act, decree, or administrative order |
| Main Purpose               | Commercial/service delivery                    | Commercial, regulatory, research, promotional  |
| Degree of Autonomy         | Relatively higher (separate legal entity)      | Varies (some more controlled)                  |
| Profit Orientation         | Usually profit-seeking                         | May be non-profit (e.g., regulatory)           |
| Examples                   | NNPC, NPA, former NEPA                         | CBN, NAFDAC, NCC, NITDA, Universities          |
| Funding                    | Revenue + government grants                    | Grants dominant in regulatory/research         |
| Control                    | Board + minister                               | Minister + sometimes more direct control       |

**Nigerian Usage**  
In practice, the terms are often used interchangeably, but “public corporation” is narrower (commercial entities), while “parastatal” covers all semi-autonomous government bodies.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. Public corporations are primarily established to  
   A. regulate private businesses  
   B. provide essential services and promote economic development  
   C. conduct elections  
   D. make laws  
   **Answer: B**

2. A major problem of public corporations in Nigeria is  
   A. complete absence of government control  
   B. political interference, corruption, and inefficiency  
   C. over-privatization  
   D. lack of any commercial activity  
   **Answer: B**

3. Privatization in Nigeria aims mainly to  
   A. increase government ownership  
   B. improve efficiency and reduce fiscal burden  
   C. eliminate all public services  
   D. return to military rule  
   **Answer: B**

4. The process of making public enterprises operate strictly on profit principles while retaining government ownership is called  
   A. privatization  
   B. commercialization  
   C. deregulation  
   D. nationalization  
   **Answer: B**

5. Which of the following is a merit of privatization?  
   A. Guaranteed job security for all workers  
   B. Improved efficiency and service delivery  
   C. Complete elimination of competition  
   D. Increased government subventions  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- Definition: Government-owned commercial/service entities.  
- Types: Commercial (NNPC), Service (former NEPA), Regulatory (NAFDAC).  
- Problems: Interference, corruption, inefficiency.  
- Reforms: Privatization (sell), Commercialization (profit focus), Deregulation (remove controls).

Mnemonic for problems: **P – I – C – L – M**  
Political interference – Inefficiency – Corruption – Low morale – Mismanagement.

For reforms: **P – C – D**  
Privatization – Commercialization – Deregulation.

Nigeria: Shift from state-led (1970s) to market-led (1990s+) – telecom success vs. power sector mixed results.
