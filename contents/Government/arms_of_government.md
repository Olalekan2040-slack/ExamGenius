**Topic 3: Arms of Government**

This topic explains how power is divided and exercised in modern governments to prevent abuse and ensure efficiency. It is one of the most frequently tested areas in JAMB Government. Questions often ask for functions of each arm, how they relate, or examples of checks and balances (especially in Nigeria).

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Identify the duties and obligations of the various arms of government and their agencies.  
ii. Relate each arm to its functions.  
iii. Appreciate how these arms interrelate.

The subtopics are:  
a. The Legislature – types, structure, functions, powers  
b. The Executive – types, functions, powers  
c. The Judiciary – functions, powers, components  
d. Their relationships

### Subtopic a: The Legislature

**Definition**  
The law-making arm of government. It represents the people and makes, amends, and repeals laws.

**Types**  
- Unicameral: One chamber (e.g., New Zealand Parliament).  
- Bicameral: Two chambers (e.g., Nigeria’s National Assembly: Senate + House of Representatives; USA Congress: Senate + House).

**Structure (in Nigeria – 1999 Constitution)**  
- National Assembly (federal): Senate (109 members – 3 per state + 1 FCT) + House of Representatives (360 members – based on population).  
- State Houses of Assembly (unicameral, one per state).

**Functions**  
1. Law-making (primary function): Introduces, debates, and passes bills into laws.  
2. Approval of budgets and financial control (appropriates money).  
3. Oversight and scrutiny of the executive (investigates, questions ministers).  
4. Representation of the people (constituency services).  
5. Amendment of the constitution (with special majority).  
6. Impeachment/removal of certain officials (e.g., president, governors, judges).  
7. Approval of treaties and declarations of war (in some systems).

**Powers**  
- Exclusive powers (e.g., currency, defence, aviation – federal only).  
- Concurrent powers (shared with states, e.g., education, health).  
- Power of the purse (controls government spending).  
- Power to summon witnesses and investigate (e.g., public hearings).

**Nigerian Example**  
The National Assembly passed the Petroleum Industry Act (2021) after debates and public hearings.

### Subtopic b: The Executive

**Definition**  
The arm responsible for implementing laws, policies, and day-to-day administration of the country.

**Types**  
- Presidential: Head of state and head of government are the same person (elected president). Nigeria, USA.  
- Parliamentary: Head of government (prime minister) is separate from head of state (ceremonial president/monarch). UK, India (mostly).  
- Semi-presidential: Mix (e.g., France – president + prime minister).

**Structure (in Nigeria – Presidential system)**  
- President (head of state and government).  
- Vice President.  
- Ministers (Federal Executive Council).  
- Ministries, departments, agencies (MDAs).  
- Governors (at state level).

**Functions**  
1. Implementation and enforcement of laws.  
2. Policy formulation and execution.  
3. Maintenance of law and order (police, security agencies).  
4. Foreign relations and diplomacy.  
5. Appointment of key officials (with legislative approval in some cases).  
6. Preparation and presentation of annual budget.  
7. Defence of the country (Commander-in-Chief of armed forces).  
8. Signing or vetoing bills (in presidential systems).

**Powers**  
- Executive powers (wide discretion in administration).  
- Power to appoint and dismiss ministers/permanent secretaries.  
- Power of prerogative of mercy (pardon).  
- Emergency powers (in some cases).  
- Veto power over legislation (can be overridden by legislature).

**Nigerian Example**  
The President signs international agreements (e.g., AfCFTA) and appoints INEC Chairman (subject to Senate confirmation).

### Subtopic c: The Judiciary

**Definition**  
The arm that interprets laws, settles disputes, and administers justice.

**Components/Structure (in Nigeria)**  
- Supreme Court (apex court).  
- Court of Appeal.  
- Federal High Court, State High Courts, Sharia/Customary Courts of Appeal.  
- Magistrate/District Courts (lower courts).  
- National Industrial Court (specialized).

**Functions**  
1. Interpretation of the constitution and laws.  
2. Adjudication of disputes (civil and criminal cases).  
3. Protection of fundamental human rights.  
4. Judicial review (power to declare laws or actions unconstitutional).  
5. Punishment of offenders and enforcement of contracts.  
6. Advisory opinions (in some cases, e.g., to the president).

**Powers**  
- Power of judicial review (striking down unconstitutional laws/actions).  
- Power to interpret the constitution.  
- Contempt of court power.  
- Independence from other arms (secured by tenure, salary protection).

**Nigerian Example**  
The Supreme Court resolved the 2023 presidential election petitions and has declared some state actions unconstitutional.

### Subtopic d: Their Relationships (Interrelations)

The three arms are designed to work together but remain independent (separation of powers) while checking each other (checks and balances).

**Key Principles**  
- **Separation of Powers** — Each arm has distinct functions (Montesquieu’s theory).  
- **Checks and Balances** — Prevents any arm from becoming too powerful.

**Examples of Interrelations (especially in Nigeria)**  
1. Legislature checks Executive:  
   - Confirms ministerial appointments.  
   - Can impeach president/governor.  
   - Investigates executive actions (e.g., probes).  

2. Executive checks Legislature:  
   - Can veto bills (president).  
   - Can dissolve assembly in some parliamentary systems (not Nigeria).  
   - Initiates most bills (especially money bills).  

3. Judiciary checks both:  
   - Declares executive actions or laws unconstitutional.  
   - Protects citizens from legislative or executive abuse.  

4. Legislature checks Judiciary:  
   - Confirms judicial appointments (Senate confirms Chief Justice).  
   - Can impeach judges.  

5. Executive checks Judiciary:  
   - Appoints judges (on recommendation of NJC).  
   - Provides funding (but cannot reduce salaries).  

**Importance in Nigeria**  
The 1999 Constitution (Sections 4, 5, 6) clearly separates powers. However, in practice, executive dominance (e.g., control of funds) sometimes weakens checks.

**Quick Summary Table**

| Arm         | Main Function       | Head (Nigeria)       | Key Check on Others                          | Key Check by Others                          |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------------|----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Legislature | Law-making          | Senate President / Speaker | Investigates, impeaches, approves appointments | Veto (executive), judicial review            |
| Executive   | Implementation      | President / Governor | Veto bills, appoints officials               | Impeachment, oversight, judicial review      |
| Judiciary   | Interpretation/Justice | Chief Justice       | Judicial review, declares actions invalid    | Appointment & impeachment by others          |

**How to Achieve the Objectives**  
- Identify duties/obligations → List functions and powers for each arm.  
- Relate to functions → Explain what each arm does and why (e.g., legislature makes laws so executive can implement them).  
- Appreciate interrelations → Give examples of checks and balances (use Nigerian cases where possible).

**Self-Test (JAMB-Style)**  
1. State four functions of the legislature in Nigeria.  
2. Distinguish between the powers of the executive and judiciary.  
3. Explain two ways the legislature checks the executive.  
4. Why is separation of powers important?
