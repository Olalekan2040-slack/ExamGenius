**Topic 22: PART III – Relations with African Countries**

This topic is one of the most frequently tested and highest-scoring sections in JAMB Government, especially since 2015–2025 papers. It directly tests the syllabus statement that “Africa is the centrepiece of Nigeria’s foreign policy” — a phrase repeated by every Nigerian leader since 1960. Questions often require you to:

- Explain why and how Africa became the “centrepiece”  
- Identify the guiding principles and how they have been implemented  
- Evaluate Nigeria’s role in continental affairs (successes, failures, costs)  
- Define NEPAD, its origin, objectives, and implications for Nigeria/Africa  
- Assess whether the Africa-centric policy is still relevant today

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Evaluate the role of Nigeria in continental affairs.  
ii. Assess the role of NEPAD in developing Africa.

The subtopics are:  
a. Africa as the “centre piece” of Nigeria’s foreign policy – guiding principles, implementation and implications  
b. NEPAD – origin, objectives and implications

### Subtopic a: Africa as the “Centre Piece” of Nigeria’s Foreign Policy

**Exhaustive and Detailed Explanation**

**Meaning of “Africa as the Centrepiece”**  
Since independence on October 1, 1960, every Nigerian government (civilian and military) has officially declared that **Africa is the centrepiece** (or cornerstone) of Nigeria’s foreign policy. This means that the liberation, unity, peace, development, and dignity of the African continent and its peoples are the highest priority in Nigeria’s external relations — above relations with Europe, America, Asia, or any other region.

**Historical Origin and Evolution**  
- **1960 (Balewa – First Republic)**: First articulated at UN General Assembly — Nigeria would focus on African decolonization.  
- **1970s (Gowon / Murtala / Obasanjo)**: Radical phase — “Africa has been the centrepiece… and will continue to be” (Murtala/Obasanjo famous speech).  
- **1980s–1990s (military eras)**: Maintained despite internal crises.  
- **1999–present (Fourth Republic)**: Reaffirmed by Obasanjo, Yar’Adua, Jonathan, Buhari, Tinubu — though with more emphasis on economic diplomacy.

**Guiding Principles of the Africa-Centric Policy**

1. **Total eradication of colonialism and apartheid**  
   Support for liberation movements (financial, diplomatic, military training).  

2. **Promotion of African unity and integration**  
   Founding member of OAU (1963) → AU (2002); strong supporter of ECOWAS (1975).

3. **Peaceful settlement of disputes**  
   Mediation in conflicts (e.g., Chad, Liberia, Sierra Leone, Sudan, Mali).

4. **Good neighbourliness and non-interference**  
   Respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity of neighbours.

5. **Promotion of economic cooperation and development**  
   ECOWAS protocols, NEPAD, AfCFTA support.

6. **Racial equality and dignity of black people**  
   Anti-racism stance globally.

**Implementation (How Nigeria Has Put It into Practice)**

1. **Support for Liberation Movements (1960s–1990s)**  
   - Financial aid, training, and diplomatic support to ANC (South Africa), SWAPO (Namibia), MPLA (Angola), ZANU-PF (Zimbabwe), PAIGC (Guinea-Bissau).  
   - Boycotted apartheid South Africa in sports, Commonwealth, etc.  
   - Mandela called Nigeria “the moral conscience of the world” on apartheid.

2. **Peacekeeping and Conflict Resolution**  
   - ECOMOG (ECOWAS Monitoring Group): Led interventions in Liberia (1990), Sierra Leone (1997), Guinea-Bissau.  
   - UN peacekeeping: Largest African contributor (UNAMID, UNMIL, MONUSCO).  
   - Mediation: Bakassi Peninsula (ICJ ruling accepted), Mali crisis, Sudan.

3. **Diplomatic Leadership in Africa**  
   - Hosted OAU summits (1970s, 2000s).  
   - Pushed for AU formation (2002).  
   - Leadership in ECOWAS (multiple chairmanships).

4. **Technical and Development Assistance**  
   - Technical Aid Corps (TAC) – sent professionals to over 40 African/Caribbean countries.  
   - Scholarships for African students.  
   - Debt relief advocacy for African countries.

5. **Economic Diplomacy**  
   - Support for AfCFTA (signed 2019, ratified).  
   - Investments in other African countries (Dangote, banks).

**Implications / Consequences (Positive and Negative)**

**Positive Implications**  
- Enhanced Nigeria’s continental and global prestige (“giant of Africa”).  
- Leadership in OAU/AU/ECOWAS.  
- Moral authority on decolonization and anti-apartheid.  
- Soft power through TAC and peacekeeping.  
- Regional stability (ECOMOG successes in Liberia/Sierra Leone).

**Negative Implications / Criticisms**  
- Huge financial cost (billions on peacekeeping, aid).  
- Domestic neglect — critics say “charity begins at home” (poverty, infrastructure decay).  
- Loss of lives (Nigerian soldiers in ECOMOG).  
- Perceived arrogance (“big brother syndrome”) by smaller neighbours.  
- Inconsistency (e.g., silent on some African dictatorships).  
- Overstretch (military overstretched by internal security).

**Current Relevance (2025 Context)**  
- Still officially the centrepiece (Tinubu administration reaffirms).  
- Shift towards economic diplomacy (trade, investment).  
- Challenges: Terrorism (Lake Chad), coups (Sahel), economic competition (China’s growing influence).

### Subtopic b: NEPAD – Origin, Objectives and Implications

**Exhaustive and Detailed Explanation**

**Origin**  
- **New Partnership for Africa’s Development (NEPAD)** launched in 2001.  
- Initiated by five African leaders: Thabo Mbeki (South Africa), Olusegun Obasanjo (Nigeria), Abdelaziz Bouteflika (Algeria), Abdoulaye Wade (Senegal), Hosni Mubarak (Egypt).  
- Adopted by OAU in July 2001 (Lusaka Summit) → became AU programme in 2002.  
- Nigeria played leading role — Obasanjo chaired implementation committee.

**Objectives of NEPAD**

1. **Eradicate poverty** through sustainable development.  
2. **Promote good governance**, democracy, human rights, rule of law.  
3. **Accelerate economic growth** and integration (intra-African trade).  
4. **Attract foreign direct investment** and development assistance.  
5. **Empower women** and youth.  
6. **Reduce debt burden** and improve market access.  
7. **Promote peace, security, and stability**.  
8. **African ownership** of development agenda (no donor imposition).

**Key Components / Mechanisms**  
- **African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM)**: Voluntary self-assessment of governance (Nigeria peer-reviewed 2008).  
- **Infrastructure projects** (roads, energy, ICT).  
- **Agriculture (CAADP)** — Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Programme.  
- **Capacity building** and skills development.

**Implications for Nigeria and Africa**

**Positive Implications**  
- Enhanced Nigeria’s leadership (Obasanjo chaired 2001–2008).  
- Attracted international support (G8 Africa Action Plan 2002).  
- Promoted good governance discourse (APRM).  
- Infrastructure gains (e.g., Lagos–Abidjan corridor).  
- Shift from aid dependence to partnership.

**Negative Implications / Criticisms**  
- Slow implementation (bureaucracy, funding gaps).  
- Elite-driven (limited grassroots impact).  
- APRM voluntary → weak enforcement.  
- Dependency on Western donors despite “African ownership”.  
- Limited tangible poverty reduction.

**Current Status (2025)**  
- Integrated into AU Agenda 2063.  
- Nigeria remains active but focus shifted to AfCFTA (2019) and bilateral deals.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The phrase “Africa as the centrepiece of Nigeria’s foreign policy” was most strongly associated with the administration of  
   A. Nnamdi Azikiwe  
   B. Murtala Mohammed / Olusegun Obasanjo  
   C. Sani Abacha  
   D. Goodluck Jonathan  
   **Answer: B**

2. One major implication of Nigeria’s Africa-centric policy is that it has  
   A. completely eliminated domestic poverty  
   B. enhanced Nigeria’s continental leadership and prestige  
   C. led to isolation from Western powers  
   D. reduced Nigeria’s peacekeeping role  
   **Answer: B**

3. NEPAD was primarily initiated by  
   A. Western donors  
   B. five African leaders including Obasanjo of Nigeria  
   C. the United Nations  
   D. ECOWAS alone  
   **Answer: B**

4. A key objective of NEPAD is to  
   A. promote military intervention in Africa  
   B. eradicate poverty and promote good governance through African ownership  
   C. return Africa to colonial rule  
   D. eliminate all foreign investment  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- **Centrepiece**: Africa first since 1960 — liberation, unity, peace.  
- **Implementation**: Anti-apartheid, ECOMOG, TAC, AU/ECOWAS leadership.  
- **Implications**: Prestige (positive) vs. cost/domestic neglect (negative).  
- **NEPAD**: 2001 African initiative — poverty eradication, governance, integration.

Mnemonic for principles: **L – U – P – G – E**  
Liberation – Unity – Peace – Good governance – Economic cooperation.

Nigeria’s Africa policy slogan: **“We are our brothers’ keeper”** — used by many leaders.
