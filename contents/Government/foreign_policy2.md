**Topic 21: PART III – Nigeria’s Foreign Policy**  
**2. Nigeria’s Foreign Policy**

This topic builds directly on the previous one (general foreign policy) by applying the concepts specifically to Nigeria. It is one of the highest-yield sections in recent JAMB Government exams. Questions frequently require you to:

- Identify the major objectives of Nigeria’s foreign policy  
- Explain and evaluate the non-aligned posture (theory vs. practice)  
- Analyse relations with major powers (US, UK, Russia, China, France)  
- Discuss relations with developing countries (e.g., Technical Aid Corps, South-South cooperation)  
- Assess consistency or shifts across regimes

**JAMB Objectives for this Topic**  
Candidates should be able to:  
i. Identify the major objectives of Nigeria’s foreign policy.  
ii. Analyse Nigeria’s non-aligned posture.

The subtopics are:  
a. Relations with major powers  
b. Relations with developing countries, e.g., the Technical Aid Corps (TAC), etc.  
c. Nigeria’s Non-Alignment Policy

### Subtopic a: Relations with Major Powers

**Exhaustive and Detailed Explanation**

Nigeria’s relations with major powers have been pragmatic, guided by national interest (security, economic development, global influence) rather than rigid ideology. Since independence, Nigeria has balanced relations to avoid over-dependence on any single bloc.

**1. United Kingdom (UK)**  
- **Historical Context**: Colonial power (1914–1960).  
- **Post-Independence**: Strong ties (Commonwealth membership, education, military training).  
- **Economic**: Major trading partner (oil exports, investments).  
- **Political**: Influence waned after 1970s nationalisation; strained during Abacha era (sanctions).  
- **Current**: Development aid, security cooperation (counter-terrorism), cultural ties (education, diaspora).  
- **Evaluation**: Historically dominant → now pragmatic partnership.

**2. United States of America (USA)**  
- **Cold War Era**: Nigeria non-aligned, but US seen as capitalist/imperialist.  
- **Post-Cold War**: Strategic partner (oil, counter-terrorism – Boko Haram).  
- **Economic**: Largest buyer of Nigerian crude (until recent shifts to Asia); AGOA beneficiary.  
- **Political**: Tensions during military rule (sanctions); improved under democracy (Obama/Trump/Biden eras).  
- **Security**: Military training, equipment (despite Magnitsky Act restrictions on some officers).  
- **Evaluation**: Critical for security/economy but Nigeria wary of perceived hegemony.

**3. Russia (formerly USSR)**  
- **Cold War**: USSR supported liberation movements (ANC, MPLA); Nigeria bought arms during civil war.  
- **Post-Cold War**: Arms supplier (MiG-29s, helicopters for Boko Haram fight).  
- **Economic**: Ajaokuta Steel revival talks, nuclear energy cooperation.  
- **Political**: Closer ties under Buhari (anti-Western sentiment).  
- **Evaluation**: Strategic counterbalance to Western influence; limited economic depth.

**4. China**  
- **Early Relations**: Diplomatic ties 1971; supported anti-apartheid.  
- **2000s Boom**: Massive infrastructure loans (roads, railways – Belt and Road).  
- **Economic**: Largest trading partner (imports: machinery, exports: oil).  
- **Political**: Non-interference policy attractive; debt-trap concerns.  
- **Evaluation**: Rapidly growing; “win-win” rhetoric but rising debt and trade imbalance.

**5. France**  
- **Historical**: Colonial rival (neighbours – Benin, Niger, Cameroon).  
- **Post-Independence**: Strained (Bakassi dispute – resolved 2008 ICJ).  
- **Current**: Security cooperation (Lake Chad Basin – MNJTF).  
- **Evaluation**: Pragmatic; limited compared to Anglophone ties.

**General Evaluation**  
Nigeria maintains **balanced multi-alignment** — no exclusive bloc. Shifts reflect regimes: radical (Murtala/Obasanjo), pragmatic (Babangida/Abacha), citizen-focused (Yar’Adua/Jonathan), assertive (Buhari).

### Subtopic b: Relations with Developing Countries

**Exhaustive and Detailed Explanation**

Nigeria positions itself as a leader of the Global South, emphasizing South-South cooperation, technical assistance, and solidarity against imperialism.

**Key Areas**

1. **Technical Aid Corps (TAC)**  
   - Launched 1987 by Babangida.  
   - Nigerian professionals (doctors, teachers, engineers) serve in African, Caribbean, Pacific countries.  
   - Objectives: Technical cooperation, soft power, diaspora engagement.  
   - Impact: Over 5,000 volunteers; improved Nigeria’s image; cost-effective diplomacy.

2. **Support for Liberation Movements**  
   - Anti-apartheid: Financial/military aid to ANC, SWAPO, MPLA.  
   - Frontline States support (Mozambique, Zimbabwe).

3. **ECOWAS and Regional Integration**  
   - Founded ECOWAS 1975; leads peacekeeping (ECOMOG in Liberia, Sierra Leone).  
   - Free movement, trade protocols.

4. **Commonwealth and Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)**  
   - Active member; hosted CHOGM 2003.  
   - NAM: Co-founder role (1960s–70s).

5. **Relations with Caribbean and Pacific**  
   - Commonwealth solidarity; scholarships, aid.

6. **South-South Cooperation**  
   - China, India, Brazil partnerships (trade, technology transfer).

**Evaluation**  
Nigeria’s Africa-centric and South-South policy enhances prestige but is costly (TAC, peacekeeping). Domestic critics argue funds should address internal poverty.

### Subtopic c: Nigeria’s Non-Alignment Policy

**Exhaustive and Detailed Explanation**

**Definition**  
Non-alignment is the policy of not formally aligning with or against any major power bloc (especially during the Cold War), while maintaining independence in foreign policy decisions.

**Historical Origins in Nigeria**  
- Adopted at independence (1960) by Balewa.  
- Influenced by Nehru (India), Nasser (Egypt), Tito (Yugoslavia).  
- Formalised in Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) – Bandung Conference 1955, Belgrade 1961.

**Key Principles (in Theory)**  
- Independence in judgment.  
- Peaceful co-existence.  
- Anti-colonialism / anti-imperialism.  
- Support for liberation movements.  
- Disarmament and peaceful resolution.

**Nigeria’s Practice (Reality Check)**  
- **Cold War Era**: Officially non-aligned but tilted West (UK/US ties, oil economy).  
  - Supported liberation movements (radical).  
  - Accepted Western aid/investment.  
- **Post-Cold War**: Non-alignment evolved to **multi-alignment** (relations with all powers).  
- **Criticisms**:  
  - Not truly non-aligned (pro-Western economic dependence).  
  - Selective (anti-apartheid but silent on some African dictatorships).  
  - Pragmatic rather than ideological.

**Evaluation**  
- **Successes**: Avoided Cold War entrapment; built African leadership; peacekeeping credibility.  
- **Limitations**: Economic weakness forced compromises; post-Cold War relevance declined; domestic critics call it inconsistent.

**JAMB-Style Objective Questions (4 options each)**

1. The centrepiece of Nigeria’s foreign policy is  
   A. Europe and North America  
   B. Africa and African unity  
   C. Asia and the Pacific  
   D. Latin America  
   **Answer: B**

2. The Technical Aid Corps (TAC) is an example of Nigeria’s  
   A. military intervention policy  
   B. relations with developing countries and technical cooperation  
   C. alignment with Western powers  
   D. isolationist policy  
   **Answer: B**

3. Nigeria’s non-aligned posture during the Cold War was mainly  
   A. complete neutrality and isolation  
   B. independence from major blocs while supporting liberation movements  
   C. formal alliance with the Soviet Union  
   D. exclusive partnership with the United States  
   **Answer: B**

4. In practice, Nigeria’s non-alignment has been described as  
   A. rigid and unchanging  
   B. pragmatic multi-alignment with all major powers  
   C. total rejection of Western ties  
   D. alignment only with African states  
   **Answer: B**

**Summary & Quick Revision Tip**  
- **Major Objectives**: Africa centrepiece, non-alignment, peace, economic diplomacy.  
- **Non-Alignment**: Theory = independence; Practice = balanced relations with all powers.  
- **Major Powers**: UK (historical), US (security/economy), Russia (arms), China (infrastructure).  
- **Developing Countries**: TAC, liberation support, ECOWAS leadership.

Mnemonic for objectives: **A – N – P – E – G**  
Africa centrepiece – Non-alignment – Peacekeeping – Economic diplomacy – Global South solidarity.

Nigeria’s foreign policy slogan: **“Africa is the centrepiece”** — repeated by every leader since 1960.
