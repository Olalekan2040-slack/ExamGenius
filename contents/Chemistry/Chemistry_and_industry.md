TOPIC: Chemistry and IndustryIntroductionChemistry and industry shows how chemical knowledge is used to make useful products on a large scale. Chemical industries turn raw materials (like air, water, crude oil, natural gas, minerals) into everyday things such as fertilisers, acids, plastics, drugs, fuels, and soaps.  For a complete beginner: Think of it like a big kitchen – raw ingredients (crude oil, air) are processed with special recipes (chemical reactions) to make finished products (ammonia fertiliser, sulphuric acid, ethanol from biotechnology).  This topic appears in JAMB with 2–4 questions usually. They ask about:Types of chemical industries and their raw materials  
Important processes like Haber process (for ammonia) and Contact process (for sulphuric acid)  
How biotechnology uses living things (microbes) to make products like ethanol

Chemical industries are grouped by what they produce:Petrochemical industries – from crude oil and natural gas → fuels, plastics, solvents

Water (H₂O)  
Natural gas (CH₄)  
Crude oil (petroleum)  
Minerals (rock phosphate, salt, limestone)

SUBTOPIC: Important Processes (Haber and Contact)Objective 2: Explain Haber and Contact processesExplanation (step by step):1. Haber Process – Making Ammonia (NH₃)
Equation: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (exothermic, reversible)  Conditions:  High pressure (200–300 atm)  
Medium temperature (400–450°C)  
Iron catalyst  
Raw materials: N₂ from air, H₂ from natural gas

Refrigerant  
Cleaning agent

2. Contact Process – Making Sulphuric Acid (H₂SO₄)
Steps:  Burning sulphur or roasting pyrite → SO₂  
Catalytic oxidation (V₂O₅ catalyst) → SO₃  
Absorption in concentrated H₂SO₄ → oleum  
Dilution → H₂SO₄

Common Mistakes:  Thinking Haber process uses high temperature (no – medium 450°C)  
Confusing catalysts (Haber = iron, Contact = V₂O₅)

SUBTOPIC: BiotechnologyObjective 3: Relate processes to biotechnologyExplanation (simple):
Biotechnology uses living things (microbes, enzymes) to make useful products. It is "green" chemistry – uses mild conditions (room temperature, no harsh chemicals).Important Example: Ethanol Production (Biofuel)  Raw material: Sugarcane, cassava, corn (starch/sugar)  
Microbe: Yeast (Saccharomyces)  
Process: Fermentation – sugar → ethanol + CO₂
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂

Here are real pictures of industrial biotechnology for ethanol:This picture shows a large industrial fermenter (bioreactor) used for ethanol production.This diagram shows the typical layout of fuel ethanol fermentation plant.This one shows yeast fermentation in action for bioethanol.Uses of Biotechnology in Industry:Ethanol (biofuel, drinks)  
Antibiotics (penicillin)  
Enzymes for detergents  
Lactic acid for plastics

Relation to Other Processes:  Biotechnology is greener than Haber/Contact (less energy, no high pressure)  
In Nigeria: Cassava is used for bioethanol production

Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "The process used to produce ethanol from cassava is: A) Distillation B) Fermentation C) Cracking D) Reforming" (Answer: B)  
Question 2: "Biotechnology is important because it: A) Uses high temperature B) Uses living organisms C) Needs crude oil D) Produces only inorganic chemicals" (Answer: B)

Common Mistakes:  Thinking fermentation needs high temperature (no – room temperature, yeast works best at 25–35°C)  
Confusing biotechnology with chemical synthesis
