TOPIC: Oxidation and Reduction – RedoxIntroductionRedox (short for Reduction-Oxidation) is the name we give to chemical reactions where one substance loses electrons and another gains electrons at the same time.
This topic is very very important in JAMB – almost every year they ask 3–5 questions about it.
Many students find it hard because of the new words, but if we go slowly step by step, even a complete beginner can understand it perfectly.Think of redox like this simple story:
One friend (the reducing agent) gives away his pocket money (electrons) → he becomes poor (oxidised).
The other friend (the oxidising agent) takes the money (gains electrons) → he becomes rich (reduced).
They always happen together – no one can give without someone receiving!Here are very simple pictures that will help beginners understand redox quickly:This picture shows a simple cartoon of oxidation (loss of electrons) and reduction (gain of electrons) happening together.This colourful chart shows the famous OIL RIG rule that every JAMB student must remember.SUBTOPIC: Oxidation and Reduction DefinitionsObjective 1: Identify redox processesExplanation (very simple for beginners):
Redox is not two separate things – it is always two things happening at the same time.We use this easy rule to remember:
OIL RIG
Oxidation Is Loss of electrons
Reduction Is Gain of electronsAnother easy way to remember (many Nigerian students like this one):
LEO says GER
Loss of Electrons = Oxidation
Gain of Electrons = ReductionExamples every beginner should know:When magnesium burns in air → magnesium loses electrons → magnesium is oxidised  
Oxygen gains those electrons → oxygen is reduced

So the whole reaction is a redox reaction.Key Points (learn these 4 definitions by heart):Oxidation = loss of electrons  
Reduction = gain of electrons  
Oxidising agent = the substance that takes electrons (it gets reduced)  
Reducing agent = the substance that gives electrons (it gets oxidised)

Visual Items:  Card 1: OIL RIG (big letters)

Example Question Pattern (JAMB loves these simple ones):  Question 1: "In the reaction 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, which substance is oxidised? A) Oxygen B) Magnesium C) Magnesium oxide D) None" (Answer: B)  
Question 2: "The process of gaining electrons is called: A) Oxidation B) Reduction C) Neutralisation D) Precipitation" (Answer: B)  
Question 3: "Which of these shows oxidation? A) Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ B) Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu C) Both A and B D) Neither" (Answer: A)  
Question 4: "In Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu, the reducing agent is: A) CuSO₄ B) Zn C) Cu D) ZnSO₄" (Answer: B)

Common Mistakes Beginners Make (avoid these!):Thinking oxidation means the substance must gain oxygen (wrong – it can lose electrons without oxygen)  
Saying the oxidising agent is oxidised (no! oxidising agent causes oxidation, so it gets reduced)  
Forgetting that both oxidation and reduction must happen together

SUBTOPIC: Balancing Redox EquationsObjective 3: Balance redox equations (step-by-step method for beginners)Explanation (the easiest method for JAMB beginners – Half-reaction method):
We break the big equation into two small half equations: one for oxidation and one for reduction. Then we balance them and join them back.Step-by-step guide (follow this exactly every time):Write the two half-reactions (oxidation and reduction) separately  
Balance atoms other than H and O first  
Balance oxygen by adding H₂O  
Balance hydrogen by adding H⁺ (in acidic medium)  
Balance charge by adding electrons (e⁻)  
Make sure electrons lost = electrons gained (multiply if necessary)  
Add the two half-reactions together and cancel what is common

Very simple example JAMB likes:MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺   (acidic medium)Step-by-step solution:Oxidation half: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻
Reduction half: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂OMultiply oxidation by 5: 5Fe²⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + 5e⁻Now join:
MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O(That is the balanced equation!)Here is a very clear step-by-step diagram for balancing in acidic medium that every beginner should copy:Example Question Pattern (JAMB style):  Question 1: "In the balanced equation for the reaction between KMnO₄ and Fe²⁺ in acid, the coefficient of Fe²⁺ is: A) 1 B) 3 C) 5 D) 8" (Answer: C)  
Question 2: "When balancing redox equations in acidic medium, we add: A) OH⁻ B) H⁺ C) H₂O only D) e⁻ only" (Answer: B)

Common Mistakes Beginners Make:  Forgetting to multiply so that electrons are equal  
Adding OH⁻ instead of H⁺ in acidic medium  
Not balancing charge last

Practice this method with 5–10 simple reactions every day – soon it will become very easy!
You can do it – even if you are a complete beginner today, by the time you finish secondary school you will be balancing redox equations very fast.Keep revising OIL RIG or LEO says GER every day. Good luck in your JAMB! You will succeed!
