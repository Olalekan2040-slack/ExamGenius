TOPIC: Rates of Chemical ReactionIntroductionRates of chemical reaction means how fast a chemical reaction happens – is it very quick (like explosion) or very slow (like rusting of iron)?  For a complete beginner: Think of it like cooking. Sometimes food cooks fast (high heat), sometimes slow (low heat). In chemistry, we learn what makes reactions go faster or slower.This topic is very important in JAMB – usually 3–5 questions every year. They ask you to:Explain why some reactions are fast or slow  
Name factors that change reaction speed  
Understand collision theory (the main idea why things react)  
Look at graphs and say what they mean

We will explain everything in very simple English with easy examples and clear pictures. A reaction happens when reactant particles collide with enough energy and in the right way. Anything that makes more collisions, stronger collisions, or easier collisions will make the reaction faster.The main factors JAMB always asks about:Concentration  More particles in the same space = more collisions = faster reaction  
Example: Strong acid reacts faster than dilute acid with marble

Temperature  Higher temperature = particles move faster = more frequent and harder collisions = faster reaction  
Rule: For most reactions, 10°C rise doubles the rate!

Catalyst  A catalyst speeds up reaction without being used up  
It lowers the activation energy (the minimum energy needed for collision to work)  
Example: Manganese dioxide speeds up decomposition of hydrogen peroxide

Other factors (sometimes asked):  Surface area (powder reacts faster than lump)  
Pressure (for gases – higher pressure = higher concentration)

Key Points:Concentration ↑ → rate ↑  
Temperature ↑ → rate ↑ (very strong effect)  
Catalyst → rate ↑ (no change in reactants)  
Activation energy = the "hill" particles must climb to react

Here are very clear energy diagrams showing how temperature and catalyst affect the reaction:This diagram shows the energy profile with activation energy – catalyst lowers the hill.This one compares uncatalyzed (high activation energy) vs catalyzed (lower activation energy).Example Question Pattern (JAMB style):  Question 1: "Increasing the concentration of reactants usually: A) Decreases the rate B) Increases the rate C) Has no effect D) Stops the reaction" (Answer: B)  
Question 2: "A catalyst works by: A) Increasing temperature B) Lowering activation energy C) Changing products D) Being used up" (Answer: B)  
Question 3: "Which factor has the biggest effect on reaction rate? A) Concentration B) Temperature C) Surface area D) Light" (Answer: B – temperature is strongest)

Common Mistakes Beginners Make:  Thinking catalyst is used up (no – it remains the same)  
Saying higher concentration makes particles move faster (no – it only makes more collisions)  
Forgetting temperature affects collision frequency and energy

SUBTOPIC: Collision TheoryObjective 2: Explain collision theoryExplanation (super simple):
Collision theory says: For a reaction to happen, reactant particles must:  Collide with each other  
Collide with enough energy (more than activation energy)  
Collide in the right direction (orientation)

Only a few collisions are successful – that's why some reactions are slow.How factors affect it (easy summary):Higher concentration → more particles → more collisions  
Higher temperature → faster movement → more collisions + more energetic collisions  
Catalyst → provides new path with lower activation energy → more successful collisions

Visual Type: diagram
Visual Items:  Diagram showing successful vs unsuccessful collisions  
Picture of particles with high energy overcoming the "activation energy barrier"

Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "According to collision theory, reactions are faster at higher temperature because particles: A) Become bigger B) Collide more often and with more energy C) Change colour D) Disappear" (Answer: B)  
Question 2: "Not all collisions lead to reaction because: A) Particles are too small B) They don't have enough energy or wrong direction C) There is no catalyst D) Reaction is endothermic" (Answer: B)

Common Mistakes:  Thinking every collision causes reaction (only some do)  
Forgetting orientation matters (particles must hit correctly)

Rate means how fast concentration changes. We show it on graphs.Common graphs:Concentration vs time – starts steep (fast), then flattens (slows down)  Rate at any point = slope (steep = fast rate)

Rate vs concentration – if straight line through origin = first order (rate doubles when concentration doubles)

Key Points:Initial rate = steepest part at beginning  
Average rate = total change / total time  
Graphs help us see order of reaction (how rate depends on concentration)

Visual Type: graph
Visual Items:  Concentration-time curve  
Rate vs concentration straight line for first order
