TOPIC: Organic CompoundsIntroductionOrganic chemistry is the study of carbon-containing compounds (except a few like CO₂, carbonates). Most things we use daily (fuels, plastics, medicines, food, soaps) are organic compounds.  For a complete beginner: Carbon is special because it can join with itself and other elements to form long chains and rings. This is why there are millions of organic compounds!This is one of the biggest and most important topics in JAMB Chemistry – usually 6–8 questions every year. They ask you to:Classify compounds (alkane, alkene, alcohol, etc.)  
Give correct IUPAC names  
Identify isomers  
Write equations for reactions (combustion, addition, substitution, esterification, polymerization)  
Recognize functional groups

SUBTOPIC: Nomenclature and IsomerismObjective 2: Give IUPAC names and understand isomerismExplanation (step-by-step for naming – very important!):
IUPAC naming rules for beginners:Find the longest carbon chain – this gives the base name (meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, etc.)  
Number the chain so that the functional group or double/triple bond gets the lowest number  
Name branches (side chains) as prefixes (methyl-, ethyl-) with their position  
For functional groups: alcohols -ol, acids -oic acid, alkenes -ene, etc.

Examples:CH₃–CH₂–CH₃ → propane  
CH₃–CH=CH₂ → propene (double bond at carbon 1)  
CH₃–CH₂–OH → ethanol  
CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–OH → butan-1-ol (not butan-4-ol)

Isomerism = same molecular formula but different arrangement
Two main types in JAMB:Structural isomers – different connectivity (e.g., butane and 2-methylpropane)  
Geometric (cis-trans) isomers – in alkenes (only when each carbon in double bond has two different groups)

Question 2: "Compounds with the same formula but different arrangement are called: A) Allotropes B) Isotopes C) Isomers D) Polymers" (Answer: C)

Common Mistakes:  Numbering the chain from the wrong end (always give lowest number to functional group)  
Forgetting to name branches with position

SUBTOPIC: Important Organic Compounds and Their ReactionsObjective 3: Write reaction mechanisms and equationsExplanation (simple reactions you must know):Alkanes  Mostly unreactive (substitution with halogens in light)  
Combustion: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ + (3n+1)/2 O₂ → nCO₂ + (n+1)H₂O

Alkenes  Very reactive because of double bond  
Addition reactions (add across double bond):  Hydrogenation: C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ (Ni catalyst)  
Halogenation: C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (brown bromine decolourises)  
Hydration: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (H₂SO₄ catalyst)

Alkynes  Similar addition reactions (can add twice)

Alcohols  Oxidation (with KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇): primary → aldehyde → carboxylic acid  
Esterification: Alcohol + carboxylic acid → ester + water (H₂SO₄ catalyst)
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

Carboxylic acids  React like acids: with bases, carbonates (effervescence of CO₂)  
Esterification (above)

Polymerization  Alkenes form addition polymers (e.g., ethene → polyethene/plastic)  
Condensation polymers (e.g., nylon from diamine and dicarboxylic acid)
