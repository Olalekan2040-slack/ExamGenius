TOPIC: Separation of Mixtures and Purification of Chemical Substances
Introduction

This topic explains how substances are classified and how mixtures are separated into their components. JAMB frequently tests this topic with objective questions on purity, types of substances, and correct separation methods. Questions are usually straightforward and based on understanding basic principles.

SUBTOPIC: Pure and Impure Substances
Objective 1: Distinguish between pure and impure substances

Explanation:
A pure substance contains only one type of particle and has a fixed composition. An impure substance contains two or more substances mixed together. Pure substances have uniform properties throughout, while impure substances do not. In JAMB questions, purity is tested using composition and physical properties.

Key Points:

Pure substances contain one type of substance

Impure substances contain more than one substance

Pure substances have uniform properties

Visual Items:

Identical particles only (pure substance)

Different particles mixed together (impure substance)

Example Objective Question:
Which of the following is a pure substance?
A. Air
B. Salt solution
C. Distilled water
D. Soil

Common Mistakes:

Assuming all clear substances are pure

Thinking mixtures are always impure liquids

Objective 2: Use boiling and melting points as criteria for purity

Explanation:
A pure substance melts or boils at a fixed temperature, while an impure substance melts or boils over a range of temperatures. JAMB often presents temperature data and asks which sample is pure.

Key Points:

Sharp melting/boiling point indicates purity

Impurities affect melting and boiling points

Temperature range suggests impurity

Visual Type: graph-comparison
Visual Items:

Sharp melting point curve

Broad melting range curve

Example Objective Question:
Which of the following observations best indicates that a substance is pure?
A. It melts over a wide temperature range
B. It boils at different temperatures
C. It melts at a fixed temperature
D. It dissolves in water

Common Mistakes:

Thinking higher boiling point means purity

Ignoring the word “fixed” in questions

SUBTOPIC: Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures
Objective 3: Distinguish between elements, compounds, and mixtures

Explanation:
An element consists of only one type of atom and cannot be broken down chemically. A compound contains two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio. A mixture contains substances physically combined and can be separated by physical methods.

Key Points:

Elements cannot be broken down chemically

Compounds have fixed composition

Mixtures can be separated physically

Visual Type: icon-grid
Visual Items:

Single atom type (element)

Bonded different atoms (compound)

Unbonded mixed particles (mixture)

Example Objective Question:
Which of the following is a compound?
A. Oxygen
B. Brass
C. Water
D. Air

Common Mistakes:

Thinking alloys are compounds

Confusing compounds with mixtures

Objective 4: Differentiate between chemical and physical changes

Explanation:
A physical change does not produce a new substance and is usually reversible. A chemical change produces a new substance and is usually irreversible. JAMB questions focus on identifying the nature of changes in common processes.

Key Points:

Physical change forms no new substance

Chemical change forms a new substance

Chemical changes are often irreversible

Confusing phase change with chemical change

SUBTOPIC: Separation Processes
Objective 5: Identify properties of mixture components used in separation

Explanation:
Separation methods depend on differences in physical properties such as boiling point, solubility, magnetism, and particle size. JAMB often tests which property makes a separation method suitable.

Key Points:

Boiling point difference → distillation

Solubility → crystallization

Magnetism → magnetic separation

Visual Type: formula-cards
Visual Items:

Property-to-method mapping

Common mixtures and properties

Example Objective Question:
Which property is used to separate iron filings from sand?
A. Solubility
B. Boiling point
C. Magnetism
D. Density

Common Mistakes:

Selecting the method instead of the property

Ignoring key descriptive words

Objective 6: Specify principles involved in each separation method

Explanation:
Each separation method works based on a specific principle. Evaporation depends on volatility, distillation on boiling point differences, filtration on particle size, and chromatography on different rates of movement of substances.

Key Points:

Evaporation removes solvent

Distillation separates liquids by boiling points

Filtration separates insoluble solids

Chromatography separates based on movement
