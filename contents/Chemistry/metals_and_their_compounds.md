TOPIC: Metals and Their CompoundsIntroductionMetals are elements that are usually shiny, good conductors of heat and electricity, malleable (can be hammered into sheets), and ductile (can be drawn into wires).
Most metals are solid at room temperature (except mercury which is liquid).  For a complete beginner: Think of metals as the "strong, shiny" elements we use every day – iron in cars, aluminium in cooking pots, copper in electric wires, gold in jewellery.This topic is very important in JAMB – usually 4–6 questions every year. They ask you to:List general properties of metals  
Explain the reactivity series (order of how reactive metals are)  
Describe how metals are extracted from their ores  
Talk about special groups: alkali metals (group 1), alkaline earth metals (group 2), transition metals  
Give uses of important metals

Metals have similar physical and chemical properties because of their structure – they have free electrons that move easily.Physical Properties (what you can see/touch):Shiny (lustrous) when polished  
Good conductors of heat and electricity  
Malleable (can be hammered into thin sheets)  
Ductile (can be drawn into thin wires)  
Usually high density (heavy)  
High melting and boiling points (except alkali metals)  
Sonorous (make ringing sound when hit)

Chemical Properties:Lose electrons easily to form positive ions (cations)  
Form basic oxides (some amphoteric like Al, Zn)  
React with oxygen, water, acids (depending on reactivity)

Common Mistakes Beginners Make:  Saying metals are brittle (no – non-metals are brittle)  
Thinking all metals react violently with water (only alkali metals do strongly)

SUBTOPIC: Reactivity Series and ExtractionObjective 2: Explain reactivity series and extractionExplanation (easy to remember):
The reactivity series is a list of metals arranged from most reactive to least reactive.
More reactive metals displace less reactive ones from compounds.Here is the reactivity series you must learn by heart (from top to bottom – most reactive to least):K → Na → Ca → Mg → Al → C → Zn → Fe → Sn → Pb → H → Cu → Ag → AuImportant points:Metals above hydrogen displace H₂ from acids  
Metals above carbon can be extracted by reduction with carbon (coke)  
Metals below carbon (like Cu, Ag, Au) are found native or easily extracted

Extraction of Metals:Very reactive (K, Na, Ca): Electrolysis of molten compound (expensive)  
Moderately reactive (Al, Mg): Electrolysis of molten ore  
Less reactive (Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb): Reduction with carbon in furnace  
Least reactive (Cu, Ag, Au): Found free or roasted then reduced

Forgetting potassium is more reactive than sodium

SUBTOPIC: Alkali, Alkaline Earth, and Transition MetalsObjective 3: Describe properties of special groups of metalsExplanation (simple):  1. Alkali Metals (Group 1: Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs)  Very soft (can cut with knife)  
Low density (float on water)  
Very reactive – react violently with water to give hydroxide + H₂  
Stored under oil/kerosene to prevent reaction with air/moisture

2. Alkaline Earth Metals (Group 2: Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba)  Harder than alkali metals  
Less reactive than group 1  
React with water (but slower) and acids  
Example: Calcium in limestone (CaCO₃), magnesium in chlorophyll

3. Transition Metals (d-block: Fe, Cu, Zn, Cr, Ni, etc.)  Have variable oxidation states (e.g., Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺)  
Form coloured compounds (blue CuSO₄, green FeSO₄)  
Good catalysts (Fe in Haber process, Ni in hydrogenation)  
High melting points and densities

Common Mistakes:  Thinking transition metals are soft (no – they are hard)
