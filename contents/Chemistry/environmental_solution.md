TOPIC: Environmental PollutionIntroductionEnvironmental pollution refers to the contamination of the natural environment (air, water, soil) by harmful substances or energy, making it unsafe for living things. It is caused mainly by human activities like industries, transportation, agriculture, and poor waste management. This topic is very important in JAMB because it connects chemistry to real-life problems in Nigeria and the world, such as oil spillage in the Niger Delta, air pollution in cities like Lagos, and plastic waste everywhere. Each type has different sources (causes) and brings different bad effects on human health and the environment. Air pollution comes mostly from burning fuels and industries. Water pollution comes from oil spills, industrial waste, sewage, and farming chemicals. Soil pollution comes from waste dumps, pesticides, and oil leaks.Key Points:Air Pollution
Sources: Vehicle exhaust, factory smoke, bush burning, gas flaring
Pollutants: Carbon monoxide (CO), sulphur dioxide (SO₂), nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), particulate matter (dust/smoke), ozone at ground level
Effects on health: Breathing problems, asthma, lung cancer, eye irritation
Effects on environment: Acid rain, global warming, damage to plants
Water Pollution
Sources: Oil spillage, industrial waste discharge, sewage, fertilizer/pesticide runoff, plastic dumping
Pollutants: Oil, heavy metals (lead, mercury), detergents, bacteria, plastics
Effects on health: Cholera, diarrhoea, skin diseases, cancer from heavy metals
Effects on environment: Death of fish and aquatic life, eutrophication (too much algae growth), destruction of ecosystems
Soil/Land Pollution
Sources: Improper waste disposal, mining, oil spills, excessive use of pesticides and fertilizers
Pollutants: Heavy metals, plastics, chemicals, oil
Effects on health: Contaminated crops cause poisoning, cancer, birth defects
Effects on environment: Loss of soil fertility, desertification, harm to soil organisms

Visual Type: diagram-comparison
Visual Items:  Comparison table or infographic showing sources → pollutants → effects for air, water, and soil

Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "Which of the following is a major source of air pollution in Nigeria? A) Oil spillage B) Gas flaring C) Sewage discharge D) Plastic dumping" (Answer: B)  
Question 2: "The disease commonly caused by drinking polluted water is: A) Asthma B) Cholera C) Skin rash D) Lung cancer" (Answer: B)  
Question 3: "Acid rain is mainly caused by: A) Carbon monoxide B) Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides C) Oil D) Plastics" (Answer: B)  
Question 4: "Eutrophication in rivers is caused by excess: A) Oil B) Nutrients from fertilizers C) Heavy metals D) Smoke" (Answer: B)

Common Mistakes:  Thinking only factories cause pollution (vehicles, homes, farms also contribute a lot).  
Confusing air pollution effects (breathing) with water pollution effects (stomach diseases).  
Saying oil spillage only affects water (it also pollutes soil badly).

Control measures include changing how we do things (prevention), cleaning up existing pollution, and making laws. Many of these methods are already being used in Nigeria but need more effort from government, companies, and individuals.Key Points:General Control Measures  Use cleaner fuels (LPG instead of firewood/coal)  
Plant trees (afforestation) to absorb CO₂ and reduce dust  
Proper waste management (recycling, proper dumpsites)  
Education and awareness campaigns

Air Pollution Control  Install catalytic converters in vehicles  
Use low-sulphur fuels  
Enforce emission standards for industries  
Stop gas flaring in oil fields

Water Pollution Control  Treat sewage before discharge  
Prevent oil spills with better pipelines and quick response  
Ban dumping of waste into rivers  
Use biodegradable detergents

Soil Pollution Control  Use organic farming (less chemical pesticides)  
Proper disposal of industrial and household waste  
Bioremediation (using microbes to clean oil spills)  
Recycling plastics and metals

Example Question Pattern:  Question 1: "Which method is most effective in reducing air pollution from vehicles? A) Planting trees B) Using catalytic converters C) Dumping waste D) Burning tyres" (Answer: B)  
Question 2: "To control water pollution from industries, the best action is: A) Discharge waste directly B) Treat effluent before release C) Increase production D) Ignore laws" (Answer: B)  
Question 3: "Gas flaring can be controlled by: A) Burning more gas B) Reinjecting gas or converting to useful products C) Dumping in rivers D) Using more diesel" (Answer: B)  
Question 4: "The best way to reduce soil pollution from plastics is: A) Burning them B) Recycling and proper disposal C) Throwing in rivers D) Burying everywhere" (Answer: B)

Common Mistakes:  Thinking only government can solve pollution (individuals can help by not littering).  
Suggesting burning waste as a solution (it causes more air pollution).  
Forgetting that prevention is better than cure.
